Equal CER, Unequal Damage: Severity-Weighted Character Error Rate for OCR
Authors: Nguyen Nguyen, Yongyi Zang
Venue: ACL
First: 2026-10-14T16:44:22+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T16:44:22+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 7 figures, 14 tables. Under review at ACL Rolling Review
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Character Error Rate (CER), the standard metric for OCR and vision-language models (VLMs), charges a typo as much as a wrong name. We introduce semCER, which keeps CER's alignment and denominator and charges each grapheme edit a severity from an LLM judge, with no word segmentation. Normalized CER keeps the score of a whole transcription, where it tracks readers as well as semCER or better, and semCER breaks that score down into named edits. The charges track readers' ratings edit by edit in English, Vietnamese, and Chinese and put the edits readers rate most severe near the top of a sorted list. On pairs drawn where CER and semCER disagreed, readers side with semCER more often than with normalized CER in English and Chinese, and equally often in Vietnamese. Across 16 languages, the judge charges a wrong diacritic or tone mark almost nothing in Spanish but nearly a wrong letter's severity in Telugu. Where we can count real words, a general VLM's wrong letter forms one far above chance while a classical pipeline's stays near it. We release the 61 million judged edits, the aggregated reader ratings, and the distilled scorer at https://github.com/nguyenx2t/semcer.

Reward Design for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Derek Lilienthal, Manisha Mukherjee, Sameera Horawalavithana
First: 2026-04-15T15:36:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T15:56:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Physical reasoning over images requires integrating perception, domain knowledge, and multi-step symbolic inference, yet Vision-Language Models (VLMs) fall far short of human performance on physics benchmarks. Reinforcement learning can improve reasoning via reward signals, but it remains unclear whether these signals must come from outside the model or can be derived from the model itself. We compare external rewards, which rely on an outside verifier (rule-based format and answer-correctness checks, or an LLM-judge rubric), with an internal attention-based reward computed from the model's own attention over image regions, requiring no labels or judge. We find that external rewards drive most of the accuracy gains, while preserving more coherent reasoning when rewarded for reasoning tokens. The internal attention-based reward, provides an annotation-free grounding signal that improves spatial-relation reasoning and is complementary to external rewards: adding it to format and accuracy rewards, at no additional annotation cost, improves average accuracy on Molmo~2 while generalizing to out-of-distribution tasks. Our results suggest that internal attention rewards can supplement external supervision and lead to improved physical reasoning.

Abstract
With the rapid development of vision-language models (VLMs), Web-GUI agents have achieved promising performance in interacting with real-world interfaces through screenshot-based grounding. However, high-resolution screenshots and multi-turn histories introduce excessive visual tokens, causing substantial inference overhead. Existing token pruning methods, mainly designed for natural images, often select tokens independently based on importance scores, which can fragment the local visual structures required for recognizing small GUI elements such as buttons, icons, and hyperlinks. In this work, we propose STP-GUI (Spatially-aware Token Pruning for GUI agents), a query-guided token pruning framework that preserves fine-grained instruction-conditioned token scoring while performing spatially coherent window-level selection under aggressive token reduction. A lightweight selector is optimized through policy learning while the VLM backbone remains frozen. Experiments on Online-Mind2Web show that \papername achieves a favorable efficiency-performance tradeoff under low token budgets. At 21% token retention, it preserves 77.7% of full-token performance while reducing inference latency by 31% and FLOPs by 57%. Results on Android in the Wild further suggest that the method transfers to mobile GUI environments.

Reflect After Failure: Brain-Inspired Self-Evolving Agents for Embodied Spatial Intelligence
Authors: Xin Li, Xiaohuan Gu, Yifan Wang, Shaobin Ling, Tianli Shi, Jingao Xu, Zhimeng Yin, Dapeng Wu, Yun Wang
First: 2026-10-14T14:59:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:59:04+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have made substantial progress in spatial reasoning, and self-evolving agents further extend these capabilities to embodied environments by learning from interactions. However, current self-evolution remains particularly challenging in embodied spatial tasks, complex and multi-step decisions from partial visual evidence making it difficult to determine why an execution fails. The failure may stem from misperception, insufficient exploration, or incorrect planning, yet execution feedback alone rarely reveals which component is responsible. As a result, simply accumulating interaction experience can reinforce ineffective strategies. To address this, we propose HiMeco, a brain-inspired self-evolving agent for embodied spatial intelligence, inspired by metacognitive monitoring and hippocampal memory consolidation. Through Hippocampal-Metacognitive Reflection, HiMeco diagnoses failures in perception-exploration-planning (PEP), then compares execution attempts to derive reusable strategies and guidance for avoiding recurring errors. It also abstracts interaction programs into parameterized skills, using evidence contracts and execution-based validation to determine their retention and reuse. These strategies and skills guide subsequent interactions, with memory updated only between rounds and model parameters kept frozen. HiMeco achieves state-of-the-art performance on ESI-Bench, and it also improves accuracy on EmbSpatial-Bench by 3.94 percentage points without evolution.

Topology-Aware Representation Alignment for Semi-Supervised Vision-Language Learning
Authors: Junwon You, Mihyun Jang, Sangwoo Mo, Jae-Hun Jung
First: 2026-04-29T07:30:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T14:28:53+00:00
Comments: 38 pages, 13 figures, 32 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models have shown strong performance, but they often generalize poorly to specialized domains. While semi-supervised vision-language learning mitigates this limitation by leveraging a small set of labeled image-text pairs together with abundant unlabeled images, existing methods remain fundamentally pairwise and fail to model the global structure of multimodal representation manifolds. Most existing topology-based alignment methods rely on persistence diagram matching, which neither guarantees geometric alignment nor uses the image-text pairing information central to vision-language learning. We propose Topology-Aware Representation Alignment for Semi-Supervised Vision-Language Learning (ToMA), a framework that uses persistent homology to decompose pairwise relations into $H_0$-death and $H_1$-birth relations and aligns them across modalities through available cross-modal correspondences. This lightweight decomposition captures both connectivity and cycle-closing structure without constructing 2-simplices. Experiments show clear improvements on remote sensing, while fashion results reveal strong gains in classification and particularly strong retrieval improvements for difficult queries. Additional analysis shows that ToMA provides more consistent gains than alternative topology-based objectives and that lightweight $H_1$-birth edges provide useful higher-order structural signals.

Seeing at the Edge: Energy Characterization of Vision-Language Model Inference on Edge Platforms
Authors: Junlin Liao, Harshit Kondle, Di Liu, Zonghua Gu
First: 2026-10-14T12:55:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T12:55:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are moving onto edge devices, but little is known about how much energy they draw on single-board computers with NPU or GPU accelerators, or where that energy goes. We measure total-system energy, latency, and thermal behavior of Qwen2-VL-2B-Instruct and its text-only sibling on a Raspberry Pi 5 with a Hailo-10H NPU and on a Jetson Orin Nano Super, on both CPU and accelerator, and separate the fixed energy of each request from the energy that grows with output length. Because the accelerators use different quantization and serving software, we compare deployment stacks rather than silicon. Three findings stand out. First, NPU offloading makes the Pi a much more efficient VLM platform: it cuts energy per generated token by about two-thirds, leaves the host CPU largely free, and avoids the thermal throttling that the CPU backend reaches in sustained use. Second, an image adds both fixed and per-token energy, but at typical response lengths generating the answer costs far more than processing the image. Third, the Jetson GPU favors short responses through its lower fixed cost per request, whereas the Pi NPU favors longer ones through its lower cost per output token; their energy curves cross near 40 output tokens.

GeoCloak: An Agentic Framework for Semantics-Preserving Geolocation Privacy via Peripheral Clue Manipulation
Authors: Yixin Wu, Mingjie Li, Yun Shen, Michael Backes, Yang Zhang
First: 2026-10-14T11:56:53+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T11:56:53+00:00
Comments: 27 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
The rapid advancement of large vision language models (LVLMs) has made geolocation inference from images highly accurate and accessible. This poses serious privacy risks: a passive adversary with black-box LVLM access can infer a user's location from a single photo with no metadata. Existing protection methods rely almost exclusively on adversarial perturbation, which introduces perceptible visual artifacts and utility degradation. We identify the visual-geolocation asymmetry: geolocation-decisive clues, such as street signs, tend to be visually peripheral but dominate location inference. Exploiting this asymmetry, we propose GeoCloak, an agentic framework for semantics-preserving geolocation privacy protection. It acts as a clue-aware planner that analyzes each image to identify geolocation-relevant clues, dispatches targeted semantic edits to directly suppress peripheral clues, and autonomously configures adversarial perturbation to further suppress residual geolocation features, all under iterative utility-aware feedback. We evaluate GeoCloak on four diverse datasets spanning 108 countries and 576 cities against five LVLMs and three inference methods. GeoCloak reduces overall city-level accuracy from 41.2% to 9.9%, approximately half of the best-performing baseline, while achieving substantially superior utility preservation both qualitatively and quantitatively. We hope this work takes a meaningful step toward practical, semantics-preserving geolocation privacy protection, giving users greater control over the location information inadvertently embedded in their shared images.

Abstract
Adapting a single vision-language model to medical image parsing requires training choices that reflect the demands of each task. We study task-specific adaptation with Qwen3.5-9B for FLARE 2026 Task 3, covering six task families and seven imaging modalities. Our system couples a shared, frozen 4-bit backbone with LoRA adapters selected from the input question. The benefits vary across tasks, supporting the use of task-specific adapter configurations and image budgets within a shared medical vision-language model. Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-14T09:50:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T09:50:59+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models suffer significant accuracy drops under common corruptions. While test-time adaptation (TTA) successfully mitigates these shifts in CLIP-like contrastive models, its application to generative multimodal large language models (MLLMs) remains underexplored and uniquely challenging due to their sequential token generation. In this work, we demonstrate that representation-based TTA fails to recover performance in MLLMs. Our experiments reveal that even when violating standard TTA assumptions by leveraging target data offline, sample-blind feature alignment recovers only marginal accuracy. We attribute this failure to the nature of corruption shifts: unlike CLIP-like architectures, which benefit from a shared domain-shift direction within a single pooled classification embedding, MLLM visual token sequence shifts are nearly orthogonal and span the full embedding space.These findings bound what any sample-blind, position-shared correction at a single encoder site can achieve, establishing that representation-space interventions alone might be insufficient to ensure corruption robustness in MLLMs.

DarKnight: Retrieval-Guided Agentic Framework for Breaking Dark Web CAPTCHA Gates
Authors: Binghui Wu, Yeo Zi Yi, Darren Tan, Manit Baser, Levente Csikor, Dinil Mon Divakaran

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Cyber Threat Intelligence (CTI) investigators who monitor dark web marketplaces for leaked credentials and breach listings must pass the diverse CAPTCHAs that operators deploy to block automated collection. Existing solvers either need unscalable per-site fine-tuning or rely on commercial Vision-Language Model (VLM) APIs, which surrender operational sovereignty: provider policies, Tor-exit blocking, government regulation, or model updates outside the investigator's control can halt or alter the solver overnight, and per-call costs recur with every session and retry. We present DarKnight, a self-improving agentic framework designed to run on a single workstation GPU inside an investigator's Tor crawler and solve CAPTCHAs with small, locally hosted open-weight VLMs that no provider decision can withdraw, without per-market training or third-party services. For each CAPTCHA, DarKnight retrieves successful and failed past attempts on similar images (dual-signal RAG), composes an image-specific preprocessing pipeline, and learns from the site's pass/fail verdict. In a case study on four real-world dark web marketplaces, agentic tool selection outperformed parameter scaling. A deployable open-weight VLM with DarKnight reached 83.2\% on the most heavily defended market, 37.2 percentage points above its base model, outperforming even the frontier Claude Opus~5 (44%). DarKnight recovered 48.2% of baseline failures at a 7.6% degeneration cost, indicating that selective tool use can avoid much of the degradation caused by uniform preprocessing.

PhyMetric: Diagnosing Physical Plausibility in Text-to-Video Generation via Scene-Level QA
Authors: Siwei Meng, Xuerou Cai, Rui Hu, Ping Liu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-14T09:00:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T09:00:58+00:00
Comments: This work is accepted by NeurIPS 2026 Evaluations and Datasets Track
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Text-to-video (T2V) generation models can produce visually realistic videos that nevertheless violate the physical consequences implied by the prompt and scene. Existing benchmarks largely rely on holistic plausibility scores or broad quality dimensions, leaving it unclear which physical behavior is being tested and why a model fails. We introduce PhyMetric, a scene-level diagnostic benchmark that recasts physical plausibility evaluation as verifiable, scene-grounded question answering. PhyMetric contains 1{,}115 scenes spanning 50 physical categories across six domains, paired with 8{,}920 answer-balanced binary questions over 12 evaluation dimensions. Each scene is associated with eight targeted yes/no questions and a structured physical prior specifying the governing law, expected temporal sequence, observable signatures, and common failure modes. At evaluation time, a vision-language model (VLM) judge answers each question from question-aware sampled frames conditioned on the prior, and correctness is hierarchically aggregated into scene, dimension, physical category, and model scores. Experiments on four open-source T2V models and three VLM judges show that scene-specific binary QA is a critical component of the protocol: when each targeted question is replaced by a single generic plausibility query, evaluator accuracy drops to the chance level. Structured physical priors offer a complementary signal that improves human alignment of the resulting scores, with effects that vary across VLM judges of different capacity. These findings establish PhyMetric as a benchmark that turns physical plausibility evaluation from opaque holistic scoring into interpretable, scene-level inspection. Our project page is available at: https://mengsiwei.github.io/PhyMetric/.

Beyond Size: How Gradients Shape Pruning Decisions in Large Language Models
Authors: Rocktim Jyoti Das, Mukul Ranjan, Mingjie Sun, Liqun Ma, Zhiqiang Shen
First: 2023-11-08T18:59:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T08:28:09+00:00
Comments: Code and models at https://github.com/VILA-Lab/GBLM-Pruner
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large Language Models (LLMs) with billions of parameters are prime targets for network pruning, which removes some model weights without hurting performance. Prior approaches such as magnitude pruning, SparseGPT, and Wanda either concentrated solely on weights or integrated weights with activations for sparsity. However, they overlooked the informative gradients derived from pretrained LLMs. In this paper, we present a novel sparsity-centric pruning method for pretrained LLMs, termed Gradient-based Language Model Pruner (GBLM-Pruner). GBLM-Pruner leverages the first-order term of the Taylor expansion, operating in a training-free manner by harnessing properly normalized gradients from a few calibration samples to determine the pruning metric, and substantially outperforms competitive counterparts like SparseGPT and Wanda in multiple benchmarks. Intriguingly, by incorporating gradients, unstructured pruning with our method tends to reveal some structural patterns, which mirrors the geometric interdependence inherent in the LLMs' parameter structure. Additionally, GBLM-Pruner functions without any subsequent retraining or weight updates to maintain its simplicity as other counterparts. Extensive evaluations on LLaMA-series, Qwen-Series and Deepseek models across zero-shot, STEM-Reasoning and Coding benchmarks show that GBLM-Pruner surpasses magnitude pruning and Wanda by significant margins and performs competitively against SparseGPT which involves intensive weight updates. We further extend our approach on Vision Transformer and Vision Language Models. Our code and models are available at https://github.com/VILA-Lab/GBLM-Pruner.

Abstract
Interpreting an ambiguous utterance in real-world interactions often requires context beyond a single utterance. When the utterance refers to objects in a visual scene, it is necessary to maintain and update the set of plausible referents as context accumulates, without assuming that the available information identifies a unique object. However, prior work on interactive visual grounding, which localizes objects referred to by linguistic expressions, has focused on identifying a unique object once sufficient dialogue context becomes available. To address this gap, we propose Candidate Set Visual Grounding (CSVG), a task for estimating the set of objects consistent with the given dialogue context. We further introduce CandVG, a benchmark that evaluates how the candidate set changes across dialogue turns. Our evaluation of vision-language models shows that stronger visual grounding capability does not necessarily yield better CSVG performance. In particular, models have difficulty predicting the complete candidate set, owing to challenges in candidate generation and premature commitment to a small set of candidates in early turns. Nevertheless, we find that this limitation can be partially mitigated in the evaluated large-scale models using instructions designed for CSVG.

Abstract
Gaze coordinates attention in human interaction through coupled eye, head, and eyelid motion. A humanoid robot must choose where to look while generating a suitable movement toward that target; fixed tracking rules alone do not model the temporal structure of human gaze. We separate these tasks with an asynchronous vision-language model that selects a scene referent, a reactive perception module that maintains fixation, and a Realistic Gaze Transformer (RGT) that generates target-conditioned motion. What Breaks in Multi-Image Schema Application? Factorial Oracle Interventions Reveal an Instance-Information Bottleneck

Abstract
Modern vision-language models can accept multi-image contexts, but aggregate accuracy rarely reveals where a multi-image reasoning pipeline fails. We study a controlled schema-application task in which a model must locate two target characters in a 16-image sequence, identify their visual attributes, consult an item-specific visual codebook, and compose two ordered nonce code names. We introduce a within-item 2-by-2 factorial Oracle design: Oracle-A supplies a verified textual schema, Oracle-B supplies a verified ordered target-appearance representation, and both interventions retain the original images and final question. An auxiliary retrieval diagnostic reached only 49.1% ordered-appearance accuracy even after removing code-name generation. Providing both Oracle sources yielded 100% accuracy across all models. These results localize the dominant recoverable deficit to upstream ordered instance recovery rather than primarily to downstream schema use and composition.

Masked Diffusion Vision-Language Models for Temporal Action Localization
Authors: Fengshun Wang, Zhengbo Zhang, Zhigang Tu
First: 2026-05-28T12:39:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T05:26:23+00:00

Abstract
Temporal action localization (TAL) requires recognizing the target event and localizing its start and end times precisely in untrimmed videos. Recent vision-language formulations improve semantic reasoning and support language-conditioned outputs, but their autoregressive decoders still generate tokens from left to right, preventing later semantic evidence from revising earlier timestamp predictions. We adapt masked diffusion vision-language models (MDVLMs) to TAL so that semantic tokens and boundary tokens remain editable throughout iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, allowing temporal boundaries and semantic content to be refined jointly. Direct adaptation, however, creates two TAL-specific mismatches: standard masked diffusion training corrupts all positions uniformly at random, but the time tokens are more reliable when enough semantic context is available; and token-level cross-entropy does not reflect temporal IoU. To address these mismatches, we introduce a Planned Training Objective that uses boundary-aware masking and step-weighted reconstruction to rehearse the late recovery of time tokens, together with a Step-Level IoU Reward that provides overlap-aware supervision during denoising. A standard sequence-level cross-entropy term provides the base reconstruction signal. Experiments on ActivityNet-RTL, ActivityNet-1.3, and THUMOS-14 show that MDVLM-TAL improves both temporal reasoning and boundary localization over autoregressive vision-language baselines, with especially strong gains under stricter temporal IoU criteria. The source code is available at https://github.com/ycwfs/MDVLM-TAL .

Enabling Validation for Robust Few-Shot Recognition
Authors: Hanxin Wang, Tian Liu, Shu Kong
First: 2025-06-05T07:37:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-14T04:19:25+00:00
Comments: Project website: https://hannawang09.github.io/projects/vest/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
A key challenge in Few-Shot Recognition (FSR) is the scarcity of labeled task-specific data for training. Contemporary methods mitigate this challenge by exploiting pretrained Vision-Language Models (VLMs) for their learned transferable features. Some recent ones augment the scarce data by retrieving relevant yet out-of-distribution (OOD) examples from external data sources. However, they largely overlook an equally fundamental challenge: the absence of validation data for model selection and hyperparameter tuning. As a result, the adapted VLMs overfit the few-shot training data and generalize poorly to OOD test data. We show that retrieved data provide a natural source of validation, but their direct use creates a fundamental paradox. Since the retrieved samples are inherently OOD w.r.t the few-shot ID training data, adapting a VLM to the ID data constantly degrades its performance on the retrieved data. Consequently, conventional validation relying on the accuracy of retrieved data favors no adaptation and prevents improved generalization. To resolve this dilemma, we formulate validation as a multi-objective optimization (MOO) problem between performance gains on the few-shot ID data and the performance preservation on the retrieved data. We introduce a simple yet effective validation strategy by choosing an operating point on the Pareto front of the MOO problem. This strategy enables hyperparameter tuning, which had been unrealistically done by using test data, thereby mitigating overfitting and improving generalization. We integrate it into a stage-wise finetuning pipeline, termed Validation-Enabled Stage-wise Tuning (VEST).
