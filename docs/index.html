MiD-VLM: Detecting and Grounding Global Semantic Misinformation in Image-Caption Pairs
Authors: Tarunya Prasad, Bharatesh Chakravarthi
First: 2026-10-23T17:55:02+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:55:02+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Semantic misinformation can arise when an authentic image is paired with a caption that changes or distorts its narrative. Because the image itself may contain no visible signs of manipulation, methods designed to detect pixel-level artifacts can miss these misleading image-caption pairs. We study this problem using DGM4+, a dataset designed to evaluate the detection of global scene and semantic inconsistencies. We propose a multimodal model that combines image-text representations from SigLIP with visual scene features from DINOv2 through a projection MLP, and adapts Qwen2-VL-7B using LoRA. The model jointly predicts whether an image-caption pair is authentic, identifies the manipulation type, and grounds the misleading caption tokens. On the DGM4+ validation split, the model achieves an F1 score of $0.9679$ for binary authenticity classification. These results suggest that parameter-efficient adaptation of vision-language models is promising for detecting semantic inconsistencies, while also motivating further work on precise token-level grounding.

Beyond Discrete Tokens: Preserving Numerical Continuity in Medical Vision-Language Models
Authors: Dianye Huang, Jiamei Guo, Yue Zhou, Mingde Li, Yi Xiong, Wei Wang, Nassir Navab, Zhongliang Jiang
First: 2026-10-23T17:40:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:40:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Objective: To develop a unified vision-language model for prompt-conditioned quantitative ultrasound measurement that addresses the mismatch between continuous measurement targets and discrete token-based numerical prediction. Methods: This article presents HAD-VLM, a hybrid autoregressive-diffusion framework that combines autoregressive text generation with continuous measurement prediction within a shared backbone. Visual prompt tuning adapts the vision encoder to ultrasound images. A diffusion process predicts a five-component representation comprising caliper endpoint coordinates and measurement length. A post-processing strategy combining the proposed coordinate-length consistency mechanism with ensemble prediction is introduced to refine measurement estimates. We evaluate four echocardiographic measurement tasks on EchoNet-LVH, with additional assessments of medical question answering and X-ray vertebral measurement. Results: HAD-VLM achieves mean absolute errors of 1.36, 2.77, 3.05, and 1.16 mm for IVSd, LVIDd, LVIDs, and LVPWd, respectively, reducing errors by 21.4-37.6\% relative to the Vicuna-based autoregressive baseline. It also outperforms fine-tuned Qwen3-VL and direct MLP regression across all four tasks. Medical question answering performance is preserved on the evaluated dataset. For X-ray vertebral measurement, HAD-VLM reduces mean absolute error from 11.4 to 6.7 pixels. Conclusion: Integrating autoregressive language generation with diffusion-based continuous prediction improves quantitative medical image measurement while retaining prompt-conditioned task flexibility. Significance: HAD-VLM provides a unified approach to combining language interaction with anatomically grounded numerical prediction for quantitative medical image analysis.

Semantic Anchoring for Robotic Action Representations
Authors: Yuan Xu, Youheng Shi, Chengyang Li, Wentao Zhu, Yizhou Wang
Venue: CoRL 2026
First: 2026-07-15T08:45:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T17:10:14+00:00
Comments: Accepted at CoRL 2026. Project page: https://xy02-05.github.io/SemanticMN
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models inherit rich semantic representations from pretrained Vision-Language Models, yet fine-tuning on limited robot demonstrations degrades this structure and undermines generalization. A fundamental question therefore arises: what constitutes a good action representation? Inspired by the mirror neuron theory's insight that observation and execution share an intention-level encoding, we examine whether a robot's action representations preserve the semantic structure captured by pretrained encoders. Systematic probing confirms that this structure erodes during finetuning, and that its quality synchronizes with both task success and out-of-distribution generalization. We further introduce a plug-and-play method that anchors action representations to a semantic manifold while decomposing representations into a shared semantic channel and a private channel, all discarded at inference, leaving the deployed model unchanged. Validated on different VLA backbones across simulation and real-world benchmarks, our method yields up to +18.7% on real-world in-distribution tasks and +21.5% on out-of-distribution generalization.

Abstract
Echocardiography is the cornerstone of cardiovascular diagnostics, yet its global utility is constrained by a critical shortage of experienced interpreters. Current artificial intelligence (AI) solutions remain limited, typically operating as opaque "black boxes" or focusing on narrow tasks that fail to capture the holistic, guideline-driven reasoning of a clinician. Here we introduce EchoChat, a generative multimodal vision-language model that performs comprehensive echocardiographic analysis to support clinician interpretation. By emulating the cognitive workflow of a cardiologist, EchoChat integrates vast medical knowledge with a chain-of-thought reasoning mechanism to explicitly ground its conclusions in visual evidence. Trained on echocardiography-instruction pairs from 46,358 examinations, our model establishes a new state-of-the-art across diverse cohorts from six international medical centers. It supports the broadest diagnostic scope of 33 cardiac conditions and findings, including 15 newly added conditions for which it achieves an average F1 of 73.7% across available external evaluations. Furthermore, in a source-blinded echocardiographer reader study, echocardiography reports co-generated with EchoChat were rated higher in diagnostic accuracy and completeness by two senior echocardiographers, and improved diagnostic performance from 72.78% to 82.75% in terms of F1 score over routine-reader only reports. These results demonstrate that integrating clinical reasoning into multimodal AI can democratize specialist expertise and transform the standard of cardiovascular care.

Attention-space Contrastive Guidance for Efficient Hallucination Mitigation in LVLMs
Authors: Yujin Jo, Sangyoon Bae, Taesup Kim
Venue: CVPR 2026
First: 2026-01-20T08:04:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T16:39:00+00:00
Comments: Accepted at CVPR 2026 Findings. Code: https://github.com/ZoYujin/ACG
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Hallucinations in large vision--language models (LVLMs) often arise when language priors dominate over visual evidence, leading to object misidentification and visually inconsistent descriptions. We address this problem by framing hallucination mitigation as contrastive guidance that steers generation toward visually grounded and semantically faithful text. We propose Attention-space Contrastive Guidance (ACG), a training-free, single-pass method that operates directly in self-attention layers, where hallucination-inducing cross-modal biases emerge. ACG constructs both image-conditioned and approximate text-only attention paths within a single forward pass, enabling efficient guidance before errors accumulate at the output layer. Because this masking-based surrogate can introduce approximation bias, we further apply a lightweight orthogonal projection that suppresses components aligned with the text-only path, yielding a more visually grounded correction. Experiments on CHAIR and POPE show that ACG improves faithfulness over existing training-free baselines while maintaining caption quality, reducing latency by up to $2\times$ compared to multi-pass contrastive decoding methods.

From Frames to Temporal Graphs: In-Context Egocentric Action Recognition with Vision-Language Models
Authors: Bessie Dominguez-Dager, Francisco Gomez-Donoso, Miguel Cazorla, Marc Pollefeys, Daniel Barath, Zuria Bauer
First: 2026-06-13T18:00:42+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T15:47:18+00:00

Abstract
Action reasoning in egocentric video requires capturing fine-grained transitions of hand-object interactions, a task where general-purpose Vision-Language Models (VLMs) often struggle when operating directly on raw pixels. We propose to decouple visual perception from symbolic reasoning by converting videos into Temporal Action Graphs. In a multi-stage prompting pipeline, we first generate dense natural language narratives over short temporal windows as a semantic bottleneck, then formalize them into structured, open-vocabulary graph representations. On the EGTEA and Epic-Kitchens-100 datasets, the symbolic representation unlocks efficient in-context learning: few-shot graph demonstrations yield substantial accuracy gains over zero-shot frame-based inference. Even in the zero-shot setting, graph-based reasoning remains competitive with pixel-based inference despite potential pretraining contamination favoring the latter. Across 11 open-weight VLMs from 6 model families ranging from 2B to 235B parameters, our findings indicate that current VLMs are more effective as symbolic reasoners than as direct visual observers. By projecting video into the language domain, we provide a scalable, fine-tuning-free alternative to end-to-end approaches that better leverages these models' latent reasoning strengths. SCDM-HTD: Spectral-Wise Conditional Diffusion Model for Hyperspectral Target Detection
Authors: Dunbin Shen, Wenfeng Kong, Jianjun Liu, Xiaorui Ma, Hongyu Wang
First: 2026-10-23T15:08:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T15:08:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Hyperspectral target detection (HTD) aims to locate and recognize objects at a fine-grained scale, playing a pivotal role in remote sensing. Despite significant progress, current HTD methods primarily focus on feature comparison while neglecting distribution learning for background-target separation, which can limit detection robustness in practical applications. This paper addresses this issue by introducing a spectral-wise conditional diffusion model that reformulates HTD as a task of generating a detection map. Specifically, we treat background and target confidence scores as training samples and use the corresponding pixel spectra as guiding conditions to capture the distributional discrepancy between background and target through diffusion and denoising. To enhance practical applicability, we propose a lightweight conditional denoising network based on multilayer perceptron to map noisy confidence scores to their ideal counterparts and adopt a deterministic reverse non-Markov chain to skip sampling steps, thereby accelerating the generation process. Furthermore, we introduce a composite loss to improve background-target separation. This loss integrates constrained energy minimization loss to enhance target saliency and suppress the background by maximizing the target response while minimizing average output energy, alongside binary cross-entropy loss to classify background and target using pixel-wise labels. Experimental results on four benchmark datasets demonstrate the effectiveness of the proposed method, with improvements in both quantitative metrics and qualitative visual effects.

CatchUp: Depth-Aware Re-Admission of Visual Tokens for Efficient Large Vision-Language Models
Authors: Eunju Park, Uiwon Hwang
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-16T11:28:46+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T12:56:16+00:00
Comments: WACV 2027. Code: https://github.com/pej0918/CatchUp
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) repeatedly process hundreds of visual tokens across decoder layers, incurring substantial computation and memory costs. Existing visual-token reduction methods often irreversibly discard tokens despite depth-varying query relevance. Although recoverable routing allows excluded tokens to return, re-admitted tokens retain stale hidden states after bypassing intermediate decoder transformations. Thus, token recoverability does not guarantee representation readiness at the current decoder depth. Under a fixed routing trajectory at a 64-token budget, replacing stale re-admitted states with current-depth oracle representations improves average RefCOCO Acc@0.5 from 18.96 to 23.18, revealing the impact of representation staleness. We propose CatchUp, a training-free framework combining reversible visual-token routing with Local Transition Correction (LTC). CatchUp repeatedly re-evaluates query-conditioned relevance over the complete visual-token set, preserving deferred tokens for later re-admission. LTC approximates their skipped representation evolution by transferring reference-to-current hidden-state transitions from semantically and spatially related active anchors. This correction improves alignment with current-depth oracle representations without recomputing the skipped decoder layers. Across three referring-expression grounding datasets and six multimodal benchmarks, CatchUp achieves the best grounding performance across all eight splits and the highest average multimodal retention among evaluated efficient methods at 64-, 128-, and 192-token budgets. At a 64-token budget, CatchUp processes 88.9% fewer active visual tokens per post-routing decoder interval, retains 92.17% of dense-model multimodal performance, and reduces end-to-end latency by 8.0%. Code is available at https://github.com/pej0918/CatchUp

This paper presents a new method for the zero-shot open-vocabulary semantic segmentation (OVSS) of 3D automotive lidar data. Abstract
Deploying Vision-Language Models for video anomaly detection confronts a fundamental constraint. Their semantic reasoning demands computational budgets incompatible with the scale of real-world video streams. We present Signal-Aware Adaptive Fusion (SAAF), a framework that achieves efficient zero-shot anomaly detection through three key innovations. First, we introduce a dual-signal adaptive sampler operating in semantic embedding space: a delta signal captures sudden events via frame-to-frame cosine distance, while a drift signal detects static and gradual anomalies through deviation from rolling temporal context. Their OR-gated combination with adaptive EMA-based thresholding provides comprehensive anomaly coverage without training data. Second, we propose a threshold-gated cascaded architecture where lightweight frame-level scoring precedes expensive video-level temporal analysis, invoking it only when confidence warrants additional context. Third, we design a signal-aware weighted fusion mechanism that dynamically adapts score combination based on which signal triggered frame selection. Across four benchmarks, SAAF delivers competitive accuracy at substantially lower cost. Among training-free zero-shot methods, SAAF sets a new state of the art on XD-Violence (92.07%), MSAD (86.70%), and ShanghaiTech (80.54%) AUC-ROC, with XD-Violence and MSAD also surpassing supervised baselines. SAAF sustains 44.95, 48.61, and 41.06 FPS on XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, and MSAD, a 2.43$\times$, 2.63$\times$, and 2.22$\times$ throughput improvement over the prior best zero-shot method. Abstract
Photonic crystal fibers (PCFs) underpin critical applications in telecommunications, sensing, and high-power laser delivery, yet their design remains a manual, expert-intensive process that couples geometric intuition with deep waveguide physics. Vision-language models (VLMs) offer a promising path toward automating PCF design, but can they truly reason about photonic physics from microscopy images, or do they merely recognize visual patterns? We introduce PCFBench, the first comprehensive benchmark for evaluating VLMs on the perception, reasoning, and decision steps of the photonic inverse-design loop. PCFBench spans 26 tasks organized into six categories of increasing cognitive demand (Geometry Perception, Physics Understanding, Text Generation, Multi-modal Reasoning, Inverse-Design Decision, and Code Generation), built from over 220K FDTD simulation samples across 18 structurally diverse fiber families. We evaluate both individual VLMs on isolated tasks and multi-agent systems (MAS) on the full sequential pipeline from perception to design. Our results reveal a striking perception-design gap: current models can identify fiber geometry with moderate success, yet consistently fail to translate that visual understanding into physics-aware design actions. Moreover, cascading agents through the pipeline exposes significant error propagation, where upstream perception mistakes compound into downstream design failures. These findings suggest that neither model scaling nor naive agent chaining suffices, and that closing the loop demands explicit physical grounding. PCFBench provides the community with a rigorous, multi-dimensional testbed to measure progress toward AI-assisted photonic engineering. The project is available at https://pcfbench.github.io/.

Abstract
Vision-language models used for decisions in histopathology rarely return calibrated probabilities over the candidates a user cares about. Decision-native models write a question and a candidate set chosen at run time into the input and return a normalised distribution from a small decision head in one forward pass. We build PathJev-P, a decision-native model for histopathology: MedGemma-1.5-4B with LoRA adapters and a decision head, trained on 40,000 patch states from three public collections whose expert labels are turned into typed questions with organ-specific candidate sets and a "none of these" option. A supervised linear probe on a pathology foundation model is better calibrated in every setting. In a qualitative whole-slide demonstration, PathJev-P labels unsupervised tissue clusters with abstention and separates carcinoma from other tissue while running the model on a fraction of the tiles.

Probing Collision Grounding in Vision-Language Models for Safe Human-Robot Collaboration
Authors: Jun Wang, Xiaohao Xu, Xiaonan Huang
Venue: CoRL 2026

Abstract
Safe human--robot collaboration requires more than visual description: a monitor must determine whether the robot body is safely separated, already colliding with the scene or a person, or about to collide. We call this capability collision grounding: binding visual observations to robot body geometry, camera viewpoint, scene layout, human proximity, and temporal motion in order to infer present and imminent contact. We introduce TouchSafeBench, a physics-grounded benchmark for evaluating collision grounding in vision-language models (VLMs). Built in Habitat~3.0, TouchSafeBench contains 2,940 simulated indoor co-presence episodes across social navigation and social rearrangement, with synchronized multi-view RGB-D observations, top-down trajectory maps, calibrated camera metadata, and simulator-derived contact labels. We study two deployment-facing tasks: classifying the current safety state and warning about imminent collision before contact. Across three frontier or robotics-oriented VLMs and nine visual representations, current models remain far from reliable: the best average Macro-F1 stays below 50\%, explicit depth is not automatically transformed into robot-body collision evidence, and robot--scene contact is consistently harder than human-contact risk. TouchSafeBench reveals a central limitation of embodied VLMs: visual fluency does not imply physical accountability. Reliable robot safety monitors will need representations that explicitly bind viewpoint, robot morphology, metric geometry, and future collision. The benchmark is available at https://jun25336-bit.github.io/TouchSafeBench/ .

PROVE: Probabilistic Visual Grounding via Patch-Level Evidence for Vision-Language Models
Authors: Mayank Nautiyal, Tianru Zhang, Li Ju, Ekta Vats, Prashant Singh
First: 2026-10-23T10:06:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T10:06:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Visual grounding with vision-language models such as CLIP typically produces a single deterministic relevance map for an image-query pair, without indicating whether the localisation is reliable or whether the referent is present at all. We introduce \textbf{PROVE} (\textbf{Pro}babilistic \textbf{V}isual Grounding via Patch-Level \textbf{E}vidence), a post-hoc evidential adapter that maps frozen CLIP patch features to per-patch Beta distributions without modifying the backbone. PROVE casts grounding as per-patch membership: each patch accumulates positive and negative evidence for whether it belongs to the referent, so a query may match zero, one, or many patches. We train PROVE with an evidential objective on soft patch-coverage targets, and define a prior-pull objective that keeps absent-referent examples near an uninformative prior, so that vacuity serves as an absence signal without a dedicated presence classifier. During inference, a single forward pass yields, in closed form, a posterior-mean grounding map, a spatial vacuity map, and an image-level absence score. We evaluate PROVE on referring-expression benchmarks: it is competitive in localisation with state-of-the-art CLIP grounding methods, its uncertainty tracks localisation error and supports selective prediction under abstention, and its image-level score detects when the referent is absent altogether. Our evidential patch-membership modelling offers a simple, drop-in mechanism for probabilistic verification in frozen vision-language grounding systems.

When to Stop Penalizing Actions? Martingale GRPO for VLA Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Zongyao Zhao, Cheng-Jie Zhao, Ruihang Jiang, Bing Zheng, Zhaolin Wang, Yuanwei Liu
First: 2026-10-23T09:56:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T09:56:27+00:00
Comments: 31 pages, 9 figures
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A failed vision-language-action (VLA) rollout does not imply that every action contributed to failure. Yet in group-relative policy optimization (GRPO), a single trajectory-level advantage is assigned to all actions in a rollout. Potentially useful recovery attempts can thus be penalized alongside the errors they aim to correct. Ideal stopping conditions for failure feedback are characterized using success-probability and policy-score martingales. At these stops, the removed gradient contribution has zero conditional expectation given the retained information. Truncation therefore preserves the expectation of a reference GRPO group-gradient estimator and can reduce its covariance. One such stop occurs when the conditional probability of success is zero, motivating error and recovery cues for selecting candidate feedback boundaries. Martingale GRPO (MART-GRPO) is introduced with a vision-language model (VLM) for event-guided truncation. The VLM is fine-tuned on our annotated training set using low-rank adaptation (LoRA). The last visible error without observed recovery is localized by the frozen VLM in complete rollout videos. Failure feedback is retained through the selected error interval and removed afterward, while successful-rollout loss terms and original loss normalization are preserved. For retrospective boundaries, changes in expectation and variance are analyzed to assess departures from ideal stopping. Experiments with OpenVLA-OFT on RoboTwin 2.0 show that MART-GRPO improves success rates over full-trajectory GRPO across multiple tasks.

RSure-Agent: Reliable Use of Tool Observations for Remote Sensing Agents
Authors: Fuyuan Liu, Nayu Liu, Wenhao Yu, Peijin Wang, Yingchao Feng, Fanglong Yao, Yue Zhou, Xue Yang, Chao Ren, Liang Wan, Wei Feng
First: 2026-10-04T00:43:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T09:54:26+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Remote sensing agents rely on perception, measurement, and raster analysis tools to solve Earth observation tasks. We refer to their judgments and quantitative results about ground objects as tool observations. However, these observations are subject to uncertainty and may be incorrect even when tools execute successfully. When agents accept incorrect observations, the errors can propagate through subsequent reasoning and cause task failure. We analyze 1,229 execution trajectories across three remote sensing agent benchmarks. On each benchmark, at least 88.1 percent of tasks depend on tool observations. Among these tasks, at least 22.7 percent contain incorrect observations despite successful tool execution. These errors propagate to the final answer in at least 82.0 percent of affected tasks on each benchmark. To address this problem, we propose RSure-Agent, a framework for verifying tool observations and limiting error propagation. We introduce a verifiable observation protocol that requires tools to return process evidence for the agent to verify their observations. We construct a task-tool reliability prior from offline task feedback. The prior summarizes each tool configuration's past performance across task types and provides a task-specific reference for verification. Using process evidence and this prior, RSure-Agent decides whether to accept an observation, request additional evidence, or reject it. We evaluate RSure-Agent on EarthBench, ThinkGeo, TerraLogic, and CHOICE-420. Across three agent benchmarks, RSure-Agent reduces the error propagation rate by 21.3 to 25.9 percentage points relative to the base configuration with verification and the prior disabled. On CHOICE-420, it improves overall accuracy over direct answering by 5.71 percentage points on average across 11 backbone models. On EarthBench, it reduces the tool-call ratio by 25.9 percent relative to Earth-Agent.

Cycle-of-Thought: Self-Evaluative Iterative Reasoning for Table Visual Question Answering in Document Images
Authors: Somraj Gautam, Gaurav Harit
First: 2026-10-23T09:50:37+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-23T09:50:37+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Recent advances in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have significantly improved Visual Question Answering (VQA) performance, yet their reasoning over structured content such as tables remains inconsistent, often plagued by spatial misalignment and logical hallucinations. Existing approaches for improving table VQA typically rely on architectural modifications or expensive supervised fine-tuning (SFT), which impose high barriers to entry due to the need for large-scale, expert-annotated datasets and significant computational resources. In this work, we propose Self-RefineVQA, a training-free, inference-time self-correction framework that improves table VQA performance without additional labeled data or model updates. Inspired by human revision behavior, our method enables a single VLM to iteratively act as a generator, critic, and refiner, forming a closed self-feedback loop that corrects common reasoning errors through prompt-guided re-evaluation. Abstract

Abstract
