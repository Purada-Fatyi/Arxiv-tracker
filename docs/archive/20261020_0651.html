Frozen Models, Evolving Expertise: Model-Agnostic Learning from Deployment Experience for Multimodal Medical AI
Authors: Yexiao He, Yucheng Tang, Pengfei Guo, Yufan He, Andriy Myronenko, Can Zhao, Ang Li, Daguang Xu, Dong Yang
First: 2026-10-06T21:41:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:58:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) are usually frozen after deployment, so they do not learn from the cases they solve. This is especially concerning in medicine, where new clinical evidence, updated guidelines, and new therapies can change established practice. Fine-tuning can update the model, but it requires access to model weights and additional training. Parameter-free methods avoid training, but they may overfit a fixed validation set, lack reliable domain knowledge, or lose visual details by saving experience only as text. To address these limitations, we present a model-agnostic framework that allows frozen LLMs and VLMs to learn from deployment experience through three forms of external expertise: a Skill that guides reasoning and tool use, a Knowledge Memory that stores reliable facts supported by earlier cases or trusted external evidence, and a Multimodal Knowledge Base that keeps visual examples and guides the model to relate each retrieved case to the current image. Instead of relying on a fixed validation set, a validation strategy keeps an update only if it helps on new cases without degrading performance on earlier ones. Across six benchmarks covering clinical diagnosis, clinical workflows, medical reasoning, and medical and non-medical visual reasoning, and with four open-weight and closed-source base models, our framework improves performance during online deployment by up to 34.2% over the base model on medical tasks, generalizes to unseen cases, transfers to other models without further optimization, and works in non-medical domains.

Large language models (LLMs) and vision-language models (VLMs) are usually frozen after deployment, so they do not learn from the cases they solve.
RCPN: towards robust pseudo-labels in unsupervised CLIP adaptation via consistent prototypes and neighborhoods
Authors: Eman Ali, Chetan Arora, Muhammad Haris Khan
First: 2025-07-22T19:08:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T17:28:20+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In unsupervised adaptation for vision-language models such as CLIP, pseudo-labels derived from zero-shot predictions often exhibit significant noise, particularly under domain shifts or in visually complex scenarios. Conventional pseudo-label filtering approaches, which rely on fixed confidence thresholds, tend to be unreliable in fully unsupervised settings. In this work, we propose a novel adaptive pseudo-labeling framework that enhances CLIP's adaptation performance by integrating prototype consistency and neighborhood-based consistency. The proposed method comprises two key components: PICS, which assesses pseudo-label accuracy based on in-class feature compactness and cross-class feature separation; and NALR, which exploits semantic similarities among neighboring samples to refine pseudo-labels dynamically. Additionally, we introduce an adaptive weighting mechanism that adjusts the influence of pseudo-labeled samples during training according to their estimated correctness. Extensive experiments on 11 benchmark datasets demonstrate that our method achieves state-of-the-art performance in unsupervised adaptation scenarios, delivering more accurate pseudo-labels while maintaining computational efficiency.

FedRAPP: Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning for Vision-Language Models with Reliability-Aware Prompt Personalization
Authors: Syed Md. Ahnaf Hasan, Md Akil Raihan Iftee, Sajib Mistry, Amin Ahsan Ali, AKM Mahbubur Rahman
First: 2026-10-16T16:29:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:29:15+00:00
Comments: 15 pages, 5 figures and 10 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Federated prompt learning enables vision-language models like CLIP to collaboratively adapt lightweight soft prompts across distributed clients without sharing raw data. However, existing federated prompt learning methods are designed primarily for supervised settings with labeled client data and assumed relatively stable training distributions. At test time, source-free federated prompt tuning remains largely unexplored. Clients may receive unlabeled target samples with heterogeneous, often non-IID distributions that differ substantially across clients and from the source training distribution, causing locally adapted prompts to drift in different directions and reducing the effectiveness of federated aggregation. In this work, we explore Source-Free Federated Test-Time Prompt Tuning (SF-FTTPT), where distributed clients collaboratively adapt soft prompts on unlabeled test streams without access to source data. Our study reveals that conventional federated aggregation methods struggle under heterogeneous client distributions, as locally adapted prompts become increasingly specialized and difficult to combine effectively. To address this challenge, we propose FedRAPP, a personalized aggregation framework that combines reliability-aware client assessment, server-side exponential moving-average prompt memory, and similarity-guided peer aggregation. FedRAPP selectively incorporates information from reliable and compatible clients while preserving local specialization, enabling more robust adaptation under distribution shift. Extensive experiments across nine CLIP benchmark datasets and ImageNet variants demonstrate that FedRAPP consistently outperforms standard federated aggregation baselines under heterogeneous client distributions while maintaining the efficiency advantages of prompt-only adaptation.

Learning to Reason About Implicit Multimodal Harm: From Diagnosis to Generalization
Authors: Anisha Saha, Varsha Suresh, Teodora Kamova, Sophia Wiedmann, Timothy Hospedales, Vera Demberg
First: 2026-05-28T13:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T16:01:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A benign image paired with a benign caption can become harmful only in combination, making detection fundamentally a cross-modal reasoning problem. Existing multimodal harm benchmarks evaluate only classification accuracy, leaving unclear whether models identify the cross-modal mechanism that produces harm. We introduce MuPHI (Multimodal Pragmatic Harm Interpretation), a controlled diagnostic benchmark in which harm arises purely from image-text composition, with benign counterfactuals and annotated harm rationales. Using MuPHI, we systematically evaluate where compositional harm reasoning breaks in vision-language models (VLMs). MuPHI reveals that VLMs reliably ground each modality yet fail to bind the evidence into the harm mechanism and their predictions respond weakly to label-determining counterfactual interventions. Moreover, existing harm detection approaches trained on labels substantially improves in-domain (ID) detection but transfers poorly across harm distributions. We therefore propose Compositional Harm Interpretation Learning (CHIL), which rewards evidence identification in each modality, cross-modal binding and verdict consistency in the reasoning chain rather than just predicted label. CHIL improves 81% OOD transfer evaluations by an average of +2.5 macro-F1 and correct counterfactual flip rate by up to +3.8 and +5.4 % points, while also improving overall reasoning quality over label-only optimization. These results suggest that optimizing how a model reasons, not just what it concludes, offers a promising direction for multimodal safety systems that generalize beyond dataset-specific correlations.

FlexPart: Text-Controlled Semantics-Aware 3D Part Segmentation at Flexible Granularity

Abstract
A 3D asset admits multiple valid part decompositions, while semantics-aware segmentation should reflect both the desired granularity and the parts a user wants to extract. Ideally, a practical segmentation model should strive for a simple principle: what you ask for is what you get. However, realizing this goal is hindered by two core obstacles: the lack of multi-granularity training data and the absence of a holistic control mechanism. We propose FLEXPART, a unified framework for text-controlled 3D mesh segmentation. To establish flexible control, we introduce a holistic control prompt comprising semantic part names with location and shape clauses. To support this mechanism, we utilize an automated Vision-Language Model (VLM) pipeline to construct a large-scale dataset of multi-granularity partitions and their corresponding prompts. Driven by this data, FLEXPART generates a view-independent part-identity field in native 3D space, directly assigning discrete part IDs to mesh faces. To compel the model to respect semantic conditions rather than rely on geometric priors, we train the model using randomized background dropping. Furthermore, an inference-time VLM planner can automatically complete descriptive clauses from 2D renderings, allowing users to simply name the desired parts in ordinary words. FLEXPART with planner-completed prompts outperforms the strongest evaluated baseline by 15% in mIoU, even when that baseline receives ground-truth guidance. On our held-out FLEXPARTBENCH, FLEXPART leads at each granularity and improves average mIoU by 22% over the strongest baseline. With only the requested part names supplied by the user, it also improves IoU by 35% over the strongest click-driven baseline on selective segmentation.

A Language-Informed Loss for Visual Generation
Authors: Suchir Agarwal, Keshigeyan Chandrasegaran, Ziyu Chen, Kyle Sargent, Juan Carlos Niebles, Li Fei-Fei, Jiajun Wu
First: 2026-10-16T15:49:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:49:12+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
End-to-end visual generation with pixel-space loss has recently achieved competitive performance without the need for learned image tokenizers. In this work, we study whether language-informed supervision can provide a richer training signal than pixel loss for visual generation. We introduce LILIE (pronounced "lily"), a Language-Informed and Lossless Image Encoder. LILIE is an invertible image transform trained such that a vision-language model can recover captions from noised features. The encoder uses a normalizing flow transformer trained jointly with a vision-language model via next token prediction. We then use the encoder to extract a simple language-informed loss for text-to-image generation. We train JiT models with LILIE loss, reducing gFD by 14 points (66.84 $\to$ 53.01) against a pixel-only baseline on the GPIC dataset. Our code, encoders, and generative models are available at https://suchiragarwal.com/lilie.

Would you still call this Dax? Novel Visual References in VLMs and Humans
Authors: Ada Defne Tür, Gaurav Kamath, Joyce Chai, Siva Reddy, Benno Krojer
Venue: EMNLP
First: 2026-06-03T20:23:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:49:10+00:00

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs), like human learners, are frequently exposed to new visual concepts, but how they map novel visual references to language after exposure remains largely underexplored, particularly when those references contradict prior knowledge from pre-training. To study this, we present the Novel Visual References Dataset (NVRD): 19,176 images spanning 90 visual concepts across different levels of visual novelty, each with up to 20 increasingly perturbed versions of the original object to probe generalization. Unlike prior work on visual augmentations of familiar concepts, NVRD comprises entirely novel, open-ended stimuli constructed from scratch, mirroring how humans encounter genuinely new concepts. We evaluate 3 open- and 2 closed-source models alongside 2,400 human judgments for direct human-model comparison, and find that (i) models struggle to acquire novel concepts in-context when they contradict prior knowledge, and (ii) while models and humans show correlated sensitivity to visual perturbations, models significantly overgeneralize, extending learned labels to stimuli that humans reject. We contribute NVRD as a corpus and benchmark for research on visual concept learning in both humans and machines.

Not Every Mention Is a Claim: Rethinking Object Hallucination Detection in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Hsin-Ling Hsu, Neeraja Kirtane, Kuan-Hao Huang
First: 2026-10-16T15:23:45+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T15:23:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Object hallucination in vision-language models (VLMs) is usually measured by checking each object a caption names against the image. Most benchmarks and detectors alike treat every object mention as a claim that the object is present. This assumption fails when a caption names an object without asserting it, as in "a bench for people to sit on", which mentions people without claiming that any are in the image. We call such mentions non-assertive. Treating them as hallucinations inflates the error rates attributed to models that use such language. To separate them from real hallucinations, we introduce NAME-Bench (Non-Assertive Mention Evaluation Benchmark), 25,497 object mentions from captions that five VLMs generate for COCO images. Each mention is labelled concrete (asserted and present), hallucinated (asserted and absent), or non-assertive, and non-assertive mentions are further divided into four subtypes: depictive, purpose, negation and hedged. All the hallucination detectors that we evaluate flag non-assertive mentions as hallucinations, at rates between 14% and 80%. Adding non-assertive mentions to detector training as negatives largely corrects this on LLaVA-1.5. On the four newer VLMs, a comparable correction takes five to ten times more data, and the detectors then catch fewer real hallucinations. These findings show that current detectors conflate mentioning an object with asserting it, and that this conflation is harder to undo on the four newer VLMs than on LLaVA-1.5. Hallucination evaluation should therefore separate what a caption asserts from what it merely mentions.

When Evidence Vanishes: Distilling Visual-Evidence Dependency in Medical Vision-Language Models
Authors: Qizhen Lan, Yiling Ma, Ruiqi Chen, Mengchen Fan, Xi Xiao, Lin Zhao, Liang He, Baocheng Geng, Pulin Che, Tianyang Wang, Xiaoqian Jiang
First: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T13:48:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors. Standard knowledge distillation copies a teacher's answer preference on the original image, but it leaves unchecked whether that preference is conditional on the visual evidence required by the question. We study this missing target as medical visual evidence dependency: support for the correct answer should decrease when the matched evidence is absent. We introduce Evidence-Conditioned Dependency Distillation (ECDD), which pairs each training image with a control matched to the evidence level and distills the teacher's drop in answer margin across the pair. A lower bound objective transfers positive dependency by penalizing only shortfalls, while original-image supervision preserves task accuracy. The same formulation applies when the evidence is a queried clinical region, a surgical instrument, or whole-image visual content. Across three medical VQA settings, ECDD keeps original-image accuracy competitive with distillation and alignment baselines while making answer support more sensitive to evidence removal. Under the DME headline objective, for example, the positive-case margin response to target rather than random masking rises from 0.786 with KD to 2.023 with ECDD, while original-image accuracy differs by 0.11 points. These results suggest that medical VLM distillation should transfer both the teacher's answer preference and its dependence on visual evidence.

Compact medical vision-language models (VLMs) can answer clinical questions correctly without reliably using the image, relying instead on question wording or learned priors.

Abstract
LLMs and LVLMs are increasingly deployed across heterogeneous hardware, ranging from data-center GPUs to resource-constrained edge devices. Their fixed-size architectures limit the ability to reuse a single model across deployment settings with different compute, latency, and model-size targets. We introduce HydraLLM, an elastic MQA-based architecture. Abstract
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) are commonly prompted with text alone or together with an image. In this paper, we find a new aspect of LVLM vulnerability in its response robustness: adding an image can unexpectedly affect responses even when text prompts ask about a concept rather than the instance of the concept depicted in the image. For example, adding an image of an animal can change the generated attribute descriptions of its breed, and framing of the image further affects those changes. We study this new problem on seven LVLMs with image sets covering 30 animal breeds and eight food categories, using two types of framings that focus on the subject (subject-focused) or place it in a broader context (subject-in-situation). (2) attribute-term distributions differ significantly between the two framing types for 41.4\% of animal and 71.4\% of food model--concept pairs, with generally higher physical-term proportions for animals and sensory-term proportions for food under subject-focused than subject-in-situation framing.

ShadowCast: On-the-Fly Context-Aware Shadow Augmentation for Remote Sensing Image Segmentation
Authors: Yechan Kim, Dongho Yoon, Jongmin Joo, JongHyun Park, Moongu Jeon
First: 2026-10-16T13:23:56+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T13:23:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Shadows cast by elevated objects alter the spectral appearance and obscure the boundaries of objects in remote sensing images, thereby degrading semantic segmentation performance. Most existing approaches handle shadows as degradations to be detected and removed, requiring additional costs from dedicated networks and shadow-specific annotations. We propose ShadowCast, which takes the opposite approach: it synthesizes shadows and injects them during training for remote sensing segmentation. It extracts masks of elevated objects, such as buildings and trees, directly from the segmentation labels and estimates an image-specific cast-shadow direction cue by probing the brightness around them. It then projects synthetic shadows along the inferred cast-shadow direction, requiring no learned network and no acquisition metadata. Experiments on three widely used remote sensing benchmarks, Vaihingen, Potsdam, and LoveDA, show that ShadowCast consistently outperforms the baseline across diverse state-of-the-art segmentation architectures. Moreover, it brings additional performance gains when combined with standard augmentation and regularization techniques, indicating that stochastic shadow injection serves as a simple and complementary augmentation strategy for remote sensing segmentation.

GeoReason: Bridging Logical Reasoning and Spatial Fidelity in Remote Sensing Segmentation
Authors: Cheng Fan, Han Wu, Tingzhang Luo, Minjing Dong, Yehui Tang, Jianyuan Guo

Abstract
Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) have significantly advanced instruction-driven perception through the "Embedding-as-Mask" paradigm. However, extending this capability to Remote Sensing (RS) reasoning segmentation remains exceptionally challenging under cluttered geographical contexts and extreme scale variations, often leading to Spatial Fidelity Degradation and Target Misidentification. We identify a critical yet underexplored bottleneck: the sequential alienation of the segmentation token [SEG] from its initial visual grounding within the autoregressive generation paradigm. Produced only at the end of long reasoning chains, [SEG] relies on visual evidence that has been progressively diluted by linguistic abstraction and autoregressive noise. This information decay is particularly catastrophic for RS targets with minuscule pixel footprints, where even marginal fidelity loss leads to localization failure. To address these bottlenecks, we propose GeoReason, a novel framework designed to fortify logical deduction and preserve spatial integrity. First, we introduce an Anticipatory Prior mechanism that injects the [SEG] token directly into the initial user query, shifting it from a terminal output to a primary condition. This paradigm shift transforms the token into a persistent spatial anchor, preventing information decay during long-form linguistic inference. Second, we enhance visual reasoning via a Location-aware Chain-of-Thought (CoT) that enforces coarse-to-fine localization using bounding boxes, coupled with a Latent Patch-to-Token Alignment (PTA) Loss that explicitly aligns the latent segmentation token with physical RS textures. Extensive experiments demonstrate that GeoReason consistently outperforms state-of-the-art methods on the Earthreason and RRSIS-D benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/Fanc0126/GeoReason.

Authors: Shadman Rabby, Md. Hefzul Hossain Papon, Sabbir Ahmed, Nokimul Hasan Arif, A. B. M. Ashikur Rahman, Irfan Ahmad
First: 2026-02-09T06:34:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-16T12:59:09+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Sycophancy in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) refers to their tendency to align with user opinions, often at the expense of moral or factual accuracy. While prior studies have explored sycophantic behavior in general contexts, its impact on morally grounded visual decision-making remains insufficiently understood. To address this gap, we present the first systematic study of moral sycophancy in VLMs, analyzing ten widely-used models on the Moralise and M^3oralBench datasets under explicit user disagreement. Our results reveal that VLMs frequently produce morally incorrect follow-up responses even when their initial judgments are correct, and exhibit a consistent asymmetry: models are more likely to shift from morally right to morally wrong judgments than the reverse when exposed to user-induced bias. Follow-up prompts generally degrade performance on Moralise, while yielding mixed or even improved accuracy on M^3oralBench, highlighting dataset-dependent differences in moral robustness. Evaluation using Error Introduction Rate (EIR) and Error Correction Rate (ECR) reveals a clear trade-off: models with stronger error-correction capabilities tend to introduce more reasoning errors, whereas more conservative models minimize errors but exhibit limited ability to self-correct. Finally, initial contexts with a morally right stance elicit stronger sycophantic behavior, emphasizing the vulnerability of VLMs to moral influence and the need for principled strategies to improve ethical consistency and robustness in multimodal AI systems.

Abstract
Web-based visual search increasingly handles queries driven by semantic intent, focusing on what is happening in an image rather than merely what it looks like. While global embeddings from vision-language models capture overall appearance, they often blur fine-grained relational structure. Conversely, scene graphs make these relationships explicit, but generating them automatically for unannotated web images produces dense, noisy graphs that retain every detected entity and relation regardless of relevance. To address this, we introduce PRISm (Pruning-based Image Retrieval via Importance Prediction on Semantic Graphs), an image-to-image retrieval framework that learns to focus on what matters in a scene. On PSG, PRISm outperforms the strongest baseline by points in MAP@1. Furthermore, on Flickr with machine-generated scene graphs and captions, PRISm remains best on top-1 metrics, while adding only 33,ms per query beyond scene graph generation.

Abstract
As climate change intensifies, floods are becoming more frequent and destructive, making accurate flood understanding an urgent priority. However, existing vision-language models perform poorly on flood-related tasks, suffering from attentional bias and inaccurate descriptions of flood magnitude. To bridge this gap, we construct the first large-scale remote sensing flood multi-modal benchmark that supports language-level flood understanding and pixel-level flood segmentation. The benchmark contains 131,200 flood image patches and corresponding 443,100 textual descriptions for flood severity and evolution. Based on this benchmark, we propose FloodVLM-Pix, a language-guided pixel-level flood understanding model that includes a flood prior module and a multi-temporal flood pixel decoder to achieve accurate flood understanding.
