Perceive, Predict, and Plan: a Unified World Action Model for Egocentric Dexterous Manipulation
Authors: Zirui Wu, Chen Guo, Tianjian Jiang, Zeren Jiang, Zeming Li, Lingchen Yang, Longteng Duan, Ying Xue, Handi Yin, Shenyuan Gao, Martin R. Oswald, Jun Zhang, Ping Tan, Jie Song
First: 2026-10-15T17:59:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:59:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We introduce P$^{3}$-WAM, a unified world action model for egocentric dexterous manipulation that learns 3D perception, action-conditioned prediction, and goal-directed planning through three complementary modes: Perceive, which reconstructs articulated hand motion and camera trajectories from video; Predict, which generates visual futures under prescribed actions; and Plan, which jointly generates action trajectories and their anticipated visual consequences conditioned on an initial image, a language instruction, and an optional visual goal. Our model integrates a compact, structured 3D action representation into a pretrained video diffusion transformer through a mixture-of-transformers architecture with shared base weights, modality-specific adaptation, and bidirectional visual-action attention. Randomized modality visibility lets video and 3D actions serve as either conditioning context or prediction targets across the three modes. Experiments show that unified training improves all three capabilities over single-mode training, including gains in planning quality under a vision-language model evaluation. Through unified training, our model outperforms specialized baselines on 3D hand reconstruction and action-conditioned video prediction across four datasets, while producing goal-directed manipulation videos with stronger instruction adherence and action progression than video-generation baselines. DK-VLM: Geometry as Position for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Han Su, Meng Tian, Tianyu Huang, Mingyu Gao, Lennox Wei, Dongxu Li, Jianhua Han, Hang Xu, Xiaohe Wu, Wangmeng Zuo
First: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:58:15+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models still struggle to understand 3D spatial relations across views and update spatial judgments as the observer moves. A common approach fuses 3D reconstruction features into visual tokens, but aligning these features with pretrained semantic representations remains challenging under limited spatial supervision. We propose DK-VLM with Dual-Kernel Attention, which computes attention from both a visual token's input position and its geometric position in the scene. A semantic kernel retains the pretrained model's native attention and positional encoding, while a separate geometric kernel uses estimated 3D points and camera poses to directly modulate attention through point displacements and relative view transformations. Then, a learned gate fuses their outputs. Geometry thus enters attention as position rather than as fused reconstruction features. We further introduce Reference-Frame Composition (RC) supervision to train the model to update its reference frame across successive judgments. It provides stepwise turn and heading targets derived from verified single-step annotations. Among the compared methods, DK-VLM-8B achieves the highest average score across six spatial benchmarks and the highest overall scores on VSI-Bench (74.33) and ReVSI-32 (58.28). RC supervision further improves multi-step spatial reasoning, while the full model preserves general video understanding. Project page: https://sumuru789.github.io/DK-VLM-website/.

EmbodiedMidtrain: Bridging the Gap between Vision-Language Models and Vision-Language-Action Models via Mid-training
Authors: Yiyang Du, Zhanqiu Guo, Haochen Zhang, Annabella Macaluso, Xin Ye, Liu Ren, Yonatan Bisk, Chenyan Xiong
First: 2026-04-21T21:40:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:55:44+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language-Action Models (VLAs) inherit their visual and linguistic capabilities from Vision-Language Models (VLMs), yet most VLAs are built from off-the-shelf VLMs that are not adapted to the embodied domain, limiting their downstream performance. In this work, we propose EmbodiedMidtrain to bridge the gap between VLMs and VLAs. We first characterize the data distribution gap between them, showing that VLA data occupy compact regions that are largely separated from the broader VLM distribution, while the degree of alignment varies substantially both across and within VLM data sources. Then, we build a mid-training data engine that leverages a lightweight learnable proximity estimator to select the most VLA-aligned candidates from a large VLM pool, and produce EmbodiedMidtrain-1M, a VLM dataset that we further use to mid-train the VLM before downstream VLA fine-tuning. Experiments on both simulated robot manipulation benchmarks and real-world tasks show consistent VLA performance gains over off-the-shelf checkpoints across VLM backbones and VLA action heads. These gains extend to downstream tasks not used to guide data selection. Further analysis reveals that mid-training provides a stronger initialization for VLA fine-tuning, with gains emerging from the earliest steps and widening throughout training. Moreover, the data engine captures both dataset-level and sample-level alignment signals, favoring spatial reasoning over text-centric tasks while preserving the diversity of the VLM data. We will release all code, data and models for future research.

MJev-Spatial: System One Spatial Intelligence via Native Attention Matching
Authors: Luozheng Qin, Yuhan Zhu, Changhai Zhou, Qian Qiao, Jun Gao, Yuhua Zhou, Harry Zhang, Xiangyu Zeng, Yuxuan Liu, Kaijie Chen, Andrew Chen, Pony Ma
First: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:37:50+00:00
Comments: 37 pages, 7 figures, 27 tables. Code: https://github.com/Fr0zenCrane/MJev-Spatial
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. In light of this, we introduce MJev-Spatial, a System 1 multimodal decision model that tackles all three types of spatial understanding tasks within a unified classification framework. Specifically, MJev-Spatial treats each candidate as a class and scores it using the backbone's pretrained query and key projections, without adding parameters. The candidates are the given options for choice tasks, or value bins and image regions refined through coarse-to-fine classification for numerical and point prediction, respectively. At inference, each classification decision takes a single forward pass and generates no answer tokens. To train on heterogeneous spatial data efficiently, we supervise all coarse-to-fine stages jointly with teacher forcing in one forward pass, and combine this with length-aware global batching and cross-GPU load balancing, raising training throughput by 30.8% over the original batching strategy. Built on Qwen3.5-4B and trained on 12.3M question-answer pairs converted from existing spatial datasets, MJev-Spatial achieves the highest overall score (65.64%) among eight tested models, ranks first on seven of twelve benchmarks, and outperforms its unmodified autoregressive base model on all twelve, while running 2.5 times faster than the fastest autoregressive baseline and 5.6 times faster than the base model.

Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation for Unified Vision-Language Models
Authors: Yuqi Xiong, Chunyi Peng, Zhipeng Xu, Qing Yang, Zhenghao Liu, Zulong Chen, Yu Gu, Ge Yu
First: 2026-10-15T17:01:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T17:01:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning (RL) can elicit specialized visual capabilities in vision-language models (VLMs), yet jointly optimizing heterogeneous objectives often introduces cross-capability gradient conflicts. Training experts independently mitigates this interference, but direct reward optimization may entangle specialized knowledge with reward-hacking biases. This paper proposes Skill-Decoupled On-Policy Expert Distillation (SCOPED), which trains capability-specific experts and extracts explicit skills through reward-guided reflection. During on-policy distillation, these skills guide expert supervision while remaining unavailable to the unified student, encouraging it to internalize procedural knowledge without explicit skills at inference time. Across eight multimodal benchmarks, SCOPED consistently outperforms the backbone and mixed-capability RL, demonstrating the effectiveness of skill-decoupled on-policy expert distillation for integrating independently acquired capabilities. Further analyses reveal interference during joint capability optimization and suggest that skill augmentation promotes more consistent expert trajectories, greater attention to task-relevant visual evidence, and better preservation of specialized expertise in the unified student. All code and data are available at https://github.com/NEUIR/SCOPED.

The Last Remote Sensing Model Built by Humans? Casting Any Earth Observation Task as Recursive Self-Improvement
Authors: Sijie Zhao, Zeqi Zhou, Heli Qi, Xuanchao Fu, Zhuo Zheng, Junshi Xia, Junjue Wang, Weihao Xuan, Naoto Yokoya
First: 2026-10-13T17:57:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:56:46+00:00
Comments: Ongoing project
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Any verifiable task can be cast as a harness and a verifier, where the harness defines what the agent may use and the verifier defines what counts as success. Every EO task can be made verifiable, through labeled benchmarks, independent sensors, physical models or the repeated observation of the same place. Given only these two components, a general-purpose coding agent builds the EO model and improves it recursively on its own outputs. Settings long treated as separate research problems, from learning with a handful of labels to adapting across domains, become choices of harness and verifier. On six EO benchmarks spanning optical, SAR and multispectral time-series imagery, with outputs that range from land-cover maps and ship instances to canopy heights and answers about large images, agent-built models surpass the state of the art on every one, and on land-cover mapping, they do so from 11% of the training labels. This establishes a new paradigm for EO, in which people design harnesses and verifiers and agents build the models, so EO models improve as agents do.

Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand.
REKI: Registering Knowledge in VLAs
Authors: Juliette Marrie, Paul Pacaud, Adrien Ramanana Rahary, Shizhe Chen, Moritz Böhle
First: 2026-10-15T16:34:06+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T16:34:06+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
We introduce REKI, a 2.4B vision-language-action model (VLA) pretrained exclusively on public data that sets a new Pareto frontier between performance and model size: on LIBERO-Plus, RoboTwin Clean2Rand and RoboCasa365, it is outperformed only by models about twice its size. REKI consists of a vision-language model (VLM) followed by a flow-matching action expert, and is pretrained on a curated mixture of public robot, egocentric human and vision-language data. Additionally, we endow it with a memory that gives the policy access to its recent past at negligible cost: a few register tokens in the action expert summarise the output of the VLM at each frame and persist in the expert's cache. This memory improves success in simulation and on real robots, and makes oscillations between poses about four times rarer. REKI uses a simple model design and training paradigm, and is optimised to be efficient at training and inference. We release the full code across data curation, training, inference and evaluation, deployment, and all pretrained and finetuned model weights: https://github.com/kyutai-labs/reki.

Retrofitting Recurrent Vision-Language Models from Pretrained Backbones
Authors: Madhav Iyengar, Andreas Geiger, Hilde Kühne
First: 2026-10-15T15:34:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T15:34:27+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://madhavesdios.github.io/recurrent-vlms
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent work shows that depth recurrence can be retrofitted into pretrained language models. In this work, we adapt this paradigm for visual understanding, using depth recurrence to reduce the parameter footprint of a pretrained vision-language model (VLM). To this end, we prune layers from the language backbone of the VLM and loop over a subset of the remaining layers. At each step of the loop, a linear adapter merges the block's output with the token representations from the preceding layers and projects the fused representation back into the block. As the pruning impacts the original model performance, we propose a three-stage recovery training: first, we leverage a language-only pretraining to recover the performance of the language backbone, followed by an alignment of the vision-language projector, and a full fine-tuning of the model via visual instruction tuning. In the first and the last stage, we distill the outputs of the pretrained non-recurrent model into the recurrent model, which further improves the recovery. We evaluate the proposed approach on the TinyLLaVA-1.4B backbone without introducing any new vision-language data during retrofitting. Our experiments demonstrate that the retrofitted recurrent model outperforms the unpruned baseline trained with the same recovery data while storing 23.3% fewer language parameters. We further show that the proposed retrofitting recurrence outperforms the setup of converting a recurrent LM into a VLM on the same data. Additionally, our ablations show how design choices, such as the adapter between recurrences and a larger recurrent block, lead to the improved performance of our approach. Finally, we find that our proposed model gains on reasoning-oriented benchmarks, whereas its performance on basic visual understanding benchmarks remains on par with the baseline.

Memory as Weights: Internalizing Long-Term History for Streaming Videos
Authors: Zhuoming Liu, Jinhong Lin, Lin Zhang, Yiwu Zhong, Yin Li
First: 2026-10-15T15:29:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T15:29:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Streaming vision-language models (VLMs) must continuously integrate information from the past while remaining responsive to current observations and user queries. Existing memory mechanisms typically compress, retrieve, or cache historical observations and reintroduce them into the input context, forcing historical memory and current observations to compete for limited context. We propose MaLoW, a streaming memory framework that instead internalizes historical context into evolving lightweight parameter updates. MaLoW uses a recurrent HyperNetwork to incrementally consolidate incoming video observations into fixed-size LoRA weights, allowing historical information to be carried forward without revisiting the full video stream. In doing so, MaLoW represents history as weights, while keeping the current observation as input. To support training, we further construct MaLoW-Instruct, a large-scale streaming video dataset containing 381K question-answer pairs over 56K videos. We conduct extensive experiments to demonstrate that MaLoW achieves strong performance on OVO-Bench, StreamingBench, and StreamArena. On the backward split of OVO-Bench, MaLoW outperforms prior memory approaches by 3.9% to 11.8% across different video backbones while incurring only modest latency overhead. Beyond streaming video understanding, MaLoW also generalizes to vision-language-action models, improving the action success rate on Simpler WidowX by 9.3%.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly used to interpret religious, educational, and culturally sensitive visual content, yet their reliability on citation verification remains underexplored. We introduce Visual Sanad, a controlled benchmark for testing whether VLMs falsely accept incorrect Qur'anic verse attributions when the same quote-reference pair is rendered under different visual framings. The benchmark contains 2,400 rendered images built from 100 English Qur'an verses, four factual states per verse, and six visual conditions ranging from plain text to decorative and authority-framed layouts. All quote-reference pairs were source-checked and human-audited before rendering. Across 24 full model-prompt runs, we observe a sharp capability split. Proprietary API-served VLMs range from near-perfect citation verification to moderate false acceptance, whereas recent open-weight VLMs often show weak discrimination and default to VALID on most images. De-biased prompting can reduce false acceptance, but often by shifting the decision threshold toward INVALID rather than by producing strong citation discrimination. Visual Sanad therefore exposes a culturally grounded multimodal factuality failure: fluent VLMs may treat displayed religious citations as self-authenticating unless they have strong citation-verification ability.

Abstract
Labeled Sentinel-1 Ground Range Detected (S1GRD) datasets remain scarce, limiting supervised deep learning for land-surface remote sensing, while self-supervised pretraining for this domain has been dominated by Vision Transformer (ViT)-based masked image modeling requiring substantial data and compute. We propose SparK-SAR, a sparse convolutional masked pretraining framework built on SparK and ConvNeXt, designed to exploit convolutional inductive biases for S1GRD imagery under constrained compute. SparK-SAR makes three core technical contributions: (i) ConvNeXt-RS, a domain-adapted encoder that halves the stem stride (4x to 2x) to produce 14x14 feature maps at stride 16x, providing 4x more spatial tokens at identical parameter count; (ii) a lightweight ConvNeXt-512 reconstruction decoder with PixelShuffle upsampling that reduces pretraining VRAM by 7.5-8.5% per GPU relative to a UNet-128 baseline while matching its accuracy; and (iii) an empirical characterization of FP16 mixed-precision pretraining instability in the standard SparK UNet-768 decoder on S1GRD data, complemented by a finding that finetuning with layer-wise learning rate decay (LLRD) improves downstream performance of pretrained encoders. On four Copernicus-Bench S1GRD tasks, our finetuned 27.8M-parameter ConvNeXt-RS encoder improves over a non-pretrained baseline by +4.7, +5.0, +5.8, and +1.4 pp on EuroSAT-S1, BigEarthNet-S1, DFC2020-S1, and Flood-S1 respectively, and outperforms the Copernicus-FM ViT (139M parameters, frozen) on three of four tasks at roughly 5x fewer parameters. A pretraining data-scaling study reveals task-dependent data efficiency: classification saturates with as little as 10-25% of the SSL4EO-S12 v1.1 corpus, while dense prediction continues to improve significantly up to 25-50%. The study also includes a CKA representation analysis and a geographic de-collision experiment.

Abstract
As Vision-Language Models (VLMs) advance, they are increasingly expected to perform spatiotemporal reasoning over motion, actions, and view changes beyond what the input images show. Abstract
Surgical teams could benefit from assistants that watch the video for requested events, such as "tell me when the clip applier appears during Calot triangle dissection", and answer questions about what has already happened. We formalise this task as request-driven event detection with exact semantics and causal alert decisions, and present SurgStreamer. It separates procedure-specific perception, which maps the 1-FPS stream to a structured state over tools, actions and phases, from procedure-agnostic reasoning, which evaluates a formal monitoring condition over this state and an event memory, either with exact rules or with a small controller that addresses entities through pointers rather than names. We evaluate this monitoring core with gold conditions. On a pre-registered, sealed test set of 12 laparoscopic cholecystectomies, SurgStreamer reached a macro event-F1 of 0.598 over full procedures. On identical 75-s windows with only the window available to every method, it scored 0.677. The tested configurations of the streaming vision-language model OneStreamer-4B, including ones fine-tuned on SurgStreamer's state or on video with an adapted vision encoder, scored at most 0.540 (Holm-adjusted p <= 0.012). At three external centres, re-training only perception raised performance on held-out procedures from 0.239 to 0.470. On a new procedure, pituitary surgery, controller weights trained on cholecystectomy matched an in-domain controller on oracle state (0.947 vs 0.938). On predicted state they missed the pre-registered non-inferiority margin (-0.024 [-0.040, -0.008]) unless the alert thresholds were re-calibrated. The results support separating perception from reasoning and point to perception under distribution shift as the main limitation.

Learning to Think and Explore in 3D World
Authors: Yijiang Li, Bingyang Wang, Nuno Vasconcelos
First: 2026-10-15T12:38:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-15T12:38:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study how vision-language models (VLMs) can learn to think and actively explore in 3D space. Instead of providing multi-view images, videos, or 3D representations to the model, we preserve the native visual encoding of VLMs, i.e., a single image, one at a time, just like humans, and treat the 3D representation only as an environment with which the model can interact through actions (e.g., move left) and observations (rendered images). Each rollout begins from an initial first-person view and a visual question. The model can then iteratively reason, navigate, and reorient its viewpoint before committing to a final answer. Following an initial trajectory-supervised finetuning, we apply reinforcement learning (RL) with rewards driven primarily by final-answer correctness. Crucially, RL provides no direct supervision over which viewpoints the model should visit or which exploration actions it should take. Instead, the model must learn an effective exploration policy solely from the extent to which its interactions improve performance. Across six 3D benchmarks, our model ACT3D achieves an average accuracy of 53.2% that improves its base Qwen model by 22.0% and surpasses proprietary Gemini 3.6 Flash. ACT3D also outperforms existing open-source 3D methods while using 50-70% fewer input frames, and generalizes to unseen domains with an average improvement of 5.9% over its supervised initialization. Further analysis reveals adaptive, question-conditioned exploration. Performance gains over the base model increase from 0.6% on questions answerable from the initial view to 16.3% on those requiring extensive exploration. ACT3D searches for occluded targets, avoids unnecessary exploration, and acquires complementary viewpoints for spatial comparisons. These findings demonstrate that outcome-based RL enables VLMs not only to reason over visual evidence, but also to learn when and how to acquire it.

Abstract
Physical AI requires models to ground visual and linguistic understanding in real-world environments while accounting for environmental constraints and execution feedback. We introduce MachEmbodied-VLM (ME-VLM), a unified vision-language model with two variants, 4B and 35B-A3B, that brings together embodied cognition and multimodal agent capabilities.
