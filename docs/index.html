Multiclass Boundary Refinement with Neural Cellular Automata for Remote Sensing Land Cover Segmentation
Authors: Pascoassis Souza Santos Meira, Gilson Antonio Giraldi, Rafael Duarte Coelho dos Santos
First: 2026-10-21T16:41:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T16:41:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Semantic segmentation of remote sensing imagery often places class boundaries a few pixels away from where they truly lie, and this position error is concentrated in the thin band measured by Boundary IoU (BIoU). We treat boundary refinement as the iterated application of a small learned local rule: the Boundary Refinement Neural Cellular Automaton (BRNCA), that starts from the class probabilities of a frozen, pre-trained segmenter and corrects them cell by cell. We generalise the binary rNCA to the multiclass case by keeping the visible channels on the probability simplex through an Aitchison (log-space) update and by adding free hidden channels. The best configuration improves the mean BIoU over the base segmenter by 2.36 points (from 46.63 to 48.99). The gain is concentrated on the irregular woodland class (+8.5 points BIoU); this suggests that position error is correlated with the fractal dimension of the class boundaries.

Abstract
Automated radiology report generation for multi-sequence brain MRI holds strong promise for alleviating radiologists' workload, yet it remains a demanding problem: brain MRI studies are inherently volumetric, each pulse sequence carries complementary diagnostic information, and annotated data are critically scarce for low-resource clinical languages. Existing brain MRI-report resources are still dominated by English reports, translated clinical text, or datasets adapted from task-specific imaging benchmarks, leaving routine low-resource clinical reporting and heterogeneous multi-sequence MRI protocols insufficiently represented. To bridge this gap, we introduce ViBMR, to the best of our knowledge, the first large-scale multi-sequence brain MRI dataset paired with routine Vietnamese clinical reports, comprising 3,649 patients and 11,875 MRI series across T1, T2, FLAIR, and contrast-enhanced T1 sequences. Building on ViBMR, we establish a comprehensive evaluation setup for brain MRI report generation, covering representative medical vision-language models, multi-sequence inputs, and feature aggregation strategies. The results provide key insights into dataset characteristics and model behavior, while demonstrating the necessity of native low-resource clinical datasets for reliable Vietnamese-origin brain MRI report generation. Our dataset and baseline models will be released for non-commercial research.

Compositionality with Primitives: A Unified View from Euclidean to Hyperbolic Vision-Language Models
Authors: Puru Vaish, Meelad Dashti, Christoph Brune, Pascal Mettes, Tatiana Tommasi, Nicola Strisciuglio
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-21T14:34:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T14:34:51+00:00
Comments: Accepted at WACV 2027 Main Conference, 17 pages, 6 figures, 8 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Compositional zero-shot learning (CZSL) aims to recognize novel combinations of previously observed objects and attributes. Prior work showed that large-scale vision-language models encode decomposable semantic structures that can be recomposed to enable generalization. The Geodesically Decomposable Embeddings (GDE) framework formalizes this by identifying directions in the tangent space at the Frechet mean of the embedding distribution, which encode primitive visual concepts. Unseen attribute-object compositions can be synthesized through vector addition of these primitives. We revisit the assumption that compositional generalization requires explicit attribute-object pair re-composition. By means of new compositional scoring functions and extensive evaluations, we show that directly using the decomposed primitives suffices for recognizing unseen compositions. In that, we gain insights on how the geometry of the embedding manifold (Euclidean, spherical, or hyperbolic) and dataset structure affect compositional reasoning. Across models (CLIP, DINOv2, MERU, HyCoCLIP), we show that compositional structure consistently emerges as primitive representations, enabling inference on CZSL benchmarks (MIT-States, VAW-CZSL, UT-Zappos50K, Clothing16K) and group robustness on Waterbirds and CelebA. Key Findings for Small UAS for Post-Disaster Damage Assessment from the CURSE'26 Exercise
Authors: Thomas Manzini, Stephen Johnson, Priyankari Perali, David Merrick, Robin Murphy

Abstract
The CLARKE building and road damage assessment AI classifier was incorporated into the Catastrophic Unmanned Remote Sensing Exercise (CURSE) 2026, which trained 23 small uncrewed aerial systems (sUAS) teams from state and local agencies and non-governmental organizations in Florida and Missouri on how to collect imagery for an incident command Remote Sensing Cell. Although CLARKE has been operationally deployed to two hurricanes (Debby and Helene), CURSE'26 presented an opportunity to evaluate the machine learning model with a larger set of never-before-seen orthomosaics. The results show that building and road damage assessment together ran in 3.5 minutes on average, and that building damage assessment achieved a Micro F1 of 0.834 with no manual adjustments to correct spatial misalignment and delivered that performance in both Florida and Missouri, demonstrating good geographic transfer. It also showed that road damage assessment performed poorly due to sensitivity to misalignment between road maps and the images, and to lighting and environmental conditions in an urban setting. The analysis of CURSE'26 presents five other findings of value to the sUAS community: that the use of sUAS and machine learning is now operationally normalized and accepted, sUAS teams in the field can improve classification accuracy by timing their flights, exercises are good operational tests but their value for evaluating learning models is less clear, edge computing is needed to overcome the wireless upload barrier, and the application of sUAS and AI to disaster response has ethical and legal ramifications for pilots and data managers.

OpenEarth-OVS44: A Multimodal Remote Sensing Dataset for Open-Vocabulary Segmentation
Authors: Yimin Wei, Hongruixuan Chen, Hao Zheng, Hao Liu, Jian Song, Yuting Zhu, Junshi Xia, Naoto Yokoya
First: 2026-10-21T12:25:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T12:25:26+00:00

Abstract
In remote sensing, open-vocabulary segmentation is particularly important for flexible and scalable land-cover understanding across diverse regions, as it improves generalization to novel categories without requiring exhaustive pixel-level annotations. Despite its great promise, progress in this area remains largely constrained by existing datasets with limited category coverage and overly coarse taxonomies, and therefore struggles to support fine-grained semantic understanding required in real-world applications such as urban functional zoning, carbon accounting, and post-disaster assessment. To address these challenges, we present OpenEarth-OVS44, a multimodal benchmark with 290,587 expert-verified segments from 2,832 RGB images and 1,341 strictly registered RGB-SAR pairs at sub-meter spatial resolution, covering 40 cities across France, Japan, and the United States under a unified 44-class taxonomy organized into 9 base categories. Three evaluation tracks are provided: an RGB-only fine-grained benchmark, a multimodal track with diverse synthetic cloudy conditions for weather-robust evaluation, and a held-out cross-region track. Extensive experiments on representative open-vocabulary segmentation models demonstrate that training on OpenEarth-OVS44 substantially improves fine-grained segmentation performance over the strongest training-free baseline, with improvements of up to 7.1 mIoU points on unseen categories and 27.6 mIoU points on seen categories. The dataset and code will be released.

Authors: Michael Glöckler, Timo Ropinski
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-19T12:13:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T11:27:48+00:00
Comments: Accepted at WACV 2027. 24 pages, including references and supplementary material. Code and checkpoint: https://github.com/michaelgloeckler/XGLASS
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) achieve remarkable performance on 2D medical images, such as X-rays or pathology images, while 3D VLMs that directly process CT/MRI volumes lag behind due to limited 3D training data and the predominance of 2D foundation architectures. To leverage powerful 2D VLMs for analyzing medical volumes, which contain hundreds of slices, it is therefore necessary to focus only on those few slices, which are diagnostically relevant. To enable such a prioritization, we propose XGLASS, a method that transfers medical expertise from 2D X-rays to 3D CT for task-aware slice prioritization. XGLASS uses disease-conditioned importance maps derived from language-aligned radiologist gaze on X-rays and spatially aligns them with coronal CT projections, bringing gaze priors and CT content into a shared 2D representation. The fused gaze-prior/CT projection is then used to predict a disease-specific ordering of axial slices, prioritizing diagnostically relevant slices. XGLASS significantly improves budgeted retrieval over 2D and 3D-native baselines, achieving a top-10 hit rate up to 75.9% for the best-performing disease. In practice, this means that after inspecting only the top 10 slices (4.17% of a 240-slice scan), XGLASS identifies a diagnostically relevant slice in more than 75% of cases. For downstream slice-wise finding classification, an XGLASS-selected 30-slice candidate set reduces model calls by 87.5% while improving F1 by 11.3 percentage points over all-slice inference, 4.2 points over the strongest 2D selector, and remains competitive with 3D-native selectors while requiring up to 130.8 times less time and 10.9 times less GPU memory per finding, supporting its use as a lightweight preselector for volumetric CT analysis. The code and pretrained checkpoint are available on https://github.com/michaelgloeckler/XGLASS.

What Transfers from Text to Vision? Capability Scaling Laws and Transfer Dynamics for VLMs
Authors: Ziran Li, Qiang Wang, Zhengyu Chen, Shanglin Lei, Borun Chen, Jingang Wang, Xunliang Cai
First: 2026-06-24T13:23:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T09:33:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Choosing the right large language model (LLM) backbone is the most consequential decision when building a vision-language model (VLM), yet it remains fundamentally unprincipled: compute-based scaling laws fail to generalize across model families, and no framework exists for directly predicting VLM performance before training begins. We propose the Capability-Driven Multimodal Scaling Law, the first cross-family framework that predicts VLM benchmark accuracy from directly observable textual capability. Given a low-dimensional capability score $S$ extracted from LLM textual benchmarks via PCA, we model VLM performance as a function of $S$, with a per-backbone transfer rate and an absorption rate that quantifies data-scaling efficiency. To fit and validate the framework, we train over 150 VLMs on 34 LLMs spanning 7 model families under a strictly controlled recipe. Evaluations on more than 200 textual and 50 multimodal benchmarks show that the law accurately extrapolates transfer rate from models up to 8B parameters to 72B-scale backbones, predicts full VLM training trajectories with high fidelity, and generalizes to entirely held-out model families. Beyond the scaling law, our analysis surfaces actionable insights: certain textual benchmarks negatively correlate with multimodal performance, exposing latent benchmark-gaming behavior; base LLMs outperform instruction-tuned counterparts as VLM backbones due to higher absorption rates and lower data-scaling decay; and different model families occupy distinct positions in the transfer--absorption space. The framework turns backbone selection from costly empirical sweeps into a principled, quantitative decision. Code and data are available at https://github.com/wangq-dev/CDMScaling.

CAVE: A Structured Credit Assignment Approach for Fragmented Visual Evidence Reasoning
Authors: Tengda Guo, Jie Leng, Hanlei Li, Yaoyuan Liang, Qingyue Zhang, Dian Yang, Mingyu Zhang, Yuhua Fu, Shao-Lun Huang
First: 2026-05-13T16:50:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T09:09:33+00:00

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved strong performance on general multimodal reasoning, yet remain challenged in integrating nonlocal visual information to support semantically underdetermined visual reasoning. We describe this challenge as Fragmented Visual Reasoning. To this end, we propose Credit Assignment for Visual Evidence (CAVE), a structured process-reward method based on GRPO for interleaved visual reasoning. Specifically, CAVE evaluates the contribution of intermediate steps at the action level via three complementary reasoning process signals: belief update, evidence acquisition, and adaptive focus control, thereby guiding the model to optimize each reasoning action and learn more reliable visual reasoning strategies. Meanwhile, we construct TRACER-Bench, which covers four nonlocal and semantically confusable reasoning dimensions and provides key intermediate evidence to supervise reasoning paths. Experiments demonstrate that CAVE substantially improves performance on tasks requiring fragmented visual evidence integration, covering both public benchmarks and our newly introduced TRACER-Bench, while retaining competitive performance on general multimodal evaluations. Further analyses reveal that CAVE effectively improves the visual reasoning capacity and exhibits stronger robustness under longer-range and deeper cross-region dependencies.

Asking What a Director Sees Can Change What Vision-Language Models Choose
Authors: Jiaxuan Guo, Xingyu Ren, Kejia Zhang, Jingxin Yang, Youran Sun, Haizhao Yang
First: 2026-10-21T07:21:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T07:21:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models are now routinely tested on theory of mind: whether they can work out what another person sees, knows, and means. A common design asks the model to report that perspective and then to act for the person, and reads a correct report beside a wrong action as a failure to use the perspective. That reading rests on a quiet assumption: that asking for the report leaves the action unchanged. We show that it does not. In a rendered Director Task, a partition hides the largest block from a director who asks for "the largest block". Asking first which blocks the director can see raises the share of replies that choose this hidden block by 12 to 88 percentage points on six GPT models and by 17 on Qwen3.8-27B, even though the models mostly report correctly which blocks the director sees. Reasoning traces and prompt rewordings suggest why: the question shifts how the request is read, from the director's view toward the model's own. When the prompt states what the director knows and refers to, fewer than 1% of replies pair a correct report with a hidden-block choice. Any evaluation that elicits a perspective report before the action it scores needs a baseline without the question, and we deliver one: paired prompts that differ only in that question, a design any perspective-taking benchmark can adopt.

Abstract
Thermal cameras enable robots to perceive human activities in darkness while revealing less personally identifiable appearance information and fewer details of the surrounding environment than RGB cameras. As vision-language models (VLMs), originally developed for RGB imagery, are increasingly integrated into embodied systems for scene understanding and high-level planning, thermal sensing offers a promising way to extend activity perception to low-light and privacy-sensitive settings. However, the failure modes of VLMs in thermal human activity understanding for human-robot interaction (HRI) remain underexplored. We fill this gap by evaluating seven locally deployable VLMs, including models developed for physical AI and robotics, on two person-centric thermal datasets. Our diagnostic tasks examine fine-grained action recognition, activity timeline reconstruction, and the ability to withhold unsupported answers. Paired RGB recordings serve as a performance reference, while controlled variations in frame sampling, thermal input presentation, and reasoning are used to assess whether the observed errors can be mitigated. Our results show that models can recognize broad activity categories but struggle with fine-grained actions such as coughing, sneezing, and yawning. They also produce inaccurate event timelines and hallucinate actions involving objects. Simple thermal input preprocessing and additional reasoning offer only limited, model-dependent gains. Abstention behavior also varies across models even under explicit instructions, limiting their reliability for practical robotic use.

Encoded but Unused: Bounding the Sources of VLM Errors
Authors: Anat Veikherman, Mor Ventura, Nitay Calderon, Lihi Zelnik-Manor, Roi Reichart
First: 2026-10-21T05:47:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T05:47:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In this paper, we investigate why vision-language models (VLMs) produce errors by decomposing them into two sources: visual-representation failures, in which the visual evidence required for the correct answer is not encoded, and visual-utilization failures, in which this evidence is encoded but not successfully used. We propose to estimate the visual-utilization failure rate using lightweight probes and derive theoretical bounds on the true rate that account for probe imperfections. We introduce VELens (Visual Evidence Lens), a rank-based probe that traces visual evidence for both a target object and its ground-truth attribute, such as color, material, or shape, across VLM layers. We evaluate VELens on over 6,000 images using LLaVA-1.6-7B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B. Our method achieves ROC-AUC scores of 0.95 and 0.91, respectively, and outperforms alternative probes in both low-resource and unseen-attribute settings. Using it to diagnose errors, we estimate that visual-utilization failures account for 84.4% of LLaVA errors and 70.0% of Qwen errors, with conservative lower bounds of 77.5% and 61.0%, respectively. Overall, our study identifies visual utilization as the dominant bottleneck in VLMs and suggests that a central opportunity for improvement lies in developing better mechanisms to locate, route, and use the visual information they already encode.

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) excel at 2D recognition but often fail to maintain consistent 3D relations across sparse viewpoints, leading to spatial hallucinations under occlusion and perspective changes. Existing methods commonly fuse all frames with dense geometry, incurring substantial computation and context overhead while encouraging blind reliance on imperfect estimators. We present Think-Capture, a query-conditioned framework that first identifies task-critical objects and then acquires their geometry on demand. A 3D toolkit lifts zero-shot masks into a common coordinate frame, and a Symbolic 3D State Abstraction compresses object and camera states into structured text. It also remains robust under coordinate and mask perturbations. The full stack fits one 24 GB GPU and serves as an asynchronous high-level reasoner on an Agibot G1. Our code is available at https://github.com/819346675/Think-Capture.

DisasterRoute: Evidence-Guided Dual-System Routing for Visual Disaster Question Answering
Authors: Tan Zhang, Xing Deng, Lu Zhang, Xiaofeng Zhu, Ping Hu
First: 2026-10-21T05:41:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-21T05:41:44+00:00

Abstract
Visual question answering from low-altitude UAV imagery must deliver timely and accurate judgments to support disaster response under limited edge computing resources. Routine questions may be answered through direct decisions, whereas more challenging questions may benefit from step-by-step reasoning. However, existing research primarily focuses on improving models' visual understanding and generative reasoning capabilities, with limited exploration of efficient direct decision-making and its adaptive coordination with generative reasoning. We develop Disaster-S1 based on a vision-language model adapted to the disaster domain. Trained for option discrimination, it outputs a probability distribution over answer options in a single multimodal forward pass for fast decision-making. We further propose DisasterRoute, a dual-system routing framework that combines prediction confidence with disaster evidence to decide whether to accept the initial answer or invoke the disaster reasoning model DisasterVL for review. By allocating inference computation on demand, the framework retains the efficiency of direct decision-making and improves overall question-answering accuracy through selective review. Experiments on DisasterBench show that Disaster-S1 achieves superior accuracy with low latency, while DisasterRoute further improves accuracy through selective review at substantially lower latency than full reasoning.

Abstract
Recent advancements in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) enable end-to-end visual grounding via language prompts and generative decoding, demonstrating largely improved zero-shot and open-vocabulary detection capabilities. As a result, VLMs are rapidly being deployed at the edge in safety-critical real-world systems. However, the physical adversarial robustness of edge VLMs has not been systematically studied, and the fundamental differences in output with modern object detectors create methodological challenges for fair evaluation. To compare the two families directly, we evaluate every selected representative model on one outcome: whether an adversarial patch worn on a person evades detection of the model. We report this by introducing a unified metric, conditional attack success rate (cASR), counted only on images the model still localizes under a matched gray occluder, and partition every failure into omission (no box), substitution (a box on the patch), and displacement (a box on neither). We benchmark the physical adversarial vulnerability of compact grounding VLMs against a range of object detectors, and find that the VLMs can be as vulnerable as the detectors. We disclose that failure mode is largely associated with the output format of a model rather than its architecture family. Of the three, only omission is visible to a downstream system; displacement and substitution return confident, well-formed boxes, making them potentially more dangerous failures. Further, we find that, under distribution shift, VLMs that primarily failed by omission instead shift to failing more by displacement/substitution. Thus, a grounding VLM can inherit the detector's vulnerability while showing the possibility to adopt more dangerous failure modes. Our code and data are available at https://github.com/ericliang1/physical-adversarial-robustness.

Resuming Vision-Language Inference across Onboard Interruptions
Authors: Xiaolong Huang, Yinyuan Zhang, Daliang Xu, Yun Ma, Qing Li, Mengwei Xu, Shangguang Wang, Gang Huang, Xuanzhe Liu

Abstract
Executing vision-language models (VLMs) aboard low-Earth orbit satellites enables flexible processing of observation data without first downlinking it to the ground. On commercial off-the-shelf payloads, however, a request may span multiple execution windows: power and thermal constraints, fault-triggered reboots, and competing payload demands can interrupt inference before it completes. Each shutdown discards volatile activations and KV caches, forcing an unprotected request to repeat work after restart. We present OrbitWeaver, a framework that preserves VLM inference progress across onboard interruptions through coordinated checkpointing and recovery. Its phase-aware checkpointing selects recovery boundaries according to the state lifetimes and computational characteristics of vision encoding, language-model prefill, and decoding, balancing storage traffic against recomputation. Its dependency-aware dynamic recovery schedules restoration from partially completed states across the layer and token-chunk dimensions. By overlapping checkpoint and weight reads with recomputation, it reduces restart latency; the recovery planner computes an optimal execution order under its scheduling model within 100 ms. Experiments on satellite payload hardware using on-orbit telemetry traces show up to a 4.07-fold end-to-end speedup and a 3.56-fold reduction in SSD write volume over the evaluated baselines. A 72-hour deployment on two low-Earth orbit satellites demonstrates that OrbitWeaver retains inference progress and resumes execution across repeated bus-enforced shutdowns.

Abstract
Estimating food mass from a photograph is central to image-based dietary assessment, but whether trained models transfer to new cameras and foods is unclear. We fine-tuned DINOv2-base on 2,253 weighed Nutrition5k dishes to predict mass from one RGB photograph and compared it with a zero-shot vision-language model, Qwen3-VL-32B, on Nutrition5k and three external datasets: ASA24 studio portions, ECUSTFD whole foods and Food Portion Benchmark meal trays.
