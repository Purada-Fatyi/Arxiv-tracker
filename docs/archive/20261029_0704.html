Corrupted but Correct: Why Vision-Language Models Lie to Themselves Internally
Authors: Arun Josephraj Arokiaraj, Chenyi Huang, Zekun Wu, Adriano Koshiyama
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-02T15:27:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:55:45+00:00
Comments: Accepted at the VLM4RWD Workshop (Grounded and Faithful Vision-Language Models for Real-World Deployment), NeurIPS 2026. 8 pages, 2 figures, 3 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A targeted adversarial perturbation can drive a vision-language model's (VLM's) teacher-forced training loss for a fixed target caption to near zero, yet the same model, allowed to generate freely, produces the original, correct description with no trace of the target. We call this dissociation the train/inference gap, and give it a precise mechanistic account on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct using a controlled two-stage PGD attack on 200 held-out COCO images. First, we show that image-level pixel statistics, including a correctly re-implemented, texture-based attackability measure from the CNN robustness literature, have essentially no predictive power over which images are corrupted (best predictor r=-0.050, p=0.484; ridge regression R^2=0.069). Second, using the logit lens, we localise the gap to a single autoregressive step: the rank of the target token, conditioned on the correct first token already being generated, is fixed at exactly 3,488 out of 152,064 vocabulary entries for every image and every condition, with zero variance. Third, tracking target-token rank across all 28 LLM decoder layers reveals that the visual encoder corrupts every image's representation by a comparable margin regardless of eventual outcome, but the language model decoder then differentially arbitrates: amplifying the corrupted signal for susceptible images and actively suppressing it, past its clean-image baseline, for resistant ones (p<0.001, rank-biserial r=0.579). A linear probe on the merger hidden state separates these two outcomes with AUC=0.858, though we flag a circularity concern in this estimate. Together these results argue that adversarial robustness in autoregressive VLMs is substantially a property of the language decoder's prior, not the visual encoder, with direct implications for where faithfulness evaluations and defenses for deployed VLM systems should be targeted.

Perceiving and Predicting Dynamic Sports: Spatiotemporal Intelligence in Vision Models for Egocentric Tennis Video
Authors: Tudor Braicu, Joseph DelPreto, Jared Boyer, Mónika Farsang, Maximilian Stölzle, Tim Niklas Seyde, Daniela Rus
First: 2026-10-27T17:47:14+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T17:47:14+00:00
Comments: 8 pages, 5 figures. Project page: https://tennis-vision.csail.mit.edu/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Agile robots require spatiotemporal intelligence to interpret scenes, reason about interactions, and anticipate future events. Yet it remains unclear how well foundation vision models support these capabilities, particularly from a moving egocentric camera, which is a natural view for robots. To investigate this, we collect an annotated tennis dataset and train a suite of vision models on spatiotemporal tasks. Our dataset contains egocentric video synchronized with five allocentric videos, estimated global positions, labeled contact times, and annotated stroke types. We then evaluate seven prediction tasks from egocentric images: 2D ego player and 3D ball localization, future player and ball trajectories, forehand or backhand stroke anticipation, time to the next ego player ball contact, and 3D ball position at that contact. We compare image encoders, video encoders, and vision-language models with jointly trained prediction heads. The best performing models achieve mean current-frame localization errors of 0.14 m for the ego player and 0.54 m for the ball, future forecasting errors of 0.34 m for the ego player and 0.91 m for the ball over 1.1 s, and a mean contact-time prediction error of 0.14 s. Auxiliary depth information substantially improves 3D ball localization, suggesting that inferring metric depth remains a challenge. Longer image histories do not improve ball forecasting, indicating that representations exploit scene context rather than rely solely on inter-frame motion. Finally, we propose a conditional diffusion model to generate whole-body kinematic tennis motion from vision encoder predictions on egocentric video at real-time rates, adapting movements as predictions are refined. These results move towards a deeper understanding of how foundation vision models can support real-time dynamic robots using egocentric video.

Which Hand Was Late? Diagnosis for Robot Failure Recovery in Bimanual Tool Use
Authors: Baptiste Bonin, Caro Strickland, Audrey Durand
First: 2026-10-27T16:53:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T16:53:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Failure diagnosers for robot manipulation are increasingly vision--language models (VLMs), trained and evaluated on single-arm failures produced by perturbing a successful demonstration. Bimanual tool use adds failures of other kinds: a tool that slips out of its socket while still held, two correct gestures made at different times, and attempts that fail for two reasons at once. On a simulated bimanual tool-use task whose failure causes are read from the recorded trace, we measure what each sensing channel lets a diagnoser tell. The tool's slip is read on the tool hand; which hand was late is read on both arms, while a sensor on one hand reads its own hand's delay and at most part of the other's; and whether a late attempt was also pushed too weakly is not identifiable from passive sensing when the tool hand is late. Within two attempts, a repair policy that escalates when the cause is not identifiable repairs as many failures as one told the true cause. A VLM's internal state carries the late hand almost perfectly, along a direction no closer to the one its answer reads than a random one; whether the answer reports it depends on the wording of the question.

Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without observing real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. Furthermore, ZAP also generalizes to an unseen embodiment as well as new objects, goals and strategies never seen during training. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

DRIFT: A Residual Flow Adapter for Decoding Continuous Outputs in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Zhuoming Liu, Jinhong Lin, Kwan Man Cheng, Lin Zhang, Shayok Bagchi, Yin Li
First: 2026-06-04T06:37:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T15:50:15+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many modern vision-language models (VLMs) build on autoregressive decoding of discrete tokens. While text-based output interfaces enable scalable pretraining and strong zero-shot generalization across diverse tasks, they are poorly suited for problems that require precise continuous outputs, such as localizing temporal boundaries of events or generating robotic control actions. To address this challenge, we propose DRIFT, a general framework for adapting pretrained VLMs to continuous decoding tasks. DRIFT combines a base predictor, which provides a coarse estimate of the target output, with a generative refinement module based on flow matching that iteratively improves the prediction. This residual formulation transforms the generative modeling problem from learning a global output distribution to modeling a localized residual distribution around a strong prior, substantially simplifying optimization. We evaluate DRIFT on both perception and planning tasks, including visual grounding and robotic control. Across multiple tasks and architectures spanning MLLMs, VLAs, and WAMs, DRIFT consistently outperforms a strong set of regression- and generative-based solutions.

TimeLineXR: An Interactive Exploration of Time in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Nidham Tekaya, Markus Passecker, Matthias Zeppelzauer
First: 2026-10-27T14:50:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:50:59+00:00

Abstract
Recently, it has been shown that vision-language models (VLMs) have a certain intrinsic understanding of time. They can estimate the temporal origin of objects and encode time along a low-dimensional, non-linear manifold inside their embedding space~\cite{tekaya2025matter}. That result is quantitative and reproducible, but the structure itself stays out of reach for end users: they cannot see it, query it with their own images, or inspect where a query lands relative to other years on the time axis. We present TimeLineXR, an interactive demonstration that turns this time axis into an explorable 3D scene. A web demo lets users upload or select images and see each placed on a color-coded 3D time axis; a Three.js WebXR scene renders the same time axis as a room-scale representation that can be grabbed, scaled, inspected, and queried by throwing image cards into it. Both clients run the same prediction pipeline against the same cached temporal index: encode the query with CLIP, project it through cosine-kernel KPCA, and snap it to the Bézier time axis. The system turns each image into a point on the time axis, so users can compare where different objects land and read off their estimated first year of appearance.Code source and reproduction steps can be found in: https://github.com/sterngefeuert/timeline-xr.

SQuID: A Benchmark for Quantitative Spatial Reasoning on Satellite Imagery
Authors: Peter A. Massih, Eric Cosatto, Martin Renqiang Min

First: 2026-01-19T21:14:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T14:45:28+00:00

Introduces the SQuID benchmark and QASR baseline for quantitative spatial reasoning on satellite imagery. Dataset DOI: 10.57967/hf/7565. Code and data are publicly available
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) describe satellite images fluently but struggle with the quantitative spatial reasoning that remote sensing (RS) analysts perform routinely, such as counting buildings, measuring forest area in hectares and computing distances in meters. We introduce SQuID (Satellite Quantitative Intelligence Dataset), a benchmark of 2,000 questions over 587 images from four sources at 0.3 to 1.0 m ground sampling distance, covering 24 question types in three difficulty tiers. Answers are computed from curated segmentation masks or, for 50 questions, from human consensus, and numeric answers are scored against tolerance ranges informed by human annotations. SQuID also releases the reference masks behind its answers, so that modular systems can be evaluated using reference rather than predicted masks, and 75,687 training question-answer pairs on disjoint images (SQuID-Train). Among 15 evaluated systems, the best zero-shot end-to-end VLM reaches 28.1%, and RS-specialized VLMs score lower. As a baseline, we propose QASR (Quantitative Agentic Spatial Reasoning), in which an LLM writes a program that segments the image and measures the masks with geometric operators. QASR reaches 42.0%. On the 1,950 mask-derived questions, running its programs on the reference masks instead of predicted masks raises accuracy from 42.2% to 82.6%, so segmentation is its main source of error. Fine-tuning Qwen2.5-VL-7B on SQuID-Train raises its accuracy from 16.1% to 40.7%, close to QASR. Multi-condition questions (Tier 3) remain the hardest in every setting, at 62.0% for QASR with reference masks and 13.2% after fine-tuning. These results leave clear room for end-to-end models that learn metric spatial reasoning, and SQuID provides the data to train and evaluate them.

Vision-Language-Action or World-Action? Just Action Models
Authors: Yonghao Yu, Lang Huang, Runyi Li, Toshihiko Yamasaki
First: 2026-10-27T13:29:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T13:29:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
The vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action model (WAM) communities debate whether control policies should inherit pretrained knowledge from vision-language models or from visual generators. The debate, however, bundles three separable choices: backbone initialization, the objective applied to future observations, and whether the machinery for that future loss is kept at inference. A gap between a VLA and a WAM therefore cannot simply be attributed to any one of them. We show that decoupling these choices yields a common framework for both families, which we term Just Action Model (JAM): a pretrained visual backbone with an action expert, trained with an action loss and a foresight loss. The foresight loss trains current-observation features to predict frozen encodings of the recorded future through a prediction head discarded after training, so no visual generation is involved in either training or inference, and the backbone can be chosen freely. We first compare objectives within fixed policy architectures, then evaluate the foresight recipe across backbone families. The resulting JAM policies outperform their matched FastWAM and ImageWAM counterparts on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, and achieve similar or slightly higher success rates on RoboTwin. Compared with FastWAM, JAM improves the success rates from 97.0%/55.3%/91.2% to 98.2%/64.5%/91.4%, respectively. With cached foresight targets, JAM reduces estimated online training FLOPs per sample per update by 73% relative to FastWAM. Our results show that future supervision improves control without visual generation or additional inference machinery, and strong foresight performance is attainable with either generator-initialized or vision-language backbones. Abstract
Interpretable Transformers often perform well in domain specific settings but lose effectiveness beyond that. Achieving ViT-like broad applicability requires models with consistent performance across domains. Recent works on Interpretable Transformers, INTR and Prompt-CAM, were developed keeping domain adaptability in mind. Prompt-CAM leverages learnable prompts to promote adaptability. However, these prompts are fixed in nature and therefore struggle to adapt to domains with substantial variation in input image characteristics, resulting in reduced interpretability. To address this limitation, we propose INTR++, which extends Prompt-CAM by incorporating a lightweight Prompt Adapter Module (PAM). PAM enables prompts to be adaptive by enriching them with domain-specific knowledge, thereby ensuring consistent interpretability across diverse domains including those with varying image characteristics such as medical imaging, micro-scopic imaging, and remote sensing imagery. INTR++ retains the standard architecture and end-to-end learning of Interpretable Transformer based models, while adding only a small number of additional parameters compared to Prompt-CAM. We demonstrate that INTR++ delivers consistent improvements in all aspects across domains like animals, plants, medical imaging, microscopic imaging, remote sensing imagery and object-centric data.

CLEAR: Coupling Local Evidence with Affective Representations for Multimodal Emotion Recognition in Conversation
Authors: Linan Zhu, Zihao Zhai, Mengfei Xu, Xiao Han, Yuqian Fu, Xiangfan Chen, Zihang Zhang
First: 2026-05-18T15:27:10+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T12:22:55+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
In video calls and livestreamed discussions, recognizing speakers' emotions helps identify emotionally salient segments and supports fine-grained video content analysis. Multimodal Emotion Recognition in Conversation (ERC) provides an approach to recognizing emotions in such content by integrating textual, visual, and acoustic information with conversational context. Current mainstream emotion recognition methods largely leverage the contextual understanding capabilities of multimodal large language models (MLLMs) to predict emotions by generating labels. However, emotion understanding and label prediction may be inconsistent, leaving the internal affective information underutilized. Moreover, when the current utterance is jointly modeled with extensive dialogue history and multimodal information, its local discriminative cues may be diluted and become less salient in the resulting contextual representation. To address these limitations, we propose CLEAR, an ERC framework that couples multimodal contextual representations with local discriminative cues. To mitigate the mismatch between emotion understanding and label prediction, we use the continuous internal representation of an emotion-supervised vision-language model (VLM) instead of its generated emotion labels, and further train a classifier based on this representation. To strengthen local discriminative cues, we use sample-adaptive residual coupling to dynamically integrate textual and acoustic evidence from the target utterance into the contextual representation. Experiments on IEMOCAP and MELD demonstrate that CLEAR achieves competitive performance, supporting the effectiveness of the proposed approach. The code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/vlm-210F/.

Abstract
Existing referring segmentation methods on Gaussian scenes primarily target whole objects, relying on segmenter-generated candidates from which a vision-language model (VLM) selects. Such predetermined fixed candidates may omit fine-grained parts needed as targets or cues for identifying objects. We introduce SplatScout, a query-driven, closed-loop approach that places perception under VLM control. The model actively selects views, directs segmentation, and inspects rendered 3D results to refine its predictions. For part-level evaluation, we introduce PartRef-ScanNet++, comprising 261 manually verified queries across 12 ScanNet++ scenes with mesh-derived ground truth. Ablations demonstrate the benefits of iterative refinement, while comparisons across VLM backbones highlight the importance of effective tool-feedback utilization. SplatScout also achieves higher scores on four existing referring benchmarks. Code and the PartRef-ScanNet++ benchmark are available at https://github.com/kaust-violab/SplatScout.

Abstract
Visual perception is evolving from passive recognition toward active, task-oriented observation. We investigate GUI grounding as a scalable source of spatial supervision for learning observation behaviors that transfer beyond interfaces. DIP-CAD: A Design-Intent-Preserving Agent Harness for Parametric CAD Modeling
Authors: Xi Cheng, Chenxi Zhai, Hang Cheng, Mingyu Fan, Pingfa Feng, Long Zeng
First: 2026-09-19T01:45:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T11:52:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Parametric CAD modeling requires not only matching the target shape, but also preserving design intent under parameter edits. However, most existing methods focus on improving static shape generation accuracy, while the dependencies that govern later edits are often overlooked. To tackle this issue, we present DIP-CAD, a design-intent-preserving agent harness for generating parametric CAD models from images. DIP-CAD combines vision-language model reasoning with CAD kernel operations to establish dependencies that encode the design intent. In parametric CAD, these dependencies are mainly established through geometry references in modeling commands and constraints in parametric sketches. At the command level, ID-based explicit geometry reference binds modeling commands to selected faces, edges, or vertices. At the sketch level, a local-coordinate bridge ensures accurate sketch placement, providing the basis for correctly establishing sketch constraints. Constraints among sketch primitives and constraints to external projected geometry further preserve both internal sketch relationships and dependencies on existing geometry. To provide a more comprehensive data foundation for parametric CAD modeling tasks, we introduce the Geometry Explicit Reference Dataset (GERD), whose structured data format links stepwise modeling commands to intermediate CAD models through explicit geometry references. On GERD-EVL and a DeepCAD test subset, DIP-CAD improves mIoU for static model generation by 11.1% and 5.6%, respectively. In parameter-editing evaluations, the corresponding mIoU improvements are 7.0% and 4.2%. Case studies further demonstrate how parameter edits drive shape updates while preserving design intent.

V-LUMEN: Visual Lookup Memory for Embedding Scaling in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Miso Choi, Daekeun Kim, Eunji Kim, Jungbeom Lee
Venue: NeurIPS 2026

Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026

Abstract
Embedding-level scaling expands the capacity of language models through lookup-based memory rather than by increasing model depth, width, or dense computation. Extending this idea to vision-language models (VLMs) is challenging because visual features are continuous, spatially structured, and query-dependent, unlike discrete text tokens that naturally support n-gram lookup. We introduce V-LUMEN, a visual lookup memory for VLMs. V-LUMEN discretizes visual features with a frozen codebook, builds spatially aware memory keys through spatial aggregation-based hashing, and modulates them with text-conditioned relevance signals; all addressing is non-parametric, and the retrieved embeddings are fused into the backbone through a gated residual pathway. On five reasoning-intensive benchmarks, V-LUMEN substantially improves a frozen Qwen3-VL-2B backbone and transfers zero-shot to three benchmarks whose images are disjoint from the training data. At a matched parameter budget, moving part of a LoRA or MoE adapter's capacity into V-LUMEN preserves accuracy on two backbones, indicating that external visual memory is an independent capacity axis rather than a stronger adaptation method. Because lookup cost is constant in table size and incurred only once at prefill, this capacity grows in storage rather than in per-token compute. Code is available at https://github.com/miso-choi/V-Lumen.

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have demonstrated remarkable multimodal capabilities but suffer from prohibitive computational overhead due to the massive number of visual tokens. While token pruning is a promising solution, existing methods still suffer from two major drawbacks: 1) unreliable pruning criteria that fail to accurately localize task-specific regions due to inherent attention biases or query-agnostic designs, and 2) suboptimal pruning strategies that employ rigid, non-adaptive schedules, overlooking the dynamic evolution of token redundancy across different model depths. To address these challenges, we propose LearnPrunerV2, which extends the previous two-stage heuristic framework (LearnPruner) into a fully learnable architecture. Specifically, we integrate lightweight learnable modules into every layer of the VLM backbone, which estimate token importance by fusing intrinsic visual saliency with extrinsic query relevance captured via a shadow token. By uncovering a consistent staircase redundancy pattern across VLM layers, we further introduce Budget-Deterministic Pruning (BDP) to establish a hardware-friendly schedule with fixed per-layer budgets. Extensive experimental results across various benchmarks and VLMs demonstrate that LearnPrunerV2 consistently outperform previous state-of-the-art methods. Notably, LearnPrunerV2 preserves 99.8% of the original performance when pruning 77.8% of visual tokens on LLaVA-v1.5, achieving a superior accuracy-efficiency trade-off.

Abstract
End-to-end video quality assessment (VQA) methods leave the regional evidence underlying their predictions largely implicit. We introduce an explicit pixel-level regional representation for VQA, instantiated as a Quality-Relevance Heatmap (QRH) that combines localized degradation evidence with perceptual priors under vision-language model guidance, without requiring pixel-level annotations. These results support explicit regional representations as a useful interface for downstream VQA.

Vision-Language Agents Can Learn in Their Own Latent Worlds
Authors: Haoxi Li, Sikai Bai, Jianfei Ma, Qinglin Hou, Xiaocheng Lu, Tao Han, Jie Zhang, Song Guo

Abstract
Learning from experience is essential for building the next generation of agents that continually expand their capabilities. Pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) already internalize rich world knowledge, yet this knowledge is rarely leveraged to drive policy improvement through online interaction. Realizing this potential efficiently remains challenging, as policy updates shift the agent's interaction distribution, requiring the world model to adapt accordingly, while continually updating large pretrained models is computationally expensive. To address this challenge, we introduce Dwell, a model-based reinforcement learning (RL) framework that reframes a VLM as a recurrent latent transition operator, endowing it with the ability to predict, adjust, and act in latent space. Leveraging the VLM's own representations and its dual role as both world model and policy, the agent continually learns from real interactions to anticipate and evaluate action outcomes, and from latent imagination to improve its behavior. Across five interactive visual environments, Dwell achieves the strongest overall performance among trainable methods while substantially reducing the compute required for imagination, showing that effective world models for VLM agents need not reconstruct the future; they need to predict futures that are useful for action. Abstract
Mobile GUI agents are typically fine-tuned 7B-scale vision-language models (VLMs) that generate each action as text. However, these VLMs decode every action token by token, and even agents that score candidate actions keep a large language model or VLM in the loop, making them slow and memory-hungry. To address these issues, we propose GUI-JEPA, to our knowledge the first decoder-free GUI agent policy built on a Joint-Embedding Predictive Architecture (JEPA), which selects actions in the latent space of a JEPA encoder pretrained on unlabelled GUI screenshots. Its action- and region-conditioned latent predictor turns the screen latent into one latent per candidate action, letting lightweight heads score all candidates against the instruction in a single batched pass, without decoding a token. We further show that the scorer relies on this latent pathway and that the predictor is trained for the decision itself: a randomly initialised predictor does just as well as one pretrained on screen dynamics. On AndroidControl, GUI-JEPA is 2.8-4.0x faster on the same GPU than a Qwen2.5-VL-7B agent we fine-tune with the same inputs, while reaching 86-92% of its in-distribution accuracy with 6% of its parameters. Abstract

Abstract

Look Only When Needed: Certificate-Guided Visual Routing for Efficient Diffusion VLMs
Authors: Donghyeon Cho, Joowon Kim, Seungho Shin, Eunho Yang
First: 2026-10-27T05:43:30+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-27T05:43:30+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Diffusion vision-language models (DVLMs) process their entire input at every denoising step. Since visual tokens make up 89-97% of this input on the benchmarks we study, most of the decoding computation is spent repeatedly processing the same image. Visual token pruning methods partially relieve this burden by removing part of the visual tokens, but they lose the removed information and still process the remaining image at every step. To measure how often the image is actually needed, we compare LLaDA-V's predictions with and without the image at every decoding step and find that removing the image changes a token being unmasked at only 22.8% of steps on average and at as few as 7.9%. We therefore propose LOWN, a decoding method that decides at every step whether to process the image and how many tokens to unmask. LOWN predicts the masked tokens without the image and processes the image only when this prediction disagrees with the last image-conditioned prediction. Agreement certifies the earlier prediction for reuse, and the number of certified tokens sets how many are unmasked in that step. We further observe that the need for the image differs across benchmarks, so LOWN's adaptive decision is needed rather than a fixed schedule. Without training or a tuned threshold, LOWN retains 99.6% of LLaDA-V's score on ten benchmarks at 35.3% of the decoding FLOPs on average. LOWN also generalizes across LLaDA-based DVLMs, composes orthogonally with visual token pruning, and runs under key-value caching for further efficiency gains. Code and project page are available at https://hyeon9698.github.io/LOWN/

Abstract
Final-answer accuracy reports whether a vision-language model was right, but not whether its answer was grounded in the image, whether its stated reasoning reflects that evidence, or what the answer cost to produce. Those are the properties that decide whether a system can be deployed. We present PRISM-BENCH, a benchmark of 1,000 expert-curated questions spanning five under-represented reasoning categories, in which every item carries an idealized reasoning trace written by one human annotator and validated by another. The traces support two diagnostics that accuracy cannot provide. A perception probe, in which the model answers from a description it wrote itself, attributes roughly 85% of frontier-model failures to misperception and places the bottleneck at grounding. Trace alignment predicts answer correctness for all 25 systems tested (macro AUROC 0.66) and survives length control, so it measures reasoning content and not verbosity. We further introduce CORE, an efficiency-aware metric that reorders 24.2% of model pairs relative to accuracy while remaining uncorrelated with response length. Across 25 open and closed systems, including GPT-5 and Claude Opus 4.6, the best accuracy is 66.0%. On the four categories with a human baseline the best system trails humans by 18.4 points, and recent frontier gains fall on recognition and not on spatial grounding.

Abstract
Do vision-language models (VLMs) share our ability to communicate visually? We share preliminary findings, starting from a surprising observation: simple visual symbols like a red heart can strongly influence model behavior on seemingly unrelated tasks, such as steering models towards recursive solutions on simple coding problems. Notably, this steering effect is largely implicit, as models' direct interpretations and thinking traces about visual symbols diverge substantially from their observed behavior. In visual communication between models, this leads to a generation--interpretation gap: while models tend to send shape primitives that they (and humans) find intuitive, these visual cues often fail to steer other models. In further communication games involving abstract concepts and stories, models can creatively use visuals to transmit meaning to one another, although some communications remain non-intuitive to humans. These findings reveal that VLMs might be capable of rich, creative, and visually-grounded communication, but their interpretation and production of visual meaning diverges from human conventions in surprising ways. Together this work highlights both the promise and challenges of visual communication between humans and VLMs.

Abstract
Planning with world models offers a powerful paradigm for robotic control. Conventional approaches train a model to predict future frames conditioned on current frames and actions, which can then be used for planning. However, the objective of predicting future pixels is often at odds with the actual planning objective; strong pixel reconstruction does not always correlate with good planning decisions. This paper posits that instead of reconstructing future frames as pixels, world models only need to predict task-relevant semantic information about the future. For such prediction the paper poses world modeling as a visual question answering problem about semantic information in future frames. This perspective allows world modeling to be approached with the same tools underlying vision language models. Thus, vision-language models can be trained as "semantic" world models through a supervised finetuning process on image-action-text data, enabling planning for decision-making while inheriting many of the generalization and robustness properties from the pretrained vision-language models. In this paper, we introduce this new method and validate it on simulated robotic tasks leading to improvements over typical paradigms of reconstruction-based action-conditional world modeling.
