GUILD: Guided Iterative Data Collection for Imitation Learning
Authors: Shivin Dass, Kevin Nguyen, Rohan Yelandur, Junhong Xu, Roberto Martín-Martín
First: 2026-11-03T18:57:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T18:57:35+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Imitation learning policies in robotics are trained on datasets collected through costly real-world teleoperation. Often, the data-collection process is unguided, relying on the teleoperator's intuition, resulting in data that contributes little to overall policy performance. Recent work has shown that curating such unguided datasets can substantially improve policy performance, but these approaches operate by filtering existing datasets after collection and therefore do not prevent the acquisition of uninformative data. In this work, we propose GUILD, a novel Guided Data Collection (GDC) method that actively reasons about which data should be collected to maximize downstream policy performance. We introduce an iterative framework that alternates between 1) task proposal and data collection and 2) data evaluation to close the loop. For task proposal, we leverage semantic priors from vision-language models to identify relevant environments, object configurations, and goals for data collection. However, since semantic relevance does not imply policy improvement, we perform data evaluation. By leveraging predictive data attribution we approximate how the collected trajectories influence policy performance on a target deployment setting. This signal is then used as feedback to guide subsequent data collection, closing the data collection loop. We evaluate our approach in simulation and real robot settings, demonstrating that guided data collection substantially improves sample efficiency and deployment performance compared to passive data collection strategies, multiple baselines and experienced roboticists. Asynchronous Robot Policies
Authors: Sombit Dey, Luc Van Gool, Danda Pani Paudel
First: 2026-11-03T16:30:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T16:30:27+00:00
Comments: For code, checkpoints and more results, check out https://arp.insait.ai
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Scaling vision-language foundation models has significantly advanced generalist robot manipulation, but the increased model scale introduces computational latency that degrades real-time physical reactivity. Existing Vision-Language-Action (VLA) architectures execute high-level semantic reasoning and action ex- ecution sequentially within the primary control loop, creating a fundamental tension between semantic-reasoning capability and robot agility. To address this, we propose Asynchronous Robot Policy (ARP), an architectural framework that decouples semantic reasoning from low-level action generation using asynchronous processes operating at independent frequencies. In ARP, a high-capacity Vision-Language Model (VLM) asynchronously updates a semantic memory at low fre- quency, while a lightweight Vision-Action Expert, conditioned on this semantic memory, generates robot actions at a much higher control frequency. Such a formulation, enables having a fast perception-action loop without compromising on the semantic capability. To overcome asynchronous memory staleness, ARP trains the vision-action expert on the semantic staleness and execution delay, with temporally mismatched observation across processes. On Franka manipulation tasks, ARP achieves an 18% higher success rate and 20% less time need for execution in quasi-static setups, while 3-5 times baseline performance in dynamic simulation environments. Furthermore, ARP enables operating the VLM at 10 times lower than its nominal frequency with no significant performance loss making it much more efficient than the baseline. These results highlight the architectural advantages of modeling robot policies as asynchronous processes, enabling real-world reactive deployment without sacrificing the scale of foundation models.

Diffusion-DRF: Free, Rich, and Differentiable Reward for Video Diffusion Fine-Tuning
Authors: Yifan Wang, Yanyu Li, Gordon Guocheng Qian, Sergey Tulyakov, Yun Fu, Anil Kag
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-01-07T18:05:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T16:12:06+00:00
Comments: Published in NeurIPS 2026. Webpage: https://yfwang1999.github.io/Diffusion-DRF/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Video diffusion alignment has been heavily relied on scalar rewards. These rewards are typically derived from learned reward models in human preference datasets, requiring additional training and extensive collection. Moreover, scalar rewards provide coarse, global supervision, offering limited prompt-generation mismatch credit assignment and making models prone to reward exploitation and unstable optimization. We propose Diffusion-DRF, a free, rich, and differentiable reward framework for video diffusion fine-tuning. Diffusion-DRF employs a frozen, off-the-shelf Vision-Language Model (VLM) as the critic, eliminating the need for reward model training. Instead of relying on a single scalar reward, it decomposes each user prompt into multi-dimensional questions with freeform dense VQA explanation queries, yielding information-rich feedback. By direct differentiable optimization over this rich feedback, Diffusion-DRF achieves stable reward-based tuning without preference datasets collection. Diffusion-DRF achieves significant gains both quantitatively and qualitatively, outperforming state-of-the-art Flow-GRPO by 4.74% in overall performance on unseen VBench-2.0.

Cracks in the Foundation: A Civil Infrastructure Dataset to Challenge Vision Foundation Models
Authors: Nicola Farronato, Niccolo Avogaro, Thomas Frick, Mattia Rigotti, Rizwan Ullah Khan, Michele Magno, Konrad Schindler, Cristiano Malossi, Florian Scheidegger
First: 2026-05-18T13:51:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T15:44:56+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Automated structural health monitoring is essential to prevent catastrophic infrastructure failures. Precise, pixel-level defect segmentation is needed to accurately assess structural integrity, but progress in defect segmentation for civil infrastructures has been held back by an extreme scarcity of data, which requires costly expert annotation. The need for data is accentuated by algorithmic hurdles intrinsic to the problem, including center-bias and the need to rely more on shape when inspecting nearly textureless building materials. To remove the bottleneck, we introduce Cracks in the Foundation (CiF), the largest and most detailed civil infrastructure (instance) segmentation dataset to date, comprising $\approx$150,000 high-resolution tiles meticulously curated over five years in collaboration with civil engineering experts. With the help of this unprecedented data source, we expose a blind spot of current visual AI: despite the advent of promptable Foundation Models (FMs) and Vision Language Models (VLMs), and despite the impressive abilities of today's specialised segmentation models, it turns out that dense image understanding in the built environment is nowhere near solved. Our evaluations indicate that even the most recent zero-shot FMs face significant challenges when deployed on real-world infrastructure and even the performance of specialised models with domain-specific supervision plateaus at $\approx$25\% mAP. CiF establishes inspection of civil infrastructure, an elementary and seemingly easy perceptual task, as an open challenge that reveals fundamental weaknesses of present-day models trained predominantly on internet images, literally and figuratively highlighting cracks in the current foundation model paradigm. VIGOR: Visual Gain Ordering for Hallucination Mitigation in Multimodal Discrete Diffusion Language Models
Authors: Junzhe Chen, Tian Qin, Eugenie Shi, Tianshu Zhang, Qiang Ju, Lijie Wen
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-03T15:28:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T15:28:11+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Multimodal discrete diffusion language models (dLLMs) generate responses through iterative mask prediction and offer a promising alternative to autoregressive large vision-language models. However, they still suffer from hallucination, producing outputs that are plausible under language priors but insufficiently grounded in the image. We argue that hallucination in multimodal dLLMs is not only a token prediction problem, but also a premature commitment problem: during iterative denoising, some masked positions are unmasked too early because their confidence is driven more by language priors than by visual evidence, and these early commitments can bias later generation. To address this issue, we propose Visual Gain Ordering (VIGOR), a training-free and plug-and-play inference strategy that reorders masked positions according to counterfactual visual evidence. At each denoising step, VIGOR compares the confidence of each candidate token under the original image-conditioned pass and an additional visual-ablation pass, and prioritizes positions whose predictions are more strongly supported by the image. Experiments on LLaDA-V and Lumina-DiMOO show that VIGOR consistently reduces hallucination and improves multimodal reasoning performance across benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/THU-BPM/Vigor.

Learning Where to Look: Observation Policy Optimization for Thinking with Images
Authors: Junfeng Wang, Jiawei Liu, Yongchao Xu, Tao Jiang, Jiangbo Ai, Jin Zhang, Chong Wang, Qixing Zhang
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-03T14:10:16+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T14:10:16+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Project page: https://github.com/Yann0122/Observation-Policy-Optimization
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Complex visual question answering often requires vision-language models to think with images, i.e., actively acquire local visual evidence by zooming into informative regions of high-resolution images. Our error analysis shows that many failures in this setting originate from incorrect observation actions, where the model fails to localize key regions or obtain crops that contain sufficient evidence for answering. We further observe that bounding-box tokens exhibit substantially higher generation entropy than ordinary language tokens, indicating high uncertainty when the model decides where to look. However, existing reinforcement learning methods based on final-answer rewards provide only trajectory-level feedback, making it difficult to assign precise credit to the observation actions. We propose Observation Policy Optimization (OPO), an RL algorithm for optimizing observation actions in tool-augmented thinking with images. OPO consists of two core modules. First, Uncertainty-Guided Observation Branching (UOB) treats each image zoom-in tool call as an observation event and selectively branches at high-uncertainty, low-redundancy events during rollout, enabling efficient exploration of alternative cropping decisions and their resulting visual evidence. Second, Evidence-Localized Advantage Attribution (ELAA) compares sibling branches using local evidence-sufficiency rewards and assigns credit only to the corresponding observation-action tokens. By decoupling observation-action optimization from trajectory-level outcome supervision, OPO improves visual evidence acquisition and further enhances visual reasoning performance. Experiments show that OPO consistently improves Qwen3-VL models across scales and achieves competitive performance against larger models and tool-augmented baselines. Code is publicly available.

Gaze2HOI: Gaze-Aware Intention Disambiguation for Egocentric Hand-Object Interaction Forecasting
Authors: Chuanyang Ma, Chenye Wang, Yixuan Wang, Huiling Zhou, Man Zhang, Xingqun Qi, Muyi Sun, Caifeng Shan, Zhenan Sun
First: 2026-10-20T08:59:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T14:06:23+00:00
Comments: 22 pages, 8 figures. Project page: https://gaze2hoi.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Anticipating how users will interact with their surroundings is essential for proactive systems that provide timely assistance in extended reality and embodied applications. Prior work typically forecasts hand-object interactions directly from historical observations, leaving intention and contact geometry implicit. However, similar observation histories can precede semantically or geometrically distinct futures. Inspired by behavioral evidence that gaze can provide early cues for underlying intention, we propose Gaze2HOI, a gaze-aware framework that formulates hand-object interaction forecasting as a hierarchical problem comprising intention inference, contact grounding, and coupled hand-object motion generation. Specifically, Gaze2HOI first weights gaze cues by fixation strength and recency, and uses them to modulate the visual representations of a vision-language model (VLM) through Temporal Gaze Saliency Modulation (TGSM), thereby reducing semantic ambiguity in intention inference. Subsequently, an Anticipatory Contact Grounding (ACG) module maps these structured intentions into explicit contact, support, and placement fields, effectively mitigating geometric ambiguity. Driven by these explicit conditions, a diffusion-based model jointly generates the six-degree-of-freedom (6-DoF) object trajectories and future articulated motion of both hands. To evaluate Gaze2HOI, we further introduce EgoAIM, a phase-aware benchmark that organizes egocentric hand-object interactions into four phases defined by evolving hand-object relations and provides fine-grained intention annotations. Extensive experiments demonstrate that Gaze2HOI achieves state-of-the-art performance in both intention inference and motion forecasting, even approaching oracle-level end-to-end performance in specific interaction phases. Heads That Write, Not Just Point: Image Retrieval Heads in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Junsung Park, Sangwon Yu, Donghun Kang, Yeongtak Oh, Jaewon Jeong, Gyeongtae Yoo, Han Cheol Moon, Jungbeom Lee, Sungroh Yoon
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-11-03T13:48:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T13:48:28+00:00
Comments: Accepted to NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) can process interleaved text and multiple images in a single context, but how they internally identify the image relevant to a question remains poorly understood. We study this grounding step as in-context image retrieval and introduce ICIR-MCQ, a controlled probe for head-level analysis of image retrieval. Using each head's attention mass over image-token spans, we first identify sparse pointer heads that reliably point to the target image. These heads transfer beyond the probe: a single pointer head is competitive with external vision-language retrievers on a naturalistic multi-image retrieval benchmark, showing that LVLMs contain a strong internal image retrieval signal. Attention alone, however, shows where a head reads from, not what its output passes on to later layers, so we introduce a complementary output-side analysis. For each head, we train a lightweight image retriever on its value-weighted output and use it to identify writer heads, whose outputs contain enough information to identify the target image. Surprisingly, the pointer and writer criteria select substantially different head sets, with only 35 to 47 heads overlapping among the top-100 heads under each criterion. Targeted set-partition ablations show that the heads most important for standard multi-image inference are concentrated in this pointer-writer intersection, while heads selected by only one criterion show less consistent separation from random-head controls. The most consistent causal effects arise from heads selected by both the attention-based pointer and output-side writer criteria. Code and data: https://github.com/parkquasar/Image-Retrieval-Heads.

OCR in the Foundation Model Era: From Specialist Models to Unified Models

Abstract
Optical Character Recognition (OCR) is among the oldest and most widely deployed applications of artificial intelligence. This survey reviews its development, focusing on the Foundation Model Era. We define OCR through faithful transcription: within a specified visual scope, OCR should completely reconstruct the text, notation, and document structure observable in the source image, and every textual, notational, or structural element in the output must be verifiable from visual evidence in that image. We trace the development from specialist OCR models toward unified models, examining joint text detection and recognition, structured sequence generation, and the use of pretrained vision-language models (VLMs). We organize contemporary OCR systems into five coexisting families: modular toolkits and task-specific models, specialized document VLMs, hybrid workflows, general-purpose VLMs, and commercial products and managed services. We compare their architectures, data, and training, examining hallucination and omission, insufficient spatial grounding, and inference cost in generative OCR. We review evaluation from character and scene text recognition to parsing individual structural elements and full-page transcription, highlighting gaps in consistent output definitions, attribution of performance to individual components, and coverage of real capture conditions and visible content and structure. Finally, we summarize the challenges facing OCR and discuss directions for future research. We continuously track related work at https://github.com/zhihengli-casia/Awesome-OCR-in-the-Foundation-Model-Era.

VLMs Read Aloud: The Gap Between Recognizing and Using Visual Text
Authors: Zhiyun Shi
First: 2026-11-01T03:51:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T12:49:32+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, 7 figures, 21 tables
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Visual-text compression renders long contexts as images and is engineered around how much of the text a vision-language model (VLM) can recover. Yet recovered text is not usable text. VLMs can transcribe the text in an image word for word and still fail to answer from it; given back their own transcript, they answer nearly as well as from the original text. We call this difference the read-use gap. Recognizing and using visual text have different resolution requirements: Qwen3-VL-8B's recognition saturates at 15-pixel characters while use catches up only at 24 pixels, and scaling from 2B to 32B raises recognition without narrowing the gap. Putting the evidence into words closes it: thinking VLMs recite the evidence in their thinking, and their own transcript, written without seeing the question, recovers most of what thinking adds. In a preregistered held-out test, the gap ranks how much eight VLMs gain from their own words over pixels (Spearman $ρ$ = 0.86), which recognition rates do not. We propose ALOUD, which spends visual resolution on finding and recognizing evidence and language tokens on using it. Without keeping the source text, it doubles the accuracy of a frozen Qwen3-VL-8B on long documents compressed 5x and 10x: at 5x, from 27.7% to 54.8%, near the original text's 58.5%, with a context smaller than the compressed pages themselves. For less than the cost of reading the original text, it leads at equal budget every method that does without it, at every level, and beats AGAR and VERA, which paste the original text back; at 5x it also beats LensVLM-9B, which is trained and keeps the original text. Visual-text compression is limited not by how much text a model can recover, but by how much it can use.

Abstract
The proliferation of Large Vision Language Models (LVLMs), trained on vast and often sensitive datasets, raised concerns regarding data privacy and unauthorized usage. Unlearnable Examples (UEs), which subtly perturb training data to mislead the models, have emerged as a promising defense method. This paper presents the first exploration of UEs specifically on LVLMs, which poses unique challenges due to their inherent multimodal nature. We propose a novel text-based perturbation method, Natural Textual Unlearnable Examples (NTUE), which can effectively mislead LVLMs by subtly manipulating the image label, establishing a shortcut between an identity of interest and a separate target, without introducing factual misinformation or affecting readability. Comprehensive experiments compared with traditional UE methods demonstrate the superior performance of NTUE. Our findings show that NTUE achieves a high success rate with a poison rate as low as 5%, underscoring its potential as a practical and robust solution for protecting multimodal data in the era of LVLMs.

Abstract
Robotics and AI systems are increasingly deploying large vision-language models (LVLMs) to follow high-level instructions, from routine household chores to more complex organizational tasks. Yet, broad directives like "clean the room" or "organize the desk" frequently omit crucial details about user preferences and specific arrangement of objects that influence the desired outcome. Without any further guidance, such models tend to overestimate their grasp of ambiguous instructions and frequently assume they have sufficient understanding despite missing important details. In this paper, we propose CLARA (Conversational cLArification for Robotic Arrangement), a cognitively-driven clarification framework that proactively elicits key information from users, in a similar fashion as humans' clarification seeking behavior. Through targeted questioning, CLARA refines ambiguous requests into structured, detailed instructions describing precisely how objects should be organized or rearranged, according to the user's preferences. These clarifications then guide a diffusion-based image generation pipeline, producing realistic visual renderings of the final, desired scene. This final rendering also serves as a visual confirmation of the system's understanding from the user. Experimental results and user studies demonstrate that CLARA leads to more accurate and consistent outcomes compared to another State-of-the-Art LVLM system, highlighting the importance of interactive clarification for bridging the gap between vague directives and fully grounded task specifications.

Abstract
We formalize and enable the task of open tree decomposition, which segments an image into hierarchical trees of visual components with unconstrained granularity and flexibility. Specifically, we provide the foundation benchmark for this new paradigm with the following three key contributions. BtsPruner: Role-Aware Visual Token Pruning for Large Vision-Language Models
Authors: Zihao Qi, Zhibo Zhang, Miaoxin Zhang, Jincheng Cai, Zengqiang Yan
First: 2026-11-03T09:07:57+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T09:07:57+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Large Vision-Language Models (LVLMs) often process hundreds or thousands of visual tokens, resulting in substantial computational and memory overhead during inference. Existing training-free pruning methods typically estimate token importance using language-model attention, visual attention, or feature similarity. However, these methods treat visual tokens with uniform criteria and overlook that tokens may serve different roles during visual encoding. In this work, we investigate the evolution of visual token roles in LVLMs and observe that tokens progressively transition from local detail carriers to globally referenced representatives as encoding proceeds. Based on this observation, we propose BtsPruner, a training-free role-aware visual token pruning framework that preserves complementary token roles under a fixed budget. Specifically, BtsPruner first identifies the token-role transition point based on the evolution of attention concentration. It then discovers globally referenced representatives from later encoding stages and recursively retrieves local detail tokens from earlier stages to recover complementary fine-grained evidence. Extensive experiments across LLaVA-1.5, LLaVA-NeXT, Qwen-VL, InternVL, and CogVLM demonstrate that BtsPruner achieves superior performance retention under aggressive token reduction. On LLaVA-1.5-7B, BtsPruner preserves 91.6% of the dense model's performance using only 32 of 576 visual tokens (5.6%). In the high-resolution LLaVA-NeXT-7B setting, it achieves up to a 5.95x CUDA-latency speedup.

Bridging CLIP with DINO: Cross-Modal Information Maximization for Online Test-Time Adaptation
Authors: Taehoon Kim, Wonjun Hwang, Hyunsouk Cho

Abstract
Vision-language models such as CLIP enable zero-shot classification from arbitrary class names, but under test-time corruptions, their visual encoder projects distorted features onto incorrect textual anchors with high confidence: a phenomenon we term modality bias. Standard entropy minimization reinforces these overconfident errors, which creates a confirmation-bias loop and collapses the predicted label distribution. We propose an online test-time adaptation (TTA) method that breaks this loop at three levels. At the representation level, a frozen self-supervised backbone (e.g., DINO v2) is combined with CLIP through a cross-covariance logit ensemble. CLIP text prototypes are projected into the feature space of this text-free backbone, so the self-supervised branch classifies native image features and is less affected by the text bias of CLIP. At the optimization level, a gated test-time information-maximization objective pairs per-sample sharpening with batch-level diversity, while an adaptive gate excludes unreliable samples from the gradient. At the prediction level, an online logit adjustment corrects the residual class-distribution skew. CDIM outperforms the compared CLIP-based online TTA baselines on ImageNet-C, CIFAR10-C, and CIFAR100-C, and remains competitive in continual and mixed-domain TTA. Extensive analyses and ablation studies verify the contribution of each of its components. Our code is available at https://github.com/B1ueC1oud/CDIM

LenGuard-GPC: Length Guarding with Guided-Prompt Consistency for Spatial Reasoning Reinforcement Learning
Authors: Xingjian Tao, Yiwei Wang, Yujun Cai, Xinghua Qu, Jing Tang
First: 2026-07-19T13:24:40+00:00 · Latest: 2026-11-03T08:04:07+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multi-view spatial reasoning requires vision-language models to compare visual evidence across images, align object correspondences, and infer spatial relations over long visual contexts, a setting where chain-of-thought reasoning tends to grow verbose without becoming more accurate. Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards is a natural fit for this task, but standard GRPO reward relies on sparse outcome-level feedback and gives no signal about where a reasoning trajectory goes wrong, nor any control over its length. We propose LenGuard-GPC, a dense reward framework that addresses both problems together. For each sampled trajectory, it compares the token-wise predictive distributions under a standard prompt and a guided prompt, and uses the resulting token-sum KL divergence as a dense reward signal. Since this KL penalty accumulates over tokens and would otherwise reward shorter responses regardless of their quality, we introduce a staged length bonus that keeps reasoning length within a controlled range without simply encouraging brevity. On six multi-view spatial reasoning benchmarks, LenGuard-GPC improves accuracy over vanilla GRPO while reducing average response length.

Abstract
Test-time prompt tuning (TPT) has emerged as a promising technique for adapting large vision-language models (VLMs) to unseen tasks without relying on labeled data. However, the lack of dispersion between textual features can hurt calibration performance, which raises concerns about VLMs' reliability, trustworthiness, and safety. Current TPT approaches primarily focus on improving prompt calibration by either maximizing average textual feature dispersion or enforcing orthogonality constraints to encourage angular separation. However, these methods may not always have optimal angular separation between class-wise textual features, which implies overlooking the critical role of angular diversity. To address this, we propose A-TPT, a novel TPT framework that introduces angular diversity to encourage uniformity in the distribution of normalized textual features induced by corresponding learnable prompts. This uniformity is achieved by maximizing the minimum pairwise angular distance between features on the unit hypersphere. We show that our approach consistently surpasses state-of-the-art TPT methods in reducing the aggregate average calibration error while maintaining comparable accuracy through extensive experiments with various backbones on different datasets. Notably, our approach exhibits superior zero-shot calibration performance on natural distribution shifts and generalizes well to medical datasets.
