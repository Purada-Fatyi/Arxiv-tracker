Pretraining Knows What, Simulation Teaches How: Compositional Generalization in a Sim-Trained Robot Agent
Authors: Benjamin Pikus, Daniel Saunders, Mircea Mironenco, Salman Mohammadi, Mathew Mathew, Sirou Zhu, Divya Makkar, Xuhua Huang, Qinbo Li, Supreeth Narasimhaswamy, Siyuan Cen, Thomas Neff, Dejia Xu, Shyamal Buch
First: 2026-10-26T17:44:34+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T17:44:34+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A robot policy needs to understand what a task is asking for in a scene, and how to move the robot to accomplish that task. To achieve these two skills, we introduce Zero-real Agent Policy (ZAP), a recipe that teaches a small off-the-shelf vision-language model to control a robot via a simple tool harness. We use a pretrained 27B parameter model to bootstrap world understanding and reasoning, followed by supervised fine-tuning and GRPO on simple simulated tasks to teach it how to move the robot. Without any real-world robot data during training, ZAP reaches 96.7% success on real-world tasks, matching GPT-6 Astra in our harness and outperforming the MolmoAct2 VLA (33.3%), while running 3x faster and 17x cheaper than GPT-6 Astra. We demonstrate that ZAP also generalizes to completely new objects, goals, strategies, and language by seating a test tube in a rack (and choosing a different hole in the rack when prompted), handing a test tube between arms mid-air, drawing an X on a tic-tac-toe board, and playing chess. These goals and types of manipulation don't appear in our training data. Furthermore, ZAP also transfers to an unseen embodiment. On a single-arm Franka, ZAP reaches 55.9% on RoboLab-120, 4.9 percentage points ahead of the leading VLA/WAM baseline.

Explainable Multimodal AI Approach to Reduce Unnecessary Breast Biopsies
Authors: Sadam Hussain, Mansoor Ali, Daly Betzabeth Avendaño-Avalos, Jose Gerardo Tamez-Peña

Abstract
Recently, there has been rise in multimodal vision-language models for computer-aided diagnosis (CAD), yet their application in breast diagnosis, particularly for predicting biopsy outcomes remains limited. This study presents a multimodal vision-language approach, incorporating Grad-CAM as an Explainable AI (XAI) technique, to predict benign tumors in patients recommended for biopsy, especially those classified as BI-RADS 4 and 5. A novel multimodal framework was devel- oped using Vision Transformer (ViT) for imaging feature extraction and DeBERTa-V3-Large for textual embeddings. The model was trained and evaluated on a comprehensive in-house dataset comprising 1,632 patients, each with four mammography views and a corresponding radiology report. The features of both modalities were extracted independently and fused by late fusion before classification. Grad-CAM was used to generate visual explanations highlighting key regions influencing model predictions. The proposed model demonstrated high sensitivity 90%, 95%, 99%, and 100% with corresponding specificities of 40%, 36%, 18%, and 11%, respectively. The best AUC achieved was 0.72 by multimodal approach on the test set, outperforming the image only (0.66) and text-only (0.68) baselines. The proposed multimodal architecture, enhanced with Grad-CAM, demonstrates a strong potential to identify benign tumors among high-risk patients, offering interpretable insights and thereby helping reduce unnecessary biopsies, patient anxiety, and healthcare costs.

TrySee: Modular Perception-Enhanced VLM Grounding for Closed-Loop Robot Task Planning in Specialized Domains
Authors: Elias Hofele, Thomas Trapp, David Timmermann, Tristan Schnell, Georg Heppner, Arne Roennau
First: 2026-10-26T16:20:28+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T16:20:28+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision Language Models (VLMs) have shown impressive capabilities in high-level reasoning and task planning for robots. However, specialized domains remain a challenge for VLMs' image interpretation and grounding abilities. We introduce TrySee, a modular closed-loop robot task planning pipeline integrating general-purpose VLM reasoning with domain-specific perception and established robotics components. The system consists of an inherently reactive cyclic planning pipeline, augmented with domain-specific modular vision preprocessing, to provide online grounding. Finally, a dynamic preprocessing selection mechanism contributes to the scalability of the pipeline. The system requires neither VLM fine-tuning nor end-to-end demonstrations and adapts through replaceable perception modules and motion planning. The approach is evaluated on planning tasks across 5 widely different domains, including a real-world robot disassembly task. The field tests in a brownfield environment demonstrate the approach's practicality in integrating VLM planning into specialized robotic systems with limited domain-specific training data.

ArchiveLens: A Generative Spatial Knowledge Framework for Semantic Reconstruction of Historical Maps
Authors: Yanan Wu, Yalin Yang, Chengbin Deng
First: 2026-10-26T15:29:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T15:29:24+00:00
Comments: 31 pages
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Historical maps contain dense morphological and semantic information about past urban environments, but many digitization workflows focus primarily on extracting geometry, and do not capture cartographic semantics such as building use and construction materials. This study introduces ArchiveLens, a generative spatial knowledge framework that operationalizes historical maps as entity-centric, machine-queryable geospatial databases. The pipeline decouples morphological and semantic reconstruction through three stages: tiled YOLOv11-seg instance segmentation for building footprint extraction; deterministic material classification via hue, saturation, and value (HSV)-based color thresholds; and localized attribute parsing using a quantized vision-language model constrained by domain-specific prompts. Extracted entities are integrated into a hybrid Spatial-Temporal Retrieval-Augmented Generation (ST-RAG) architecture that combines dense semantic retrieval with symbolic spatio-temporal filtering and mandatory provenance grounding. Applied to 445 georeferenced Sanborn map sheets of Chicago (1918-1950), the framework achieves high geometric and semantic accuracy. Beyond performance, ArchiveLens advances GIScience by formalizing historical cartography as structured spatial knowledge representation, allowing researchers to query building attributes and compare spatial patterns across map sheets and years.

Beyond Reasoning Gains: Mitigating General-Capability Forgetting in Large Reasoning Models
Authors: Hoang Phan, Xianjun Yang, Yuanshun Yao, Jingyu Zhang, Shengjie Bi, Xiaocheng Tang, Madian Khabsa, Lijuan Liu, Deren Lei
First: 2025-10-24T19:08:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:42:04+00:00
Comments: Implementation is released at https://github.com/VietHoang1512/recap
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has delivered impressive gains in mathematical and multimodal reasoning and has become a standard post-training paradigm for contemporary language and vision-language models. However, the RLVR recipe introduces a significant risk of capability regression, in which models forget foundational skills after prolonged training without employing regularization strategies. We empirically confirm this concern, observing that open-source reasoning models suffer performance degradation on core capabilities such as perception and faithfulness. While imposing regularization terms like KL divergence can help prevent deviation from the base model, these terms are computed on the current task and therefore do not guarantee preservation of broader knowledge. Meanwhile, commonly used experience replay across heterogeneous domains makes it nontrivial to decide how much training emphasis each objective should receive. To address this, we propose RECAP-a replay strategy with dynamic objective reweighting for general knowledge preservation. Our reweighting mechanism adapts online using short-horizon signals of convergence and instability, shifting the post-training focus away from saturated objectives and toward underperforming or volatile ones. Our method is end-to-end and readily applicable to existing RLVR pipelines without training additional models or heavy tuning. Extensive experiments on benchmarks using Qwen2.5-VL-3B and Qwen2.5-VL-7B demonstrate the effectiveness of our method, which not only preserves general capabilities but also improves reasoning by enabling more flexible trade-offs among in-task rewards.

Measuring Cross-Modal Synergy: A Benchmark for VLM Explainability
Authors: Joël Roman Ky, Salah Ghamizi, Maxime Cordy

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) map complex visual inputs to semantic spaces, but interpreting the cross-modal reasoning of VLMs currently relies on post-hoc explainers evaluated via unimodal perturbation metrics. We expose a limitation in this paradigm: because multimodal datasets contain language priors and modality biases, VLMs frequently exhibit cross-modal redundancy, allowing them to answer visual queries using text alone. Consequently, unimodal metrics penalize faithful explainers, triggering an evaluation collapse where visual and textual rankings fundamentally contradict each other. To resolve this, we introduce Synergistic Faithfulness ($\mathcal{F}_{syn}$), a scalable metric rooted in the Shapley Interaction Index that strictly isolates the joint Harsanyi dividend between modalities, serving as a highly accurate surrogate ($ρ= 0.93$) while achieving up to $88\times$ computational speedup. Evaluating 9 distinct XAI methods across 3 VLM architectures and 3 benchmark datasets, reveals that explainers proposed for VLMs heavily over-index on visual salience and significantly underperform adapted attention-based methods in capturing true cross-modal synergy. By decoupling visual plausibility from cross-modal faithfulness, this work provides a rigorous evaluation framework required to safely audit VLM reasoning in high-stakes deployments. NGM: A Plug-and-Play Training-Free Memory Module for LLMs
Authors: Yuwen Qu, Wenhui Dong, Camil Hamami, Ming Zhao, Chenyang Si, Caifeng Shan
First: 2026-05-16T09:12:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-26T14:26:14+00:00
Comments: 17 pages, 4 figures. Revised manuscript with expanded experiments and analysis and updated author information. Project page: https://pioneerqyw.github.io/ngm-project/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Recent studies have introduced memory-augmented architectures that decouple knowledge storage from neural computation, enabling more direct access to stored representations. However, existing approaches often rely on learned memory embeddings or explicit memory and retrieval components, requiring additional training or increasing architectural complexity. To address these limitations, we propose N-gram Memory (NGM), a training-free, plug-and-play module that repurposes a backbone model's pretrained token embedding space as memory. NGM consists of a Causal N-Gram Encoder and a Cosine-Gated Memory Injector. The former constructs N-gram representations by directly aggregating pretrained token embeddings, while the latter uses cosine similarity with ReLU gating to adaptively control the amount of retrieved memory injected into contextual representations. Unlike conventional memory-augmented architectures, NGM requires neither additional embedding training nor a separately learned memory bank or external retrieval infrastructure. We evaluate NGM on ten benchmarks across six language-model backbones spanning two model families and multiple scales, including four Qwen3 models (0.6B, 1.7B, 4B, and 8B) and two Llama-3.2-Instruct models (1B and 3B). NGM consistently improves the ten-task average across all six backbones. Notably, it improves HumanEval by 4.26, HumanEval+ by 2.41, and GPQA-Diamond by 6.56 percentage points. Furthermore, NGM extends beyond text-only language models to vision-language models, with Qwen3-VL-2B achieving improvements across all five VLM benchmarks. Abstract
Deploying large vision-language models (LVLMs) on resource-constrained embodied agents for 3D visual question answering necessitates cloud-edge collaboration. However, offloading massive visual data to the cloud over bandwidth-limited and noisy channels introduces severe latency and transmission distortion. To address this, we propose TP-JSCC, an efficient token pruning and joint source-channel coding method for LVLMs-based 3D-VQA in Cloud-Edge Collaboration. First, we develop a Local and Global Semantics-based Hybrid Token Pruning (LGS-HTP) module to significantly reduce the visual token count. By decoupling foreground-background tokens and jointly selecting local and global semantics, LGS-HTP effectively reduces redundancy while preserving reasoning-critical tokens. Second, we introduce a lightweight Token Joint Source-Channel Coding (T-JSCC) module to further compress the pruned tokens and ensure their robust transmission over error-prone channels. By employing lightweight cascaded linear layers and Signal-to-Noise Ratio (SNR)-adaptive mechanism, this module adapts to fluctuating channel conditions while maintaining minimal computational overhead. Extensive experiments on ScanQA and SQA3D datasets indicate the exceptional efficiency and robustness of our approach. Specifically, on ScanQA validation set, the LGS-HTP module could achieve 90% pruning ratio while incurring less than a 1% CIDEr loss. Building on this, the T-JSCC further compresses the tokens by 18 times, resulting in an overall 180 times data volume reduction for robust transmission, while saving 90% in computational complexity for LVLM deployment. Abstract
Referring expression grounding in overhead imagery requires oriented bounding boxes rather than horizontal ones, since aerial objects appear at arbitrary orientations. Most grounding models emit box coordinates sequentially; Parallel Box Decoding (PBD) instead predicts all geometry slots in one forward pass, but only for four-coordinate horizontal boxes. We extend this block-aligned interface to a six-slot oriented representation using a double-angle parameterization. Rather than assuming correctness from a decreasing loss curve, we verify the extension with image/prompt-shuffle controls and visibility/leakage probes, which show that the corrected model depends on both the image and the prompt rather than on prompt statistics alone. On the aerial vehicle visual grounding (AVVG) benchmark, whose targets are genuinely oriented, the extended model improves rotated intersection over union (rotIoU) from 0.1253 for the zero-shot baseline to 0.303 +/- 0.012 (mean +/- standard deviation over three training seeds) across the complete 6139-record test split spanning all 120 images. Learning oriented boxes rather than their axis-aligned envelopes adds 0.055 +/- 0.008 rotIoU beyond a matched control (per-seed 95% confidence intervals all excluding zero), whereas block expansion alone yields no rotIoU gain distinguishable from zero.

Abstract
Edge SoCs pack CPUs, GPUs, and NPUs, yet vision--language model (VLM) runtimes keep generation on one engine and leave the others idle. Streaming VLMs expose further parallelism: new frames arrive while earlier queries are still being answered. Engines differ in compute and memory access and contend when they co-run, so no single placement of stages wins across SoCs and workloads. Backend-local KV state keeps other engines from continuing the work. \name coordinates heterogeneous accelerators to execute stateful VLM inference concurrently. Abstract
Emotion recognition in artworks remains challenging because affective meaning is often subjective, implicit, and shaped by relationships between visual form and semantic associations. While vision-language models provide strong multimodal representations, direct image-to-emotion prediction may overlook explicit relations among formal, semantic, and affective cues that support emotional interpretation. This paper proposes Visual-Semantic Affect Graphs (VSAG), a graph-based framework that represents artworks through interpretable feature nodes, including color, brushstroke, composition, light-shadow, line quality, and extracted visual entities. The graph learns feature-emotion associations for dominant emotion prediction, with valence-arousal structure modeled through an optional hierarchical auxiliary affect module. Results show that direct VLMs provide useful but modest baselines, while graph-based models improve dominant emotion prediction. These findings suggest that explicit visual-semantic structure can support both prediction and interpretation in artwork emotion recognition.

Abstract
Spatial intelligence lets an agent perceive and act in the physical world, and many of its judgments must be made at interaction rates: which spatial relation holds, how far an object is, or where to place it. Vision-language models (VLMs) produce these answers by free-form text generation, paying for autoregressive decoding and answer parsing even though the output space is compact: one of a few options, a scalar, or a 2D point. In light of this, we introduce MJev-Spatial, a System 1 multimodal decision model that tackles all three types of spatial understanding tasks within a unified classification framework. Specifically, MJev-Spatial treats each candidate as a class and scores it using the backbone's pretrained query and key projections, without adding parameters. The candidates are the given options for choice tasks, or value bins and image regions refined through coarse-to-fine classification for numerical and point prediction, respectively. At inference, each classification decision takes a single forward pass and generates no answer tokens. To train on heterogeneous spatial data efficiently, we supervise all coarse-to-fine stages jointly with teacher forcing in one forward pass, and combine this with length-aware global batching and cross-GPU load balancing, raising training throughput by 30.8% over the original batching strategy. Built on Qwen3.5-4B and trained on 12.3M question-answer pairs converted from existing spatial datasets, MJev-Spatial achieves the highest overall score (65.64%) among eight tested models, ranks first on seven of twelve benchmarks, and outperforms its unmodified autoregressive base model on all twelve, while running 2.5 times faster than the fastest autoregressive baseline and 5.6 times faster than the base model.

Abstract
Understanding and operating with scientific charts is a standard approach in data analysis, and while humans are good at interpreting such charts, open vision-language models (VLMs) are known to perform poorly when it comes to question answering based on such data types. Motivated by an initial observation that many of their errors are due to inexact answering, we raise the following natural question: how much accuracy can we recover without changing the model at all? In this paper, we study this question by searching over inference programs and the code around a "frozen" version of the model. Using ideas from autonomous evolution (famously used in AlphaEvolve), we first start from a baseline inference program, and in each round we show an LLM judge the code together with a few failures on training questions, and ask for an improving evolutionary move. As usual in autonomous evolution approaches, the candidate programs are kept in a small pool ranked by training accuracy, and the next edit is performed on top-scorers from this pool. We run this approach on three VLMs and four popular benchmarks on scientific charts, and we measure the fraction of descriptive questions whose answer exactly matches the ground truth. The accuracy improves in all experimental settings. On average, it rises from 16.6% with the starting program to 41.3% with the evolved one, and the largest single increase is 55.2 percentage points. As a side benefit, our evolved program is 3.8x faster than the starting program, because short answers are easier to grade and faster to generate. Our results demonstrate that a large part of the observed errors arise due to the inference pipeline, and that optimizing it can improve the "frozen" VLM without the need to retrain it.

Abstract
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) have emerged as a powerful paradigm for multimodal intelligence, but their growing deployment also expands the attack surface of prompt injection. Despite this growing concern, existing attacks still suffer from a critical limitation: the injected prompt for one modality only steers the model's interpretation of that singular input. Alternatively, these attacks remain multimodal but fail to achieve cross-modal prompt perturbation. To bridge this gap, we introduce a novel cross-modal prompt injection attack CrossMPI, which can steer the model's interpretation of both textual and visual inputs via image-only prompt injection. Our design is underpinned by the following key breakthroughs. First, we turn the focus of the injected prompt perturbation optimization from the visual embedding space (typically with only $10^5$ parameters) to the model hidden state space (for multimodal information integration and with $10^7$ parameters). Then, two strategies are adopted to mitigate the optimization challenges posed by the larger parameter space. To constrain the optimized model parameter space, we introduce a layer selection strategy that identifies the layers most critical to multimodal integration. Interestingly, deviating from the past experience, our analysis reveals that the optimal layers for LVLM prompt perturbation reside in the middle of the model rather than the last. To constrain the image perturbation space, we propose a new distance-decremental perturbation budget assignment strategy that allocates budgets decrementally as the pixel distance to semantic-critical regions increases. Extensive experiments across multiple LVLMs and datasets show that our method significantly outperforms baseline approaches.

Vision-language models (VLMs) ground a referring expression in one of two ways. They decode the box as coordinate tokens, which is precise but takes one sequential step per token, or they regress it from a hidden state with a small head, which takes one step but loses accuracy at high IoU. The two fail differently: a head usually finds the right object but places its edges loosely, while coordinate tokens place edges precisely. We therefore use the regression head as a speculative drafter for the model's own coordinate tokens. Locus writes the head's box as coordinate tokens and checks them with the same backbone by Jacobi iteration over a shared image prefix. The fixed point is the model's greedy coordinate-token answer, reached in about 5.60 sequential passes on average instead of 18.46 decoding steps; head and verifier are one set of weights, trained jointly. On the 8 RefCOCO, RefCOCO+ and RefCOCOg splits, verification raises Locus-2B's Acc@0.9 from 54.87 for its own head to 59.13, and the one-pass head is ahead at Acc@0.5 of every published model with at most 3B parameters except Florence-2-L. Against Jag, the strongest open regression-head model, at its own size and training budget, Locus ties at Acc@0.5 and, with typical acceptance, gains +4.52 points at Acc@0.9, with paired significance tests. Exact answers come 1.5x faster than vLLM greedy-decoding the same coordinate tokens with the same weights; a pure regression head remains faster still.

Abstract
Road anomalies such as collisions, violations, and sudden hazards demand more from video understanding than detection: a practical system must verify what happened, describe the scene, and explain the cause. We find these competences pull in opposite directions for vision-language models: closed-form questions reward precise, evidence-grounded reasoning, while open-ended answers are scored by similarity to reference text, where matching the annotation style matters as much as reasoning correctly. We present Reason-or-Recite (RoR), a two-specialist framework for the Traffic Anomaly Reasoning track of the 2026 AI City Challenge. RoR (1) rewrites the official chain-of-thought annotations into a structured, temporally grounded schema and LoRA-tunes a 9B vision-language model on the enhanced traces; (2) fine-tunes a 4-bit-quantized 27B model on answers alone, absorbing the annotation style; and (3) selects between the two candidates per question with a training-free LLM judge. RoR ranks first on the in-domain leaderboard with mean score 0.6788.

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly proposed as general-purpose tools for scientific data interpretation, yet their reliability on real astronomical observations across diverse modalities remains untested. We present AstroVLBench, a benchmark of over 4,100 expert-verified instances across five tasks spanning optical imaging, radio interferometry, multi-wavelength photometry, time-domain light curves, and optical spectroscopy. We systematically evaluate six state-of-the-art frontier models and find that performance is strongly modality-dependent and uncorrelated with general benchmark rankings. Mechanistic cross-model ablations reveal three concrete bottlenecks. First, prompt grounding exhibits marked model and task dependence: physical guidance yields more balanced classifications on optical imaging, whereas phenomenological prompts better navigate undersampled time-domain data. Second, presenting one-dimensional measurements as numerical tables rather than rendered plots consistently improves light-curve classification across all models by up to 23 percentage points, demonstrating that plot rendering can obscure physically relevant signal. Third, an expert audit of optical spectroscopy reveals a notable "right-answer-wrong-reason" phenomenon: across evaluated models, an average of 26% of correct predictions are accompanied by physically inconsistent or invalid reasoning chains, showing that accuracy alone is insufficient for trustworthy scientific deployment. AstroVLBench provides the first systematic, multi-modal baselines for VLMs in observational astronomy and identifies the representation, grounding, and reasoning bottlenecks that must be addressed before VLMs can be trusted as autonomous scientific interpreters.

Abstract
GUI agents built on vision-language models interact with continuously evolving interfaces, yet conventional inference repeatedly recomputes representations of largely overlapping observations and histories. This mismatch makes multi-step interaction costly, while changes in context, interface content, and action arguments prevent straightforward cache reuse. We introduce GUIFlow, a framework for full-pipeline incremental inference in multi-image GUI interaction. Our central insight is that advancing a decision need not require rebuilding all supporting representations: each interaction step can instead selectively update computational states maintained across the trajectory. To support new decisions as the context evolves, GUIFlow adapts how past computation is reused: historical KV states are position-aligned and injected into the current context; change signals guide selective updates of current-observation states in the vision encoder and language model; and earlier action responses serve as speculative drafts verified by the target model. Together, these mechanisms enable cross-step reuse throughout visual encoding, prefill, and decoding without additional training, a separate draft model, or changes to the model-visible inputs and action interface. Experiments demonstrate up to 3.39x geometric-mean speedup across offline multi-image benchmarks and up to 3.49x in online, closed-loop interaction on OSWorld, while maintaining task performance broadly comparable to the dense baseline.

Abstract
Physical understanding and reasoning depend on forming compact and generalizable representations of the world. While modern vision-language models can recognize and explain diverse physical events, they often lack explicit representations of the underlying mechanisms-such as object states, physical parameters, and governing dynamics-needed for reliably reasoning how the world evolves and responds to interventions. In this work, we introduce Code-as-World, a paradigm that represents physical worlds through executable world representations. By expressing physical composition, dynamic evolution, and visual appearance as executable code, Code-as-World provides a compact, quantitatively grounded, and controllable abstraction of the physical world. Abstract
Physical reasoning underpins how embodied systems understand and act in the physical world, yet vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable on questions about dynamics and physical causality. Progress is critically constrained by the scarcity of grounded counterfactual training data, as observational videos capture only the factual trajectory that occurred. The self-evolving paradigm offers a route beyond fixed datasets by enabling models to construct their own training problems. Abstract
Training-free few-shot adaptation of vision-language models typically constructs a classifier from CLIP's final projected image representation, leaving intermediate representations unused. Combining them is not straightforward. Class scores from different representations differ in spread by orders of magnitude for the same query. Direct averaging allocates influence by numerical scale rather than predictive information. We propose Layer-Ensemble Gaussian Discriminant Analysis (LE-GDA), a method that fits regularized shared-covariance discriminants independently to intermediate and final representations extracted in one frozen encoder pass. LE-GDA standardizes each discriminant's class scores per query, selects a small representation multiset on labeled validation data, and combines it with the zero-shot classifier without gradient-based optimization. Across 11 datasets, four CLIP backbones, five shot counts, and three independently sampled K-shot training sets, LE-GDA achieves the highest mean accuracy of all evaluated methods on every backbone, raising the 16-shot mean from 78.01% to 79.54% over a matched GDA-CLIP (final-feature) baseline and improving that baseline for every backbone-shot combination. At one shot, LE-GDA retains a 1.20-point mean gain over the baseline even though covariance estimation is unavailable and per-query standardization is neutral. A control with richer per-class structure inside a single representation does not reproduce the gain, indicating that the benefit comes from combining representations. These results show that intermediate CLIP representations carry information beyond the final embedding and can improve training-free few-shot adaptation. Code and evaluation resources are available at https://github.com/pavodi-NM/le-gda.

Abstract
Change Detection & Captioning (CDC) aims to jointly localize changed regions and describe their semantic content from bi-temporal remote sensing images. While recent approaches have demonstrated the effectiveness of unified modeling, they primarily rely on shared representations or jointly optimized architectures, leaving task-specific communication under heterogeneous supervision less explored. However, detection and captioning differ fundamentally in their supervision granularity, leading to heterogeneous representations with uneven reliability across tasks and spatial locations. In this paper, we reformulate CDC as a selective information routing problem between heterogeneous task branches, where the key challenge is to determine when and where information should be transferred. To this end, we propose CoDeRNet, a unified framework that preserves task-specific representations while enabling bidirectional exchange of complementary information. CoDeRNet introduces a Confidence-Decomposed Routing (CoDeR) mechanism, which decomposes the routing condition into receiver-side need for support and sender-side reliability cues to guide selective transfer. Experiments on LEVIR-MCI and WHU-CDC show that CoDeRNet achieves strong overall CDC performance, particularly improving caption generation while preserving competitive change localization. Extensive analyses further show that the benefits arise not merely from joint learning or generic cross-task communication, but from explicitly modeling selective cross-task information routing. These results highlight the importance of conditional information transfer for unified CDC under heterogeneous supervision.

Abstract
Visual counting benchmarks report whether vision-language models produce the correct number, but not whether errors arise from perception or reasoning. We introduce DecoyCount, a benchmark built from 1,660 real-world images with designed decoys across seven mechanisms, along with paired prompts: a neutral question, a matched alignment cue, and a novel multi-hop, multi-image question that situates the same counting problem in a realistic scenario. Using this design, we find that counting failures are induced partly by visual misalignment: models can execute the intended counting process when cue-guided but fail to select it unaided, and we term this difference in accuracy the reasoning-alignment gap. Despite being highly capable on general reasoning, frontier models remain below 50\% accuracy on our benchmark, while alignment gaps range from 11.8-22.1 percentage points; strikingly, we observe that more capable models, greater thinking budgets, and agentic processes often induce larger gaps. Because humans may bring context that models lack, we test whether textual underspecification contributes to misalignment using a scenario-based multi-hop condition without explicit hinting, but this added context does not improve aggregate performance. Our analysis concludes that stronger general reasoning does not by itself generalize to aligned visual reasoning, leaving misalignment with human visual expectations as a substantial component of the remaining deficit.

Abstract

Abstract
Safety pipelines often block content by thresholding a model's first-token unsafe score. We show that a single prompt's score is not a reliable probability and compare two repairs: mean ensemble and calibration with development labels. Across seven vision-language models and two benchmarks, an input's unsafe probability varies across 15 task-preserving rewordings with a standard deviation of 0.16-0.30 on average; the inputs that vary most are more often misclassified. The 15-prompt mean has lower negative log-likelihood (NLL) than the best single prompt, even one chosen with evaluation labels, on 13 of 14 model-benchmark pairs. Development labels change the picture: jointly selecting and calibrating a single prompt outperforms the similarly calibrated mean for all seven models on held-out data from the development benchmark. Under cross-benchmark evaluation without refitting, however, the advantage reverses, with the calibrated mean outperforming the single prompt on six of seven models. We recommend evaluating first-token scores as a prompt family, using the mean ensemble as the label-free baseline, calibrating one prompt when development labels exist and deployment data match, and calibrating the mean when they may not.

Abstract
Reconstructing 1-m imagery from Sentinel-2 combines two difficulties. The input and the reference are observed by different satellites, which differ in spatial resolution, spectral response, radiometry, acquisition time, and local geometry, so no synthetic degradation operator links them. The required magnification of ten in each spatial dimension also far exceeds the factors of two to four at which remote sensing super-resolution is usually developed. We study this real-world $\times 10$ problem using YREB-SR, which pairs 2022 Sentinel-2 RGB composites with 2022 Jilin-1 RGB references resampled to a 1-m grid over the Yangtze River Economic Belt. The benchmark contains 11,559 geographically matched scene pairs, with 6,409, 2,135, and 3,015 assigned to training, validation, and testing, respectively. We develop CrossVAR, a visual autoregressive model that predicts the 1-m latent as a nine-scale hierarchy conditioned on the Sentinel-2 input and estimates the remaining quantization residual with a diffusion refiner run without injected noise. Complete 1600 $\times$ 1600 scenes are reconstructed in overlapping windows that start on the Sentinel-2 pixel grid, as every training patch does, and every window is decoded at the size on which the decoder was trained before the windows are blended with Gaussian weights. On all 3,015 test scenes, CrossVAR achieves 27.60 dB PSNR, 0.7725 SSIM, 0.1451 LPIPS, and 7.15 FID, the best value of each metric among the compared methods. These results establish a strong autoregressive baseline for large-scale cross-sensor reconstruction. To facilitate reproducibility, the source code and implementation are publicly available at https://github.com/SHANOAL/RSSR.

Abstract
Visual information worth extracting from an image and the token budget it warrants depend on the text query. We introduce the Query-Adaptive Recurrent Tokenizer (QART), a backbone-agnostic module that makes visual representations both query-steerable and budget-adaptive.
