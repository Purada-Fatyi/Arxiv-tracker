Sensorimotor Alignment: A Platonic Proxy for Embodied Policy Success
Authors: Yunfei Xie, Howard H. Qian, Shiyi Lan, Kaiyu Hang, Chen Wei
First: 2026-10-22T17:59:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:59:51+00:00
Comments: 28 pages. Project page and code available at https://yunfeixie233.github.io/s2/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Robotic foundation models are typically evaluated through costly closed-loop rollouts, which motivates cheap offline proxies for model capability. The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (PRH) suggests that as representations improve, different modalities such as vision and language converge toward a shared statistical model of reality. We ask whether motion trajectories form another such modality: as embodied models become more capable, should their sensory representations converge toward the structure of physical motion? To examine this, we propose Sensorimotor Alignment ($S^2$), a training-free offline metric that measures how well a model's sensory representation aligns with the geometry of physical motion. We find that $S^2$ correlates positively with downstream robot task success across embodied model families, making it a low-cost offline proxy for prioritizing models before closed-loop evaluation. $S^2$ can also identify promising vision-language model (VLM) backbones even before costly fine-tuning into vision-language-action models (VLAs). Beyond this correlation, $S^2$ offers representation-level explanations for phenomena observed in VLAs and world action models (WAMs). With $S^2$, we extend PRH to embodied models and make sensorimotor alignment empirically measurable, offering insight into why an embodied model succeeds beyond its closed-loop success rate.

CellTrack-Bench: Microscopic Cell Tracking Benchmark for Vision-Language Models
Authors: Ilan Theodoro, Jordão Bragantini, Loïc A. Royer
First: 2026-10-22T17:09:12+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T17:09:12+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Large language models have shown outstanding performance on text-first tasks such as coding, math, and writing. Recent efforts are shifting models' training to include scientific problems that often live in highly specialized domains beyond text, such as protein structure and biological images. However, in these scenarios, their general-purpose accuracy compared with specialized algorithms remains in question. One such domain is cell tracking, which requires spatiotemporal awareness of cell dynamics. To evaluate this, we created a new benchmark, CellTrack-Bench, which assesses Vision-Language Models' (VLMs) cell-tracking-related performance against specialized trackers. The full benchmark draws from 15 standard cell-tracking datasets across four imaging modalities and comprises 1,537 problems corresponding to 9,041 cell-linking decisions. We selected a hard subset requiring greater reasoning effort by identifying failures shared across distinct algorithmic trackers. With this proposed dataset, we: (i) evaluated different environment harnesses for VLMs; (ii) benchmarked 9 frontier closed-source models and 13 open-weight models; and (iii) provided a detailed analysis of their failure modes. Code, the stored model outputs, and a pipeline that regenerates the benchmark from the source datasets are available at https://github.com/royerlab/celltrack-bench.

ORSIFlow: Saliency-Guided Rectified Flow for Optical Remote Sensing Salient Object Detection
Authors: Haojing Chen, Zhihang Liu, Yutong Li, Tao Tan, Haoyu Bian, Qiuju Ma
Venue: ICME Spotlight
First: 2026-03-30T15:33:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:51:55+00:00
Comments: 2026 ICME Spotlight
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Optical Remote Sensing Image Salient Object Detection (ORSI-SOD) remains challenging due to complex backgrounds, low contrast, irregular object shapes, and large variations in object scale. Existing discriminative methods directly regress saliency maps, while recent diffusion-based generative approaches suffer from stochastic sampling and high computational cost. In this paper, we propose ORSIFlow, a saliency-guided rectified flow framework that reformulates ORSI-SOD as a deterministic latent flow generation problem. ORSIFlow performs saliency mask generation in a compact latent space constructed by a frozen variational autoencoder, enabling efficient inference with only a few steps. To enhance saliency awareness, we design a Salient Feature Discriminator for global semantic discrimination and a Salient Feature Calibrator for precise boundary refinement. Extensive experiments on multiple public benchmarks show that ORSIFlow achieves state-of-the-art performance with significantly improved efficiency.

DuoCook: An Overcooked-Inspired Benchmark for Visual Planning and Coordination
Authors: Ziyi Jiao, Heng Zhang, Drishti Regmi, Xiaokang Ye, Lianhui Qin, Peter Yichen Chen
First: 2026-10-22T16:26:38+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:26:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Coordinating two players in a cooking task requires a vision-language agent to track changing scene states, sequence recipe steps, and allocate shared resources under a time budget. We introduce an Overcooked-inspired benchmark for evaluating these capabilities in Unreal Engine environments constructed using coding agents. A single vision-language model controls both players from top-down visual observations, selecting high-level actions while the engine executes navigation. The benchmark contains 20 maps in five categories. These categories support evaluation of centralized coordination under equipment contention, changing terrain, and handoff dependencies. A prop legend and pot ingredient-count indicators provide explicit visual cues, while grid-based destinations support spatial grounding. The evaluation compares four VLMs on all 20 maps, measuring task score, order completion, delivery precision, action acceptance rate, and model API expenditure. A memory ablation on the same 20 maps examines the effect of a shared, self-authored notebook while retaining six-response action history in both conditions. The strongest evaluated model, \texttt{gpt-6-astra}, achieves 26.2\% mean order completion. Notebook memory has mixed score effects across models, with positive mean differences for Claude and Qwen and negative differences for GPT and Gemini.

AgentVQA: A Unified Benchmark for Agentic Visual Understanding
Authors: Advait Gupta, Sachin Konan, Tom Yin, Gabriel Sarch, Zhuang Liu
First: 2026-10-22T16:13:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:13:35+00:00
Comments: Project page: https://zlab-princeton.github.io/AgentVQA
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) can perform a broad range of tasks across diverse settings, yet their performance in agentic contexts remains poorly understood. Although online evaluations in simulators and real-world environments are essential for measuring interactive agent performance, they are costly and challenging to scale. Complementary offline VQA benchmarks can enable faster and more reliable comparisons across agentic domains, yet existing benchmarks remain limited and fragmented. To address this gap, we introduce AgentVQA, an offline benchmark for evaluating VLMs on realistic agentic scenarios across five agent domains: Web Agents, Robotics, Egocentric Videos, Games, and Spatial Planning. We build AgentVQA by manually selecting 1000 challenging, real-world agent examples from 23 source datasets and adapting them for reliable offline evaluation. The resulting benchmark preserves realistic scenarios from interactive environments across diverse domains, and its model rankings track those from corresponding online evaluations. Across the 22 evaluated models, the best achieves only 58.5% accuracy. Our cross-domain analysis shows that errors are driven primarily by failures to ground and maintain accurate representations of the current and evolving visual state, including localizing relevant UI elements, interpreting motion, tracking state changes over time, and inferring 3D spatial relationships, rather than by task misinterpretation or downstream logical reasoning.

MoG-VLM: Efficient Video Anomaly Detection through Motion-Guided Vision-Language Models
Authors: Khaled Waleed Dawoud, Muhammad Zaigham Zaheer, Khan Muhammad, Muhammad Haris Khan
First: 2026-10-22T16:03:47+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T16:03:47+00:00
Comments: Accepted at BMVC 2026. Includes supplementary material
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have shown strong potential for video anomaly detection, but dense frame-level inference makes them impractical for real-time deployment. Existing acceleration commonly relies on uniform sampling, which ignores content dynamics and can either waste computation on static segments or miss localized anomalies. We propose MoG-VLM a motion-adaptive anomaly detection framework that leverages motion purely as a lightweight control signal to decide when VLM inference is necessary. To this end, we introduce Temporal Gradient Magnitude (TGM) which performs content-aware sampling to concentrate computation on informative regions while avoiding redundant processing. We further introduce a hierarchical dual-stream design in which dense multi-frame VLM reasoning is activated only around frames with elevated anomaly likelihood, via anchor-triggered context-window expansion with early termination. A fusion module then reconciles these instantaneous semantic cues with temporal action dynamics. Across four benchmarks, our method achieves 91.91%, 84.30%, 87.88%, and 78.96% AUC-ROC on XD-Violence, UCF-Crime, MSAD, and ShanghaiTech, respectively, establishing new state of the art on three benchmarks under the zero-shot, training-free setting. On XD-Violence, we improve over the previous best result by +0.57% while delivering a 1.31x inference speedup. These results demonstrate that content-aware allocation of VLM capacity, processing fewer frames more intelligently rather than all frames uniformly, is a more effective axis of progress than uniformly densifying VLM inference across frames. Code is available on GitHub: https://github.com/kdawoud91/MoG-VLM.

Ask About the Right Moment: Training-Free Object Identity Reasoning for Video-Language Models
Authors: Di Yang, Xiaochang Li, Muyang Liu, Yufei Wang, Yanhai Xiong
First: 2026-10-22T15:59:59+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T15:59:59+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Many questions about a video concern one physical object. Where does the ball end up after the cups are shuffled? Which card is the queen? How often was the mug covered? Video-language models (Video-LMs) recognise such objects in single frames but do not keep them apart over time. On real shell-game videos, open models from 8B to 235B parameters, their thinking variants, a reinforcement-learned tool agent and three training-free tool agents all stay within ten points of chance. We separate semantic identity binding from temporal identity maintenance. An object's identity should be fixed at a moment when the evidence for it is visible, and carried afterwards by a tracker. \emph{Track, Then Bind} implements this principle without training. A promptable video tracker follows every candidate object and draws it with a track number. A frozen vision-language model answers a static question about the identifying evidence, which binds the target to a track. The answer is then derived from the tracked state in text, without asking the model to reinterpret the original video during read-out. A single configuration covers all benchmarks. With Qwen3-VL-32B it solves both VET-Bench tasks, where a fine-tuned trajectory model reaches 92\% and 90\% and the same VLM voting over 16 samples stays at 30--32\%. It answers 55.4\% of the hardest Perception Test cup games, where the best published model answers 41.5\%, and, using track-derived timelines, 48.6\% of TOC-Bench questions, against 37.1\% for the same model answering directly. Drawing the same tracks on the video and asking the question directly falls 12--19 points short of the agent on real cup games.

Perceive, Interact, Reason: Building Tool-Augmented Visual Agents for Spatial Reasoning
Authors: Changye Li, Meng Lu, Yi Wu, Ligeng Zhu
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-06-11T02:54:07+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:28:02+00:00
Comments: Accepted at NeurIPS 2026. Camera-ready version with additional experiments, analyses, and clarified methodology. 38 pages, including appendices and checklist
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
While recent vision-language models (VLMs) demonstrate strong multimodal understanding, they remain limited in spatial reasoning tasks that require active evidence acquisition and multi-step visual interaction. This limitation suggests that relying solely on implicit visual representations from vision encoders is insufficient for recovering fine-grained spatial evidence. We introduce PERception-Interaction-reason Agent (PERIA), a tool-augmented visual agent for spatial reasoning tasks across map reasoning, visual probing, and vision reconstruction. PERIA uses two lightweight tool families: vision perception tools for exposing textual, symbolic, and spatial evidence, and vision interaction tools for manipulating visual context, tracing paths, and verifying spatial relations. To train PERIA, we develop a unified recipe that combines supervised tool-use trajectory synthesis, composite rewards, and Observation-Relaxed Group-in-Group Policy Optimization (OR-GIGPO) for effective multi-tool behavior. Experiments on 13 benchmarks from 8 datasets show that PERIA-8B improves over the Qwen3-8B backbone by 10.0 percentage points on in-distribution benchmarks and 4.4 percentage points on out-of-distribution benchmarks, while outperforming previous state-of-the-art baselines of similar size by 6.9--14.8 percentage points. It also achieves performance comparable to much larger models such as Qwen3-VL-235B-A22B-Thinking and GPT-5, demonstrating the effectiveness of PERIA in enhancing spatial reasoning capabilities.

WeVisOne-VL: Unified Visual Perception from Objects to Pixels
Authors: Tianming Liang, Junyi Pan, Yuxi Li, Canmiao Fu, Zhipeng Huang, Chong Sun, Chen Li, Jing Lyu
First: 2026-10-22T14:19:50+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T14:19:50+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Existing vision-language models (VLMs) remain unreliable for fine-grained visual perception. In this report, we present WeVisOne-VL (WeChat Vision All-in-One), a powerful VLM that unifies object-level and pixel-level perception through structured next-token prediction. It supports multiple visual tasks including object detection, referring localization, counting, OCR, segmentation, and matting, enabling localization at any user-specific granularity, from points and boxes to masks and alpha mattes. Architecturally, we integrate a lightweight pixel decoder into the VLM via a special <OBJ> token to generate instance-level pixel outputs. We train the model with a three-stage curriculum SFT followed by mixed-task RL. Across over 30 benchmarks, WeVisOne-VL demonstrates leading performance on both object-level and pixel-level tasks, while maintaining robust rejection for object-absent queries. By releasing WeVisOne-VL to the open-source community, we hope to advance VLMs from broad visual understanding toward truly reliable perception systems for real-world deployment.

Abstract
During the deep learning era, innovations in remote sensing (RS) vision models primarily focused on optimizing network architectures for specific tasks and conducting end-to-end training. However, with the advent of the Vision-Language Models (VLMs) era, constrained by substantial computational resource demands, the mainstream research paradigm has shifted towards adapting general-purpose VLMs using RS vertical domain data for fine-tuning, rather than modifying their core architectures. This transition marks a change in the innovation pathway from model-centric to data-centric. Despite the increasing importance of data, the academic community still lacks a systematic, data-centric survey to guide researchers in constructing high-quality RS vision-language datasets for various application scenarios and in conducting effective model fine-tuning and performance evaluation based on these datasets. To address this gap, this paper presents a systematic review of the development status of RS datasets tailored for VLMs. It further provides a comprehensive practical guide for RS researchers in the large model era, covering the entire pipeline from data construction to fine-tuning and evaluation. This paper aims to encourage the academic community to build more diverse and higher-quality vision-language datasets for specialized RS sub-fields. Ultimately, the goal is to converge these efforts into a comprehensive RS vision-language corpus that offers extensive task coverage and a wide geographical distribution, thereby laying a solid data foundation for advancing towards general artificial intelligence in RS.

ReCoG: Relational Concept Graph for Training-Free Personalization
Authors: Changseon Yu, Hyeokjun Kweon
First: 2026-10-22T13:48:41+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T13:48:41+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Training-free personalization adapts a frozen vision-language model (VLM) to recognize user-defined concepts from only a few exemplars by retrieving concept entries at inference time. While recent retriever-based methods enrich each concept with descriptive evidence, they largely treat concepts in isolation, so the retrieved information can be plausible yet non-discriminative, especially when the personalized concept set contains highly confusable, fine-grained categories. We argue that personalization is inherently relational: correct recognition depends on the specific distinctions between a concept and its nearest alternatives. To this end, we propose ReCoG, a Relational Concept Graph for retrieval-augmented personalization. ReCoG represents concepts as nodes and augments them with directed edges that explicitly capture discriminative cues between concept pairs, elicited by a frozen VLM during database construction. At inference, ReCoG retrieves a shortlist and resolves ambiguity via explicit pairwise comparisons conditioned on the corresponding relational edges, selecting the concept that consistently wins against competing candidates. We also introduce FINGER benchmark to test personalized recognition on confusable concept sets. Across FINGER, ReCoG substantially outperforms prior methods, with the largest gains in the most confusable regimes. ReCoG also achieves consistently best performance on established personalization benchmarks including captioning and VQA, confirming that relational evidence is broadly beneficial beyond fine-grained settings. Pathways of Visual Information Flow in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Israfel Salazar, Stella Frank, Dan Oneata, Desmond Elliott, Constanza Fierro
First: 2026-07-03T14:15:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T12:44:16+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We study how visual information is routed in vision-language models (VLMs). Using causal patching on controlled synthetic and natural datasets, we find that models rely on two distinct pathways to solve visual tasks: A direct pathway, where visual information is retained in image token representations and read out by the final token at later layers, and a text-mediated pathway, where visual information is first transferred to the query tokens and then read out by the final token. Across three visual tasks, we show that pathway selection is task-dependent, and that data distribution and prompt design can also modulate which pathway is used to solve the image-based query. Moreover, using attention knockouts and corrupted-input patching, we find that these pathways are flexible, under certain interventions, models can rely on the text-mediated pathway as a fallback when the usual pathway is ablated. This behavior unifies findings in prior work and shows that ablation-based interventions can reveal what models could do rather than what they normally do. Together, our results provide a mechanistic characterization of visual information flow in VLMs and highlight the flexibility of their internal mechanisms under intervention.

We study how visual information is routed in vision-language models (VLMs).
Rethinking LLM-Guided Visual Question Answering: Does Deeper Knowledge Integration Really Help?
Authors: Aneerban Saha, Vijay Kumar, Sameer Madhav D., Priyam Srivastava, Dhruv Bansal

Abstract
Some questions about an image need commonsense or factual knowledge that its pixels do not contain. Large Language Models (LLMs) can supply this knowledge to Visual Question Answering (VQA) systems, but the depth at which their guidance should enter a vision-language model remains unclear. We prompt the 70-billion-parameter Llama~3.3 model to describe, for each question, the visual concepts to focus on, the distractors to ignore and the external facts needed. We inject this guidance into a frozen Vision Transformer (ViT) paired with an instruction-tuned Text-to-Text Transfer Transformer (T5) in two ways: decision-level late fusion and attention-matrix injection. On the Outside Knowledge VQA (OK-VQA) benchmark, and contrary to the common view that deeper is better, the shallow route wins: late fusion reaches 39.4% exact match, +10.0 points above an unguided baseline, while attention injection reaches only 31.8% under data-scarce fine-tuning. An LLM paraphrasing pipeline also enlarges the training set more than tenfold. The results suggest that integration depth should be matched to the data regime.

CLIVO: Differentiable Cluster-Based Instruction Mixing with Learned Validation Objectives
Authors: Yuhan Wang, Rui Hu, Batian Di, Tan Jiang, Dongsheng Ma, Leichao Dong, Yuanzi Li, Yalin Feng
First: 2026-10-12T14:38:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T10:05:48+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Data selection turns large multimodal instruction pools into compact training subsets tailored to a vision-language model's target capabilities. Effective selection requires both useful examples and an appropriate balance of supervision. Sample-level rankings can overrepresent similar content without optimizing this balance, while group-level methods are constrained by coarse allocation units, predefined validation sets, and costly or inaccurate mixture search. We propose CLIVO, a target-aware framework that connects fine-grained semantic allocation, automatically learned validation objectives, and differentiable mixture search. Specifically, semantic clusters define independently adjustable training shares, and merged cluster experts provide reusable proxies for evaluating mixtures. We select validation examples by correlating their proxy losses with real probe performance, then learn nonnegative weights to form a target-specific objective. With experts and validation weights fixed, a single backward pass supplies gradients for all shares, avoiding an additional mixture-response regression model. Selecting 100K instructions from an 8M pool, CLIVO attains 97.2% of the full-data four-target average score using only 1.25% of the data. At this budget, it outperforms the strongest evaluated selection baseline by 1.16 points with random within-cluster sampling; combining learned quotas with sample ranking increases this advantage to 1.55 points. CLIVO further supports interpretable, flexible, and reusable data curation through semantic visualization, modular selectors, and shared experts.

Diagnostic Loss: Asymmetric Supervision for Radiology Report Generation
Authors: Ignacy Kolton, Wenxuan Li, Zongwei Zhou, Jarosław B. Ćwikła, Arkadiusz Sitek, Pedro R. A. S. Bassi, Szymon Płotka
First: 2026-10-22T09:26:17+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T09:26:17+00:00
Comments: 26 pages, 6 figures, 7 tables. Code available at https://github.com/gmum/DLoss
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Automated radiology report generation requires models to do more than produce fluent clinical narratives: they must explicitly state supported diagnoses while avoiding unsupported ones. However, existing medical vision-language models (MedVLMs) are typically trained with token-level maximum likelihood objectives, which prioritize text agreement over diagnostically critical terms. As a result, models can miss key diagnoses and introduce wrong ones. We identify this as an objective misalignment between conventional language modeling and the asymmetric diagnostic requirements of radiology reporting. To encode these requirements, we introduce Diagnostic Loss (DLoss), a differentiable, architecture-agnostic objective that operates directly on decoder token probabilities. DLoss separately aggregates probability mass over vocabularies associated with diagnoses supported by the reference report and with unsupported alternatives. Using asymmetric pooling across token positions in the report, it encourages supported diagnoses to receive sufficient probability in at least one position while suppressing spurious probability spikes for unsupported diagnoses across all positions during training. The loss can be applied to existing 3D MedVLMs without modifying their vision backbones, language decoders, or inference procedures, and introduces no additional inference-time computation. We train and evaluate DLoss on 50,000 abdominal CT scans across several 3D MedVLM architectures on both in-distribution and out-of-distribution report-generation benchmarks. Across these settings, DLoss improves diagnostic faithfulness while maintaining competitive performance on conventional language-generation metrics, showing the value of asymmetric sequence-level supervision for radiology report generation. The code for DLoss is available at https://github.com/gmum/DLoss.

SpatialScout: Can VLM Agents Actively Explore and Map 3D Scenes?
Authors: Zhaoxi Zhang, Hao Liang, Linxuan Wu, Yiming Xu, Yifu Yuan, Kun Liang, Chenming Tang, Meiyi Qiang, Wentao Zhang, Weikang Li
First: 2026-10-19T16:17:48+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T08:51:38+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Spatial intelligence requires agents to actively acquire visual evidence and integrate observations into a coherent representation of the three-dimensional world. Yet spatial question answering offers limited evidence of whether models can construct accurate metric maps through autonomous exploration. We introduce SpatialScout, a benchmark for evaluating vision-language model (VLM) agents on active object-level 3D mapping. SpatialScout comprises 80 diverse indoor scenes containing 3,140 target objects. Given an object inventory, RGB observations, camera calibration, and camera poses, agents control a camera to explore each scene and estimate object centers in a shared coordinate frame. The task requires coordinating viewpoint selection, cross-view association, geometric estimation, and map revision under bounded interaction budgets. Evaluation jointly measures localization accuracy and target coverage through object-scale overlap scores that penalize missing predictions. Experiments with frontier VLMs reveal substantial limitations: the strongest evaluated model, Claude Opus 5.5, achieves a mean overlap score of 41.71%, with 47.23% of targets exceeding an intersection-over-union (IoU) threshold of 0.5 and only 3.25% exceeding 0.9. These results reveal a substantial gap between recovering coarse object layouts and constructing complete, precise metric maps. SpatialScout provides a quantitative testbed for developing agents that build reliable 3D spatial representations through active perception. Code is available at https://github.com/JesseZZZZZ/SpatialScout.

Watch and Learn: Teaching Visual In-Context Learning by Removing Textual Shortcuts
Authors: Nimrod Shabtay, Asaf Yehudai, Avihu Dekel, Eli Schwartz, Raja Giryes
First: 2026-10-22T08:34:24+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T08:34:24+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with in context learning compared to their textual counterparts. Our preliminary study shows that VLMs rely on textual shortcuts and underutilize the visual demonstrations. We therefore hypothesize that VLMs are insufficiently trained to perform visual in-context learning (vICL). To this end, we construct MIME, a task-agnostic vICL training set without textual shortcuts. We ensure the instruction does not state the rule linking the demonstration images to their answers, so the model must infer it from the visual demonstrations. Fine-tuning on MIME improves vICL accuracy by 8.1 to 18.0 points across three VLMs, preserves single-image performance, and improves multi-image and text only in-context learning. In contrast, stating the rule in the training data sharply reduces this gain. We further propose DTAD (Demonstration Text Attention Dropout), which randomly withholds demonstration labels during training so that the VLM must infer the rule from the demonstration images. While achieving comparable vICL accuracy to fine-tuning on MIME, DTAD yields more consistent attention to the demonstration images. On TrueMICL, Qwen3-VL-8B trained on MIME matches frontier models such as Claude Opus 5, and surpasses them with DTAD. Finally, adding MIME to the instruction-tuning mix of LLaVA 1.5 13B more than doubles its vICL accuracy without hurting single-image abilities, whereas a same-size multi-image set improves it only slightly. These results suggest that vICL data should be a standard component of VLM training.

TwinStore: A Synthetic Consumer Platform Using Vision--Language Models
Authors: Minhyeok Lee
First: 2026-10-22T08:31:18+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-22T08:31:18+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
We present TwinStore, a source-available synthetic consumer platform for physical retail built on vision--language models. TwinStore turns a store into a digital twin---from 82 ready-made formats, a floor-plan image or phone photos of its shelves---and fills it with synthetic shoppers who plan a trip, walk the aisles and decide what to buy by looking at rendered shelves with packs, price tags and promotion signs. Every trip ends in a receipt, so a layout, price, pack or promotion can be tried on the same shoppers before it reaches the store. Around this core, TwinStore provides 3D playback, paired comparisons and layout optimization, shopper interviews, a trade-area model, counterfactual edits of real shelf photos, a marketing studio with eleven tools, and an assistant that turns a plain sentence into a drafted study or an answer grounded in a run's own numbers. In a held-out validation on eleven public datasets, simulated receipts approach the households' own natural variation (category-mix divergence 0.017--0.018 against a real drift of 0.012), price elasticities fall inside the range measured in the field, and three independent decision models agree. TwinStore runs on cloud or on-device models and is distributed with installers for Windows, macOS and Linux.

Abstract

Abstract
Domain generalization (DG) aims to improve the generalization of the model to any unseen domains.
