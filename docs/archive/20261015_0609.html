The Last Remote Sensing Model Built by Humans? Casting Any Earth Observation Task as Recursive Self-Improvement
Authors: Sijie Zhao, Zeqi Zhou, Heli Qi, Xuanchao Fu, Zhuo Zheng, Junshi Xia, Junjue Wang, Weihao Xuan, Naoto Yokoya
First: 2026-10-13T17:57:51+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:57:51+00:00
Comments: Ongoing project
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Earth observation (EO) models are still built by hand. For each new task, experts design a training recipe and tune it against a benchmark. We show that this loop can be handed to an agent. Any verifiable task can be cast as a harness and a verifier, where the harness defines what the agent may use and the verifier defines what counts as success. Every EO task can be made verifiable, through labeled benchmarks, independent sensors, physical models or the repeated observation of the same place. Given only these two components, a general-purpose coding agent builds the EO model and improves it recursively on its own outputs. Settings long treated as separate research problems, from learning with a handful of labels to adapting across domains, become choices of harness and verifier. On four EO benchmarks spanning optical, SAR and multispectral time-series imagery, agent-built models surpass the state of the art and keep most of that accuracy from about 1% of the training labels. This establishes a new paradigm for EO, in which people design harnesses and verifiers and agents build the models, so EO models improve as agents do.

Abstract
Vision-Language Models (VLMs) have achieved strong performance in visual perception and spatial reasoning, leading to growing interest in using them as high-level planners for embodied AI. However, physical tasks require models to integrate spatial information, reason over temporal states, and generate multi-step plans under various constraints, raising the question of whether current VLMs can reliably support such reasoning and planning independently of execution. We introduce Plan-Bench, a benchmark for evaluating the high-level reasoning and multi-step planning capabilities of VLMs relevant to embodied worlds, while isolating the influence of action execution. It covers three complementary dimensions, spanning 16 subclasses and 699 curated samples. Extensive evaluation of 28 VLMs reveals room for improvement, with even the strongest model achieving only 75.0% accuracy. We further find that model scaling generally improves these capabilities. Ablation studies also indicate larger models show increasing potential as high-level planners. Overall, Plan-Bench provides a lightweight probe for evaluating VLMs' reasoning and planning capabilities relevant to embodied environments and assessing their potential as decision makers for physical tasks. Our benchmark and code are available at https://github.com/MinneFeng/Plan-Bench.

Game2Real-Bench: Do Toy Games Reveal and Transfer to Real-World Visual Reasoning?

Abstract
Visual reasoning is an important capability for vision-language models (VLMs) to understand visual evidence, spatial structure, constraints, and state changes. Existing evaluations mainly rely on real-world data or procedurally generated tasks. Real-world data preserve natural complexity, while procedural games provide controllable generation and verifiable solutions. However, it remains unclear whether abilities measured in toy games correspond to real-world visual reasoning and whether game examples can support transfer. We introduce Game2Real-Bench, a benchmark that studies these connections across four capability groups, including fine-grained perception, spatial reasoning, constraint reasoning, and sequential planning. We construct 1,000 procedurally generated game samples with executable verification and curate 400 real-world samples aligned by shared reasoning requirements rather than visual similarity. We evaluate 25 representative VLMs and observe strong game-to-real consistency, with Pearson and Spearman correlations of 0.935. We further study one-shot game-to-real transfer on four models. Constraint reasoning improves for several models, while fine-grained perception declines with game demonstrations. We also construct Game2Real-Dataset with nearly 24K verified game samples across 18 tasks and 65 QA families. Our benchmark, dataset, and code are available at https://github.com/QingAnLiu/Game2Real-Bench.

Abstract
With the rapid development of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), evaluating their video understanding capabilities has become increasingly important. Existing video understanding benchmarks cover only part of the evaluation dimensions, limiting systematic diagnosis of omni-modal video understanding. We introduce OmniCompass, a holistic benchmark for omni-modal video grounding and understanding. OmniCompass defines two evaluation tracks: OmniCompass-VL assesses vision-language models on tasks requiring visual evidence, while OmniCompass-AV assesses omni-modal models on tasks requiring joint audio-visual evidence. Each track includes three complementary tasks: temporal grounding (TG), spatial grounding (SG), and question answering (QA), assessing when a referenced target appears, where it is, and what happens. QA covers five perception and three reasoning categories in OmniCompass-VL, and three perception and two reasoning categories in OmniCompass-AV. OmniCompass-VL contains 820 videos and 2,460 instances across domains and three duration types: short, medium, and long. A narrative- and audio-rich subset forms OmniCompass-AV with 240 videos and 720 instances. Each instance is evaluated under two paired settings: text-only and text+image, specifying the same target through text and visual-language guidance. All instances are manually annotated with multi-round quality verification. Evaluations of 50 MLLMs show that open-source models still lag behind proprietary counterparts, particularly in audio-visual understanding. Precise temporal and spatial grounding remains challenging even for leading models, and their predictions still exhibit inconsistencies across the paired target specifications.

Trust Before Autonomy: Rethinking Agentic Remote Sensing for Crisis Response
Authors: Georgios Angelidis, Aggelos Psiris, Spyridon Evangelatos, Thomas Lagkas, Panagiotis Sarigiannidis, Iraklis Varlamis, Georgios Th. Papadopoulos
First: 2026-10-13T17:39:23+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:39:23+00:00

Abstract
Agentic remote sensing is evaluated on how many questions it can answer, whereas crisis response depends on whether its answers can be relied on and how much of the decision can safely be handed to the agent. This paper argues that \emph{autonomy should be earned, not assumed}: An Earth-Observation (EO) agent should be granted autonomy in proportion to demonstrated reliability, epistemic honesty and verifiability. The position is supported by an evidential meta-analysis of twelve agentic RS/EO/GIS benchmark studies from peer-reviewed venues. Only one repeats runs, three contain unanswerable tasks and none measures calibration. Their own tables show that completed workflows are often not correct (task success exceeds tool-call correctness by up to 54pp on identical queries), that the gap to an expert trajectory widens overall with trajectory length (from 7 to 56pp), and that capability does not predict abstention (Spearman r from -0.25 to 0.50, all n.s.). The paper then contributes three EO-specific trust properties, an autonomy ladder for crisis EO tasks, and a readiness protocol that replays archived emergency activations before any live use. In this context, current evidence justifies assistive roles only.

Abstract
We introduce LoopVL to study whether Loop Transformers can be effectively extended to vision- language models. LoopVL combines Module-Loop and Model-Loop computation to iteratively update a unified vision-language state through shared modules. We train LoopVL from scratch through language pre-training, multimodal training, and post-training. LoopVL outperforms a range of similarly sized and larger non-recurrent models on multimodal understanding and visual reasoning benchmarks. We also observe Visual Aha Moments in LoopVL, characterized by pronounced shifts in visual attention across loops. LoopVL provides practical evidence for recurrent vision-language modeling and offers an intuitive perspective on how shared parameters can support deeper multimodal computation over continuously evolving visual-language states.

Abstract
Vision-language model (VLM) agents can control robots through visual feedback and action primitives, but repeated LLM calls and redundant observations incur substantial token overhead. We introduce PyRUA-Lean, an interactive code-execution framework in which the agent composes robot primitives, including vision-language-action (VLA) policies, into Python cells that check and retry locally and return only the images and state they request. SP$^2$ec: Adaptive Self-Speculative Decoding for Vision-Language Models
Authors: Yuqi Huang, Xingyao Li, Yunlong Hou, Fengzhuo Zhang, Jiachun Pan, Vincent Y. F. Tan

Abstract
Speculative Decoding (SD) has demonstrated its efficacy in accelerating large language model inference, but for Vision-Language Models (VLMs), the diverse input modalities make it difficult for a small trained drafter to achieve consistently high throughput. Self-speculative decoding reuses parts of the target model as a drafter which retains the multi-modal capability of the VLMs, yet its efficiency depends on choosing a suitable draft configuration for each prompt. We propose Single-Peak SPeculative decoding (SP$^2$ec), a training-free bandit algorithm that adapts the draft configuration during generation. SP$^2$ec exploits the single-peak throughput pattern observed in existing self-speculative methods, including SWIFT, KnapSpec, SpecVLM, and our simple cosSim. Our algorithmic design explicitly minimizes a novel notion of wall-time regret for each given query, corresponding to the empirical wall-time latency. Theoretical guarantees on the wall-time regret show that SP$^2$ec can exploit the optimal draft configuration, with negligible exploration cost. Extensive experiments on Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct and LLaVA-NeXT-Mistral-7B across visual and textual tasks show consistent improvements of up to 11\% over the original self-speculative methods, demonstrating the wide applicability of the proposed SP$^2$ec algorithm.

Segment Where You Look: Attention as the Interface Between Language Models and Segmenters
Authors: Eslam Abdelrahman, Hamza Omar Alkaf, Yuheng Li, Siddharth Srinivasan Iyer, Nanxuan Zhao, Eli Shechtman, Krishna Kumar Singh, Mohamed Elhoseiny
First: 2026-10-13T17:06:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T17:06:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) that segment from language must pass the location of the referent to a pixel decoder. The dominant interface compresses it into the hidden state of a single [SEG] token, a d-dimensional vector with no spatial index, and leaves the decoder, which never sees the expression, to recover the referent's extent. We show that the VLM already computes a better signal: the [SEG] token's attention over the image tokens, which is defined on the patch grid by construction. Probing a frozen segmentation VLM, we find that this attention yields 18 to 22 more gIoU than the [SEG] hidden state when read by the same probe, that it gains nothing from the image where the hidden state does, and that it is concentrated in the late layers. Building on these findings, we introduce AttnSeg, which replaces the [SEG] embedding with this attention as the decoder's prompt. A learned, signed combination of the attention heads forms a dense spatial prompt, and an attention-guidance objective supervises it directly. A two-stage recipe first trains the VLM to localize without the decoder, then trains the decoder to refine, within the budget of standard fine-tuning. Applied to Sa2VA, AttnSeg improves ReasonSeg by 6.4 gIoU and GS-Eval by 10.2 at 4B (4.2 and 9.6 over the same model fine-tuned on the same data), and by 8.8 and 11.3 at 14B. The gains reach 13 to 18 gIoU on object parts, multiple objects, and single objects, where the referent must be singled out, and are negligible on stuff regions, which the decoder already recovers unaided. Code and models are available at https://eslambakr.github.io/attseg_project_page/

GeoReader: Learning Latent Geometry for Spatial Reasoning in Vision-Language Models

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) struggle with metric and multi-view relationships that remain implicit in RGB observations. Geometry foundation models recover this structure from RGB, but existing methods either fuse the estimated features with visual tokens and learn to use them from question answering alone, or align VLM latents with geometry features. Capturing geometry, however, does not ensure its use: zeroing the aligned latents of a released feature-alignment model leaves its accuracy unchanged. We introduce GeoReader, a framework that learns geometric representations for the VLM that reads them. First, we train lightweight Geometric Readers to produce continuous input tokens, supervising them with geometry descriptions decoded by a frozen VLM, so that the Readers express geometry in a form the target model can already read. We then freeze the Readers to preserve this interface while fine-tuning the VLM on spatial QA, with geometry injection conditioned on the question. Geometry descriptions serve only as training targets: at inference, the VLM answers directly from the continuous geometric tokens without generating a geometry chain of thought. With geometry predicted from RGB images, our approach reaches 94.3 on MindCube-Tiny with Qwen3-VL-4B and, with a multi-source QA mixture, an average of 72.5 across six additional spatial benchmarks with Qwen3.5-9B. These results suggest that a useful geometric representation is one the receiving model can use, not merely one from which geometry can be reconstructed.

Synthetic CT as a First Safeguard for Choosing Medical VLMs in Lung Cancer Screening
Authors: Fakrul Islam Tushar, Geoffrey D. Rubin

Abstract
Many medical vision--language models (VLMs) are publicly available, but a site adopting one for a new imaging task rarely has the labeled real data needed to compare them. Because synthetic CT has ground truth by construction, a site could instead rank candidate models on synthetic scans, if the ranking carries over to real data. We study when it does for lung CT, using five public VLMs and three routine screening tasks, namely detecting a nodule, locating its lobe, and estimating its size, on 85,021 synthetic and 26,085 real slices from seven datasets. For nodule presence and size, the synthetic ranking matches the real one with a rank correlation of 0.98. For lobe, the ranking is only moderately preserved, with a rank correlation of 0.80. We also propose TrialCouncil, a training-free method that uses synthetic accuracy to select one model, combine several, or defer uncertain cases. On real CT, it outperforms standard voting methods in every lobe and size setting and outperforms the best synthetic model on lobe, while selecting the best synthetic model works better for presence. Synthetic CT can therefore serve as a first safeguard when choosing a medical VLM without labeled real data.

DPOison-VL: Clean Preference Poisoning in Vision-Language Direct Preference Optimization
Authors: Chengtao Jian, Kai Yang
First: 2026-10-13T15:58:58+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T15:58:58+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Direct Preference Optimization (DPO) has become a widely adopted approach for aligning vision-language models (VLMs), yet the security of multimodal preference data remains largely unexplored. Existing preference poisoning attacks either corrupt explicit supervision signals or require visible triggers, making them detectable under human inspection. We introduce DPOison-VL, the first clean preference poisoning attack against VLM-DPO, which manipulates model alignment solely through imperceptible perturbations on a small fraction of training images, while leaving all prompts, responses, and preference annotations semantically intact. We formulate poison construction as a bilevel optimization and derive a gradient alignment surrogate that efficiently steers image perturbations toward the attacker-specified preference. We further introduce a preference displacement score for principled poison selection with provable first-order optimality under a fixed poison budget, and derive a sufficient condition for preference inversion. Extensive experiments across diverse attack tasks, different VLM architectures and scales, and baseline comparisons demonstrate that DPOison-VL achieves up to 98\% ASR with only 2\% poisoned data and exhibits cross-model transferability, while largely preserving clean accuracy and general utility. Our findings reveal a critical vulnerability in VLM-DPO, where imperceptible image perturbations in preference training data can silently redirect alignment.

Masked Diffusion Vision-Language Models for Temporal Action Localization
Authors: Fengshun Wang, Zhengbo Zhang, Zhigang Tu
First: 2026-05-28T12:39:04+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-13T14:50:28+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Temporal action localization (TAL) requires recognizing the target event and localizing its start and end times precisely in untrimmed videos. Recent vision-language formulations improve semantic reasoning and support language-conditioned outputs, but their autoregressive decoders still generate tokens from left to right, preventing later semantic evidence from revising earlier timestamp predictions. We adapt masked diffusion vision-language models (MDVLMs) to TAL so that semantic tokens and boundary tokens remain editable throughout iterative denoising with bidirectional attention, allowing temporal boundaries and semantic content to be refined jointly. Direct adaptation, however, creates two TAL-specific mismatches: standard masked diffusion training corrupts all positions uniformly at random, but the time tokens are more reliable when enough semantic context is available; and token-level cross-entropy does not reflect temporal IoU. To address these mismatches, we introduce a Planned Training Objective that uses boundary-aware masking and step-weighted reconstruction to rehearse the late recovery of time tokens, together with a Step-Level IoU Reward that provides overlap-aware supervision during denoising. A standard sequence-level cross-entropy term provides the base reconstruction signal. Experiments on ActivityNet-RTL, ActivityNet-1.3, and THUMOS-14 show that MDVLM-TAL improves both temporal reasoning and boundary localization over autoregressive vision-language baselines, with especially strong gains under stricter temporal IoU criteria. The source code is available at \link{https://github.com/ycwfs/MDVLM-TAL}.

Abstract
Large vision-language models (LVLMs) have shown strong potential for embodied vision-language navigation (VLN), yet their performance degrades on long-horizon trajectories due to context-length limits, redundant historical observations, and unstable spatial alignment across time. These issues make it difficult to both compress long navigation histories and reliably reuse past spatial experience. To address these limitations, we propose \textbf{MemoryVLN}, a unified memory-driven navigation system built around memory construction and management. During memory construction, \emph{Long Sequence Compression} employs forgetting-curve-inspired temporal sampling to preserve dense recent evidence and sparse yet representative distant observations under a limited token budget. \emph{Ego-Aware Memory Representation} further integrates 2D semantic tokens, 3D geometric tokens, and 6D agent poses, allowing each memory to encode both what was observed and where it was observed. During memory management, \emph{Hierarchical Memory Bank} maintains in-episode and context-level memories through cross-context warm-start and confidence-controlled write-back. \emph{Memory Enhancement} then retrieves the Top-$K$ relevant memories according to the current navigation state and fuses them with the current visual tokens. Together, these mechanisms transform raw navigation history into compact, spatially grounded, selectively retrievable, and reusable navigation experience. Extensive experiments on R2R-CE and RxR-CE demonstrate consistent improvements over the state-of-the-art baselines by a substantial margin (i.e., 2\% and 6.5\% in successful rate).

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) show promise in drafting radiology reports, yet they frequently suffer from logical inconsistencies, generating diagnostic impressions unsupported by their own perceptual findings or missing logically entailed conclusions. Standard lexical metrics heavily penalize clinical paraphrasing and fail to capture these deductive failures in reference-free settings. Toward guarantees for clinical reasoning, we introduce a neurosymbolic verification framework that deterministically audits the internal consistency of VLM-generated reports. Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have shown strong potential for remote sensing understanding, yet reliable reasoning remains challenging when tasks require fine-grained visual grounding and multi-step inference. Existing approaches typically rely on either costly pre-constructed reasoning trajectories or self-generated signals such as response consistency, which can reinforce shared errors and provide unreliable supervision. We introduce \textbf{Reasoning--Diagnosis Self-Evolution (ReDiS)}, a framework that separates reasoning exploration from candidate evaluation while allowing the two processes to evolve jointly. ReDiS instantiates two role-specific experts from a shared multimodal backbone: a \textbf{Reasoner} that explores diverse visual evidence--answer candidates and a \textbf{Diagnoser} that jointly evaluates the complete candidate set and provides credibility-aware feedback. As the Reasoner evolves, it exposes new candidate distributions and failure modes; the Diagnoser adapts to these changes and, in turn, reshapes the learning signal for subsequent exploration. Experiments on LHRS-Bench, MME-RS, and CHOICE show consistent improvements over Qwen3.5 backbones at 0.8B, 2B, and 4B scales. ReDiS improves category-averaged accuracy by 6.47 percentage points at 0.8B, while ReDiS-4B achieves the highest average performance among the compared remote sensing MLLMs.

Abstract
Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have demonstrated promising capabilities for remote sensing reasoning, yet they frequently generate visually unsupported reasoning that contradicts the visual evidence. Existing post-training methods optimize primarily for final-answer correctness, providing limited supervision for identifying and correcting hallucinations that arise during the reasoning process. To address this limitation, we propose \textbf{FaithRS}, a hallucination-aware self-reflection framework for faithful reasoning in remote sensing. We first construct \textbf{SFHD-43K}, a dataset of 43K structured, fine-grained hallucination annotations, and train a detector to identify hallucination types, underlying causes, and supporting visual evidence. Training is driven by a composite reward that jointly optimizes answer accuracy, reasoning faithfulness, hallucination reduction, format compliance, and revision safety. Experiments with Qwen3.5 backbones (0.8B, 4B, and 9B) show consistent improvements across four remote sensing benchmarks, with gains of up to 11.64 points.

Abstract
Practical remote sensing applications often require both understanding and generation capabilities for the same geographic scene. However, most existing methods address either understanding or generation in isolation, while existing unified remote sensing models' generative ability is limited to text-to-image. Abstract
