SUREON: A Benchmark and Vision-Language Model for Surgical Reasoning
Authors: Alejandra Perez, Anita Rau, Busisiwe Mlambo, Lee White, Aina Brunet, Juan Verde, Juan Caicedo, Joël L. Lavanchy, Adam Schmidt, Chinedu Nwoye, Omid Mohareri, Muhammad Abdullah Jamal
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-03-06T18:58:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:58:13+00:00
Comments: WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
A capable surgical AI model should reason from visual evidence; yet, many existing surgical benchmarks can be partially solved without visual input, as models may exploit procedural priors or common anatomy-instrument interactions to infer answers from language alone. To address this, we introduce SUREON, a video question-answering (QA) dataset and benchmark designed to evaluate visually grounded surgical reasoning. Our pipeline generates QA pairs from narrated surgical academic videos, refines incorrect options using multiple models to reduce linguistic biases, and uses trained adversarial guessers to filter out questions answerable without visual evidence. The resulting dataset contains 76K training pairs covering 168 procedures and 10 specialties, alongside a benchmark of 340 questions across 10 categories verified by expert surgeons. Benchmarking leading commercial and open-source models shows that surgical visual reasoning remains largely unsolved--no model exceeds 53% accuracy and even expert surgeons reach only 64.9%. We also introduce SUREON-VLM, a baseline trained on our curated data that avoids language shortcuts, outperforms existing open-source models on SUREON, and matches larger specialist models on multiple standard surgical benchmarks. Our training ablations show that distractor refinement and blind curation strengthen visual grounding, increasing the multimodal gain from 8.7 to 29.1 percentage points while reducing the training set from 176.6K to 76K filtered samples. SUREON thus offers both a verified testbed for surgical visual reasoning and training data that strengthens visual grounding.

VisualSphinx: Large-Scale Synthetic Vision Logic Puzzles for RL
Authors: Yichen Feng, Zhangchen Xu, Fengqing Jiang, Yuetai Li, Bhaskar Ramasubramanian, Luyao Niu, Bill Yuchen Lin, Radha Poovendran
First: 2025-05-29T20:08:36+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T17:01:52+00:00
Comments: DMLR; Project page at https://visualsphinx.github.io/
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Project1
Abstract
Vision language models (VLMs) are expected to perform effective multimodal reasoning and make logically coherent decisions, which is critical to tasks such as diagram understanding and spatial problem solving. However, current VLM reasoning lacks large-scale and well-structured training datasets. To bridge this gap, we propose VisualSphinx, a first-of-its-kind large-scale synthetic visual logical reasoning training dataset. To tackle the challenge of image synthesis with grounded answers, we propose a rule-to-image synthesis pipeline that extracts and expands puzzle rules from seed questions and generates executable rendering programs for puzzle assembly. Using standard GRPO with binary correctness rewards as a validation recipe, experiments show that VLMs trained on VisualSphinx benefit from the logical coherence and readability of our dataset and exhibit improved performance on logical reasoning tasks. The enhanced reasoning capabilities developed from VisualSphinx also benefit other reasoning tasks such as algebraic reasoning, arithmetic reasoning and geometry reasoning.

Latent-Lens: Visual Perception in Small Language Models Through Latent Communication
Authors: Mo Shahdloo, Faegheh Sardari, Mathew Salvaris
Venue: NeurIPS 2026
First: 2026-10-30T13:53:44+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T13:53:44+00:00
Comments: Published at NeurIPS 2026
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Bridging independent vision-language models (VLMs) and small language models (SLMs) in a modular multi-agent system typically forces them to exchange information as discrete text, which creates a lossy bottleneck for spatial and fine-grained details. This constraint is especially acute for SLMs, whose limited capacity makes them more reliant on the quality of the input signal. Recent latent-communication methods exchange states between language models. How a VLM can pass visual information to a separately pretrained text-only SLM though remains unexplored. We propose \textbf{Latent-Lens}, a lightweight codec that bridges the representation gap between a frozen VLM and a frozen SLM directly in latent space, enabling them to communicate purely through hidden states. The codec consists of a domain encoder that projects hidden states from the VLM into the SLM's embedding space, and an attention decoder formalised as a LoRA that teaches the SLM to attend to these visual prefixes, while training only ${\sim}1.6$M parameters (0.2\% of the combined model size). On visual question answering (CLEVR, GQA), Latent-Lens achieves $81.0\pm7.9$\% and $65.4\pm5.4$\%, compared with $58.3\pm5.7$\% and $61.8\pm1.0$\% for text communication with the same LoRA budget (+30.2--69.8pp relative to frozen text communication), while on image captioning (Flickr8k) it improves over the same baseline by +16.5 (+18.8 frozen). We also evaluate Latent-Lens with TinyLlama-1.1B and InternVL2-1B, which use different architectures, vocabularies, and hidden dimensions.

SAR-TS-BERT: Physics-Guided Self-Supervised Pre-Training With Polarimetric Features for SAR Time-Series Crop Classification
Authors: Jieqiu Wang, Yuhan Zhang, Bo Zheng, Shaoming Zhang
First: 2026-10-30T13:37:27+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T13:37:27+00:00
Comments: 18 pages, 13 figures, 18 tables. Jieqiu Wang and Yuhan Zhang contributed equally to this work
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Multitemporal synthetic aperture radar (SAR) imagery provides continuous, cloud-free observations of crop phenology for agricultural monitoring in cloudy and rainy regions, yet high-accuracy classification typically requires extensive labeled samples. Although self-supervised pre-training mitigates label dependency in remote sensing, existing frameworks primarily target optical imagery or generic spatial SAR scenes, underutilizing temporal backscatter dynamics and polarimetric scattering mechanisms. We propose SAR-TS-BERT, a physics-guided BERT-style self-supervised pre-training framework for SAR time-series crop classification. SAR-TS-BERT integrates backscatter intensities and polarimetric ratio features (VV, VH, and VV/VH) with dual-polarimetric Cloude-Pottier decomposition parameters ($H/α/A$) into a six-band input representation through two core designs: 1) a physics-guided span masking strategy (SpanPhysics) that adaptively selects contiguous masked spans based on temporal backscatter change rates to prioritize key phenological transitions; and 2) a dual-branch cross-modal fusion encoder that models backscatter and polarimetric decomposition features through separate Transformer branches followed by bidirectional cross-attention fusion. On independent test sets in Estonia (EuroCropsML) and the USA (California CDL), SAR-TS-BERT achieves overall accuracies (OAs) of 96.78% and 87.29%, respectively, and attains a mean monthly OA of 99.37% on Chongming Island, Shanghai. On the Estonia benchmark, pre-training improves OA and Macro F1 by 1.17 and 1.59 percentage points over training from scratch under full supervision, and by 2.58 and 5.19 percentage points using only 1% labeled data. Incorporating SAR scattering physics into temporal self-supervised pre-training effectively enhances crop classification accuracy, parcel-level mapping consistency, and few-shot transferability.

When Depth Lies: Benchmarking Vision-Language Models on Mirror-Induced RGB-D Ambiguity
Authors: Hao Yin, Tianchen Guo, Heming Du, Yan Ke, Yanbin Liu, Xin Yu

Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) have made significant progress in spatial reasoning, particularly by integrating RGB images with depth data, which is often assumed to faithfully reflect physical distances. However, mirrors violate this assumption: they distort depth readings while reproducing object appearances, making reflections difficult to distinguish from real objects. As existing benchmarks rarely consider such reflective scenarios, VLM performance in mirror scenes remains largely unexplored. In this work, we introduce a mirror-centric RGB-D dataset from reflective scenes to evaluate the spatial reasoning capabilities of state-of-the-art VLMs in these scenarios. Specifically, we collect 1,011 RGB-D images from 525 real-world scenes, spanning diverse indoor and outdoor environments, six reflective surface types, and both daytime and nighttime conditions. All images are annotated with reflective surface region masks and mask annotations for both directly observed and mirror-reflected objects. Then, we curate a benchmark named MIRA-Bench (Mirror-Induced Reasoning Ambiguity Benchmark), comprising 14,140 QA pairs across eight sub-tasks organized into three cognitive levels: Reflection-aware Perception, Spatial Reasoning, and Scene-level Decision-making. We evaluate state-of-the-art VLMs on MIRA-Bench and find consistent failures across tasks: models misclassify whether objects are directly observed or reflected, fail to match reflected instances to their real-world counterparts, and produce erroneous spatial inferences. Remarkably, providing depth data actually degrades reasoning performance, implying that VLMs cannot identify incorrect depth in mirror scenes. The code and data are available at https://github.com/EvaYin-cloud/MIRA-Bench

GRIT: Gradient Masking for Visually Misgrounded Tokens in Text-Rich Reasoning
Authors: Chanseul Cho, Noori Bae, Dongheon Lee, Youngmin Ro
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-30T12:05:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T12:05:54+00:00
Comments: Accepted to WACV 2027
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has recently been extended to vision-language models (VLMs), yet its effectiveness remains limited for text-rich reasoning tasks. We highlight a key factor contributing to this limitation: models frequently arrive at correct final answers despite misperceiving visual information during intermediate steps. We define this phenomenon as Correct-but-Visually-Misgrounded Reasoning (CVMR). Because standard RLVR rewards only final-answer correctness, it fails to differentiate between properly grounded and misgrounded reasoning, reinforcing both equally. Through token-level analysis, we reveal that CVMR tokens exhibit distinct attention patterns that differ systematically from well-grounded ones. Based on this observation, we propose GRIT, a training framework that incorporates visual grounding into RLVR to mitigate the impact of CVMR. Furthermore, to address the lack of text-rich reasoning benchmarks that allow for systematic evaluation across text length and reasoning depth, we introduce GRIT-Bench. Experiments demonstrate that GRIT significantly improves both reasoning accuracy and grounding fidelity over existing RLVR methods.
Summary / 总结
Reinforcement learning with verifiable rewards (RLVR) has recently been extended to vision-language models (VLMs), yet its effectiveness remains limited for text-rich reasoning tasks.
Spectral Haar Binarizer: Compact Boolean Contrast Representations for Hyperspectral Classification
Authors: Michał Romaszewski, Przemysław Głomb
First: 2026-10-30T09:31:11+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T09:31:11+00:00

Abstract
Tsetlin Machines offer an interpretable, hardware-friendly alternative to conventional machine learning models, but applying them to continuous hyperspectral measurements requires converting spectral signals into Boolean representations. Standard binarisation methods typically discretise each spectral band independently, potentially discarding important relationships between neighbouring wavelengths. In this paper, we introduce the Spectral Haar Binarizer (SHB), a contrast-based spectral representation method that constructs Boolean predicates from differences between selected spectral intervals. Inspired by Haar-like contrast features, the method uses an AdaBoost-style selection procedure to obtain a compact set of discriminative spectral predicates. We evaluate SHB on five heterogeneous hyperspectral datasets covering forensic blood detection, agricultural and urban remote sensing, and rainforest tree-species classification. Across binary and multiclass tasks, SHB consistently outperforms standard per-band binarisation strategies and provides the strongest or near-strongest results across classifiers. In binary tasks, TM-SHB achieves the best average performance, while RF-SHB is strongest in multiclass settings. Bayesian and rank-based analyses confirm that SHB's advantage is robust across datasets and not driven by isolated cases. The results show that representing continuous hyperspectral measurements is a central processing step for efficient classification. That contrast-based Boolean features provide an effective bridge between information-rich sensor measurements and compact, logic-based processing.

Parse Before You Execute: Efficient Spatial Reasoning with Executable Primitives
Authors: Xiwei Liu, Yulong Li, Xinling Zhuang, Xuhui Li, Yichen Li, Haolin Yang, Zhixiang Lu, Yutong Xie
First: 2026-10-30T07:59:08+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T07:59:08+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Spatial reasoning in Vision-Language Models (VLMs) has increasingly benefited from tool-integrated pipelines that ground computation in explicit geometry. However, existing systems often compile queries into relatively open-ended artifacts, such as code, task constraints, tool plans, or gathered evidence, leaving what must be resolved before execution under-specified. This can lead to unnecessary tool acquisition and iterative refinement, while still producing plausible reasoning traces from incomplete spatial commitments. We identify this failure mode as under-committed query compilation. To address it, we propose PEPS, a training-free agentic paradigm Parsing Executable Primitives for Spatial reasoning under a typed FESM schema over Frame, Entity, State, and Metric. PEPS follows a parse-before-execute principle: the Parser commits each query to a compact set of executable requirements; the Executor selectively grounds the required primitives through fixed geometric APIs and deterministic computation; and the Verifier checks execution sufficiency, enabling early termination or targeted repair. Extensive experiments across challenging spatial reasoning benchmarks show that PEPS consistently improves reasoning accuracy while substantially reducing refinement rounds, API cost, and end-to-end latency over strong tool-integrated baselines. These results demonstrate that explicit requirement commitment can make tool-integrated spatial reasoning not only more reliable, but also more execution-efficient.

Abstract
Lightweight vision-language models perform competitively on standard benchmarks yet fail systematically in dense-scene reasoning, where multiple objects, attributes, and relations must be jointly grounded and resolved through multi-step inference. Such capability is critical for real-world applications where models must reliably interpret cluttered environments. Yet existing training signals provide no explicit grounding between reasoning steps and the underlying visual entities and relations, leaving lightweight models free to generate fluent but visually unanchored reasoning chains. To address this gap, we first introduce DRBench, a benchmark of 14,573 questions across 2,943 images, organized into five task categories spanning three progressive reasoning layers. Building on DRBench, we propose DRScaffold, a supervised fine-tuning framework that decomposes the supervision target into four causally ordered stages, promoting grounded reasoning without architectural modification. Experiments on three lightweight VLMs demonstrate substantial gains on DRBench while preserving or improving performance on general-purpose benchmarks. Notably, Qwen2.5-VL-3B trained with DRScaffold surpasses the frozen Qwen2.5-VL-32B under zero-shot direct-answer prompting on DRBench, demonstrating that structured supervision can substitute for a significant portion of model scale in dense-scene reasoning. Our code is available at https://github.com/irene-shi/DRScaffold.

Abstract
Dynamic spatial reasoning is essential for connecting visual intelligence to thephysical world, yet remains challenging for vision-language models (VLMs), which must preserve scene state while tracking how spatial relations evolve over time. Meanwhile, video generation models (VGMs), trained to synthesize temporally coherent world dynamics, offer a rich source of spatiotemporal representations. We present GenDSR, a framework that transfers intermediate representations from VGM into VLM for dynamic spatial reasoning. Rather than treating a VGM feature as a monolithic prior, GenDSR constructs two complementary branches: a raw branch preserving holistic generative context and a delta branch emphasizing temporal variation. Task-conditioned tokenwise gates then fuse both signals and inject the result into the native visual representation. On DSR-Bench, GenDSR achieves 67.2%, surpassing the previous best result and establishing a new state of the art.

Why VLMs Miss Small Objects, and When Zooming In Is Safe
Authors: Junzhe Shi, Yuan Gan, Shida Jiang
First: 2026-10-07T02:10:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T06:22:53+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Screenshots, documents, engineering drawings, shelves and maps hold many small objects of kinds that change from task to task, and vision-language models (VLMs) promise to find them from an instruction and an example. Yet they see each image through a fixed token budget and miss small objects, so every user, and every agent that calls a model, must choose whether to send an image whole, raise its resolution, split it, or zoom. We show that VLMs miss small objects for two reasons a caller can estimate before any call: an object gets too few visual tokens (S), and a call must search and report too much (L). Raising S by a factor a multiplies the tokens needed to see an image by a^2, while splitting at the same scale lowers L at a cost at most linear in the number of views. Under two monotonicity assumptions, splitting cannot lower expected recall if no view zooms out and views overlap by one object, and neither part can be dropped; every such split pays a minimum token cost, and a simple rule pays it up to an explicit factor. Better models need less S, but an image can be arbitrarily large while a call's budget is fixed, so splitting does not become obsolete, for zoom agents either. In about 187,000 requests to 12 VLMs, up to GPT-6.1, Claude Opus 5.5 and Gemini 4, on controlled images 24 of 40 predicted orderings hold significantly and none is violated; splits that never zoom out raised recall on average over models wherever S was below 1.5, and splits that zoom out lowered it in 19 of 21 model and data settings. The rule raised recall significantly in five of ten settings, by up to 0.28 (from 0.62 to 0.90), and precision and F1 in six, at a token cost known before any call.

Authors: Zichuan Fu, Shirong Wang, Wenlin Zhang, Guojing Li, Yimin Deng, Jingtong Gao, Junjia Qi, Hanyu Yan, Yefeng Zheng, Xiaopeng Li, Wanyu Wang, Xian Wu, Xiangyu Zhao

Abstract
GUI grounding maps natural-language instructions to click locations and is essential for reliable GUI agents. The task remains difficult on high-resolution, densely populated interfaces because a vision-language model (VLM) may recognize a requested control without locating it precisely enough for interaction. Most existing methods provide various forms of localization assistance, but still rely on a direct click prediction, allowing visual ambiguity or an inaccurate initial estimate to propagate to the final result. In this paper, we introduce GUI-Lens, a coarse-to-fine grounding framework that allows a general-purpose VLM to determine the target through active visual observations. Specifically, GUI-Lens extracts OCR text and detected UI components from the screenshot and presents their positions as coordinate references. Using the instruction, the current view, and these references, the VLM selects the region and scale of the next view, which is cropped and enlarged to provide finer visual details. This process continues over successively focused views until the target is determined. Proposed crops and clicks are checked against the instruction throughout the process, and the final local position is mapped back to the original screen coordinates. Experiments on five GUI grounding benchmarks and six general-purpose base models show that GUI-Lens consistently improves grounding accuracy, achieving up to 9.3 times the accuracy of direct grounding on ScreenSpot-Pro.

Abstract
Robot agents built on vision-language models depend on both the planner and its harness: the tools, feedback, memory, and recovery logic around it. Can improvements to a large teacher's harness be carried into a compact agent with lower inference compute cost? We present TinyAstral, a two-stage approach. We first freeze an open-weight teacher and improve its harness through recursive self-improvement (RSI): frontier LLM agents read the teacher's trajectories and propose harness edits, and each edit is accepted only if it passes a paired statistical test. On RoboTwin, harness evolution raises the teacher's success from 33.4% to 63.6%; the final 4B student reaches 62.8%. The harness gains also transfer to other frozen planners without retraining. On identical episodes, the final 4B student outperforms open 4B agents, the open 27B harness-RSI model SenseNova-RoboRSI, and our own teacher model placed in three competitors' harnesses. It stays close to its teacher on all three training benchmarks, and on three benchmarks never used for RSI or training it scores close to a 552B planner running the same harness. On the measured deployment episodes, it uses about one fifth of its teacher's accelerator time.

On the Faithfulness of Visual Thinking: Measurement and Enhancement
Authors: Zujing Liu, Junwen Pan, Qi She, Yuan Gao, Guisong Xia
Venue: NeurIPS 2026 Poster
First: 2025-10-27T16:15:54+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-30T03:20:12+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Recent large vision-language models (LVLMs) can generate vision-text multimodal chain-of-thought (MCoT) traces after reinforcement fine-tuning (RFT). However, we observe that the visual information in MCoT is often inaccurate even when answers are correct, and task accuracy remains nearly unchanged despite the visual information being corrupted. This indicates a lack of faithfulness in the vision part of MCoT reasoning. We attribute this to the RL reward design in RFT, which solely incentivizes the format of interleaved vision-text cues, encouraging the model to incorporate visual information into its text reasoning steps without considering its correctness. In this paper, we first probe MCoT faithfulness by measuring how much the prediction changes when its visual and textual thoughts are intervened. Surprisingly, the model's predictions remain nearly unchanged under visual intervention but change significantly under textual intervention, indicating that the visual evidence is largely ignored. To further analyze the visual information, we introduce an automated LVLM-based evaluation metric that quantifies the faithfulness of visual cues from two perspectives: relevance and sufficiency. Our evaluation reveals that the visual information in current MCoT traces is simultaneously irrelevant and insufficient. To address this issue, we propose Sufficient-Component Cause Model (SCCM) learning. This approach encourages the MCoT to generate sufficient yet minimal visual components that are independently capable of leading to the correct answer. The proposed SCCM is annotation-free and plug-and-play, compatible with various RFT for MCoT. Empirical results demonstrate that SCCM substantially improves the visual faithfulness across a suite of fine-grained perception and reasoning benchmarks. Code is available at https://github.com/EugeneLiu01/Faithful_Thinking_with_Image.
