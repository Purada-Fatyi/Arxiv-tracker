RADIOlogy: A Single-Pass Approach to Training-Free Zero-Shot Anomaly Detection
Authors: Joaquín Figueira, Giacomo D'Amicantonio, Egor Bondarau
Venue: WACV 2027
First: 2026-10-29T17:45:35+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:45:35+00:00
Comments: Accepted to WACV 2027. Code available at https://github.com/jacomof/RADIOlogy
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
Current zero-shot anomaly detection methods often rely on complex prompt-learning techniques or computationally expensive sliding-window strategies to achieve fine-grained visual alignment to detect small-scale anomalies. This reliance stems from the fundamental limitation of standard CLIP architectures, where [CLS] token attention and spatial patch pooling struggle to capture subtle, localized anomaly features. While prompt-learning approaches aim to bridge this gap, they typically require a large number of ground-truth segmentation masks from auxiliary datasets, which are often unavailable in specialized domains. In this work, we show an emerging property of multi-head attention pooling in vision-language models: remarkable zero-shot anomaly localization and detection right out of the box. We translate this property into a single-pass anomaly detection technique, RADIOlogy, that does not require dense, windowed execution and significantly outperforms other training-free baselines. Instead, the method relies on a simple contrastive scoring approach that compares the average similarity between vision features and a suite of normal and abnormal prompts. Furthermore, we leverage the agglomerative features of RADIO models to enhance pixel-level anomaly segmentation using a dense anomaly refinement pipeline. Finally, we show how the vision-language family of training-free zero-shot anomaly detection techniques can be applied successfully to the medical domain with minimal modifications. Code is available at https://github.com/jacomof/RADIOlogy.

VeriCUA: Verification as Agentic Exploration for Computer-Using Agents
Authors: Qiushi Sun, Da Yu, Alexander Knop, Amer Sinha, Lingpeng Kong, Ben Kao, Ravi Kumar, Pritish Kamath
First: 2026-10-29T17:45:26+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T17:45:26+00:00
Comments: Work in progress
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Building and scaling computer-using agents (CUAs) across desktop, mobile, and web environments requires reliable task-completion verification for data curation, evaluation, and reinforcement learning. Typical automated approaches prompt vision-language models (VLMs) to judge trajectories or generate programs to inspect application state. However, both rely on partial views of complex digital environments. VLM judges are restricted to supplied observations, while generated programs depend on accessible state. These views can omit decisive evidence or leave completion judgments unsupported. Tailoring separate verifiers to different platforms and access settings adds further engineering cost. We present VeriCUA, a platform-agnostic framework for verification through agentic exploration. It treats verification as dynamic evidence gathering and assessment within a shared procedure for online and offline settings across platforms and observation modalities. After inspecting the available context to construct a task rubric, the verifier selects GUI or code checks for unresolved requirements and combines their findings under explicit evidence rules. Experiments show improved agreement with reference labels over the compared baselines across these settings, with the largest offline gains on hard cases and stronger rejection of false completion claims online. Further analyses connect these gains to complementary evidence and test-time exploration.

Textual Response-Aware Guidance for Zero-Shot Adversarial Robustness of Vision-Language Models
Authors: Yubo Cui, Xianchao Guan, Zheng Zhang
First: 2026-10-29T15:48:43+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T15:48:43+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) exhibit strong zero-shot generalization but remain vulnerable to adversarial attacks. Existing adversarial fine-tuning methods often use pre-trained model guidance to preserve image--text alignment while improving robustness. Our analysis of visual representation drift reveals complementary limitations of two representative forms of guidance. Matching visual features provides broad coverage by constraining all directions equally, but lacks semantic emphasis. Matching prediction distributions prioritizes semantically relevant directions, but remains limited by the candidate texts and prediction probabilities. These limitations can hinder preservation of pre-trained image--text relationships and degrade zero-shot performance. To address these issues, we propose Textual Response-Aware Guidance (TRAG), which constrains visual representation drift using the pre-trained text encoder's responses to local variations in input embeddings. Specifically, TRAG constructs anchor-level constraints from the response directions and magnitudes around semantic anchors, then aggregates them using pre-trained image--anchor relevance, yielding an image-adaptive constraint. Extensive experiments across architectures and tasks demonstrate state-of-the-art clean and robust performance. Further analysis shows that TRAG helps preserve pre-trained image--text relationships.

Abstract
Rapid intensification (RI) is one of the most dangerous and unpredictable phases of tropical cyclones, and its environmental and structural drivers are difficult to identify and separate in observations. Here we introduce CausalVisionRI, a causal-prior-constrained deep-learning system for 24-h RI warning in the western North Pacific. It integrates infrared remote sensing imagery, atmospheric and oceanic fields, and an observationally discovered causal graph into a unified model. The graph and eight sign-verified effects on 24-h intensity change provide an auditable causal prior for training. Discovered from 522 cyclones in 2000-2017, it identifies causal pathways linking atmospheric and oceanic conditions, the cyclone's dynamical and structural state, and its 24-h intensity change. On a forward holdout covering 2020-2024 and 3,004 records, the five-member ensemble achieves a Brier skill score (BSS) of 0.259 relative to a fixed development climatology, significantly exceeding four reference systems; the three LDA baselines reach at most 0.105. It also attains higher detection rates than a published knowledge-block scheme and a reconstructed contrastive-learning scheme at matched false-detection rates under our USA 30-kt standard and the published RITCF standard. Ablations show that the graph pathway adds 0.032 BSS across the holdout and 0.063 over 2020-2021, with a significant contribution in one ensemble member. A deep-learning model constrained by observationally discovered causal structure thus offers a new route to physically informed RI warning.

Evidence-Gated Task and Motion Planning with Vision-Language Models
Authors: Tsunehiko Tanaka, Matthew Stephenson, Alistair Macvicar, Edgar Simo-Serra
First: 2026-08-20T14:17:52+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T15:37:45+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Robots executing long-horizon manipulation tasks from natural-language instructions must reason about both semantic task structure and geometric feasibility. Hierarchical approaches combining Vision-Language Models (VLMs) with Task and Motion Planning (TAMP) offer a promising solution, but VLM-generated subgoals may rely on prior knowledge without sufficient observational support. This can lead to the omission of task-relevant subgoals or attempts to execute instructions whose requirements cannot be met in the environment. We propose Evidence Acquisition and Feasibility Gating (EAFG), a framework for evidence-gated task and motion planning that acquires visual evidence to specify appropriate task plans and assess whether given instructions can be fulfilled. EAFG first acquires evidence by having a VLM generate subgoals and a robot execute action sequences planned by TAMP for these subgoals. For feasibility gating, the acquired evidence is used to decide whether to proceed with task planning, acquire further evidence, or halt. When proceeding, the VLM generates task subgoals conditioned on the acquired evidence, and TAMP computes low-level actions. We evaluate EAFG in three cooking scenarios involving explicitly specified required objects, underspecified instructions, and instructions requiring an absent object. EAFG improves task completion over a VLM-TAMP baseline in the explicit and underspecified scenarios and enables appropriate halting when a required object is absent.

TaskFrame: Connecting Task-Level Planning with Frame-Level Control for Game Agents
Authors: Guangzhi Wang, Junfu Pu, Yiran Wang, Xingyilang Yin, Honghao Chen, Cheng Cheng, Lingen Li, Yinan Zhou, Jiarui Wu, Yinuo Jiang, Hongbo Jin, Xiaodong Cun, Ying Shan

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Building general game agents requires both high-level task reasoning and low-latency action control in rapidly changing visual environments. These capabilities favor models with substantially different computational profiles, making them difficult to realize within a single model. In this work, we present TaskFrame, a hierarchical planner-controller framework that connects task-level planning with frame-level keyboard-and-mouse control. A vision-language task planner decomposes a high-level goal into an ordered sequence of atomic instructions and is optimized through supervised learning and dependency-aware GRPO. The hidden states of special goal tokens that terminate each atomic instruction provide continuous goal embeddings that are cached and reused across control steps. A causal Transformer controller combines the embedding of the active goal with recent observations to predict discrete keyboard and mouse-button actions and continuous camera motion at every game step. We train the system in three stages: planner training, controller pretraining, and joint alignment. Experiments in Minecraft show that TaskFrame is competitive with general-purpose vision-language models on instruction decomposition, and that in closed-loop evaluation the agent achieves a higher combat success rate than the evaluated baselines.

Abstract
Vision--language models (VLMs) are tested for fine-grained understanding with image--text matching probes, a model must prefer the correct image or caption over handcrafted negative. In retrieval, the correct item must beat thousands of alternatives. We measure how these two evaluation methods relate by using two probing benchmarks, \svo{} and SugarCrepe, for retrieval tasks. For 100 image and caption queries we judge the top-10 retrievals from full dataset of six VLMs, with human annotators and an LLM assessor. We than compare how well models rank pool of items on the same queries, while changing only where the negatives comes from: the benchmark, a random draw, or the judged-incorrect retrievals of other models. On \svo{} the accuracy of retrieving a positive over handcrafted negatives is significantly higher than for retrieved ones (the correct item is preferred in 85\% against 77\% of the time). Using pool of ten retrieved negatives leads to same accuracy as when one uses pool of 200 to 430 random negatives. On SugarCrepe the order is reversed: its handcrafted negatives are harder than the retrieved ones. Incomplete relevance labels explain most of the low retrieval scores on \svo{}, where the top-ranked item is correct for 54--90\% of the queries under human labels against 6--21\% under the benchmark's labels. However, we detect no association between passing the probe for a query and retrieving a correct item first. The LLM assessor o3 agrees with individual annotators ($κ$ 0.57--0.61) slightly less than they agree with each other (0.64--0.68).

Abstract
In this paper, we propose Concentrate and Concentrate (CaC), a coarse-to-fine anomaly reward model based on Vision-Language Models. During inference, it first conducts a global temporal scan to anchor anomalous time windows, then performs fine-grained spatial grounding within the localized interval, and finally derives robust judgments via structured spatiotemporal Chain-of-Thought reasoning. To equip the model with these capabilities, we construct the first large-scale generated video anomaly dataset with per-frame bounding-box annotations, temporal anomaly windows, and fine-grained attribution labels. Building on this dataset, we design a three-stage progressive training paradigm. The model initially learns spatial and temporal anchoring through single- and multi-frame supervised fine-tuning, and then is optimized by a reinforcement learning strategy based on two-turn Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO). Beyond conventional accuracy rewards, we introduce Temporal and Spatial IoU rewards to supervise the intermediate localization process, effectively guiding the model toward more grounded and interpretable spatiotemporal reasoning. Extensive experiments demonstrate that CaC can stably concentrate on subtle anomalies, achieving a 25.7% accuracy improvement on fine-grained anomaly benchmarks and, when used as a reward signal, CaC reduces generated-video anomalies by 11.7% while improving overall video quality.

Abstract
Vegetation plays a central role in cities, serving functional roles such as balancing shade and sky exposure, shaping the microclimate, carbon sequestration, and providing acoustic screening. Furthermore, aesthetic choices in landscape styles (formal, modernist, picturesque) are important for citizens' well-being and should be considered in the design of urban landscapes. In this paper, we present a novel method for placing vegetation, ranging from shrubs to large trees, in 3D urban scenes. Our method uses individual lots as input and employs a conditioned Vision-Language Model (VLM) to generate a top-down rendering of vegetation, placing large trees, small trees, and shrubs on lawn areas. This rendering is then converted into a 3D scene by detecting individual plants and selecting appropriate 3D assets. To refine the proposed configurations, we define an optimization loop that keeps the VLM-generated positions fixed after a deterministic feasibility repair and uses CMA-ES to optimize per-instance scale transformations along with discrete vegetation asset substitutions. To define the objective function for the optimization, we evaluate the generated 3D scenes with efficient surrogate models of shade and path-based acoustic exposure. Furthermore, we render in-scene views at each optimization step to compute view-dependent perceptual descriptors, enabling the generation of both aesthetic and functional vegetation layouts for urban scenes.

Abstract
Foundation models offer a promising route to transferable remote sensing representations, but many current approaches depend on very large pretraining datasets and fixed sensor configurations, limiting their suitability for ecological and environmental applications, where observations often vary across platforms, spatial and spectral resolutions, and available modalities. Abstract
Prompt-tuning has emerged as a parameter-efficient technique for adapting large-scale vision-language models (VLMs) to downstream tasks while keeping the pre-trained weights frozen. However, prompt-tuned VLMs often suffer from a base-to-novel dual miscalibration problem, where models typically show underconfidence on base classes and overconfidence on novel classes. Current VLM calibration approaches address this dual miscalibration either separately or jointly, but lack a geometric understanding of the hyperspherical embedding space and thus fail to exploit angular discriminative properties to adjust distorted decision boundaries. To address this, we propose M-PFT, a novel angular-margin approach for prompt tuning that introduces two complementary objectives to promote class discriminability and semantic anchoring. This enlarges the angular margin by 1) minimizing the maximum cosine similarity between tunable class embeddings to reduce embedding collapse and 2) minimizing the angular distance between each tunable embedding and its corresponding zero-shot embedding to preserve semantic geometry. Extensive experiments across 9 prompt-tuning methods with various backbones on 15 diverse datasets show that our approach reduces the average calibration error on novel classes, while remaining competitive on base classes and maintaining comparable accuracy. Further analyses reveal that M-PFT effectively adjusts distorted decision boundaries induced by prompt tuning while preserving the pre-trained embedding space, improving calibration with negligible computational overhead.

Abstract
Medical diagnostic reasoning requires verifiable links between visual evidence and clinical knowledge, yet current medical vision language models are aligned with objectives that reward the wrong behavior. Uniform supervised fine tuning lets easy cases dominate updates while clinically critical boundary cases saturate prematurely. Sparse rewards based only on final-answer correctness cannot assess anatomical grounding or clinical logic, inviting reward hacking through answer shortcuts. We propose MedCBR, a two-stage alignment framework coupling boundary-aware preference optimization with rubric-grounded reinforcement learning. First, we construct counterfactual boundary preferences at the model's capability frontier using empirical pass rates and preference pairs that share reasoning prefixes but diverge at decisive diagnostic branches. Difficulty Weighted Boundary Preference Optimization (DBPO) concentrates learning on this calibrated boundary region. Second, we distill reusable clinical rubrics from boundary trajectories and activate them according to prediction entropy. Entropy Gated Grounded Policy Optimization (EGPO) rewards answer correctness, anatomical grounding, and reasoning fidelity while suppressing rubric noise on reliable samples. A reference policy anchored to the boundary-aligned model couples population-level boundary priors with instance-level uncertainty, making diagnostic reasoning more accurate and clinically grounded. Experiments on six medical benchmarks show consistent improvements over strong general, specialized, and reinforcement learning baselines, reaching 61.2% average accuracy with the largest gains on challenging differential diagnosis tasks. Ablations show super-additive gains: combining the two stages exceeds the sum of their individual improvements.

Venue: NeurIPS 2026 Spotlight
First: 2026-09-30T02:41:33+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T10:40:34+00:00

Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2 · Code3
Abstract
As vision language models are increasingly deployed in clinical diagnosis, understanding how they internally resolve competing visual and textual signals becomes a safety imperative. Existing mechanistic analyses remain confined to unimodal text and offer no explanation for why a single misleading sentence can override a correct image based diagnosis, or why a model commits to a confident answer despite insufficient visual evidence. We find that these two safety risks, arbitration failure where textual context overrides visual grounding and brake failure where the model commits without adequate evidence, are mediated by spatially disjoint attention head populations: arbitration heads form a mid-to-deep wideband reflecting cross-layer evidence competition, while brake heads concentrate in a narrow middle-to-late layer band that regulates evidence sufficiency and abstention behavior. To ground these observations in causal circuitry, we introduce CRAFT, which localizes each failure mode to a minimal causal head set via dual criteria and verifies necessity and sufficiency through temporal probes and Tuned Lens trajectory analysis. Excising arbitration heads sharply reduces conflict following with negligible degradation on clean inputs, while excising brake heads restores appropriate abstention under degraded visual evidence. The two interventions target spatially disjoint head sets and produce distinct corrective effects, underscoring the mechanistic separability of the failure modes. Experiments across multiple medical VQA benchmarks and VLM architectures validate both the localization and interventions, demonstrating that the identified heads causally drive each failure mode and that targeted modulation generalises without retraining. The code is available at https://github.com/zhcz328/CRAFT.

First: 2026-10-29T09:06:15+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T09:06:15+00:00
Comments: 23 pages, including references and appendices

Abstract
Thinking vision-language models reason for thousands of tokens, yet still miss small details in high-resolution images. Zooming methods pay for extra inference passes, and outcome rewards never tell the model where to look at. In Qwen3.5-4B, we find that the bottleneck is selection, not seeing: a few attention heads, which we call localization heads, pick the evidence out of thousands of visual tokens; ablating four of them costs 13.2 points more than ablating four random heads, and their focus fades as the reasoning grows. We therefore propose Localization-Head Alignment (LHA), which makes attention a training signal: whenever the reasoning states a visual fact, these heads learn to look at the focal region rather than the background, as an auxiliary loss in both SFT and RL training. On benchmarks of V*, HR-Bench 4K and TreeBench, LHA lifts Qwen3.5-4B from 74.1% to 76.6% accuracy, the best single-pass 4B result we evaluate, ahead of multi-pass zooming and released 7B models. Alignment alone adds 1.8 points over otherwise identical training while shortening reasoning by 20%, and the gains carry over to Qwen2.5-VL-3B.

Thinking vision-language models reason for thousands of tokens, yet still miss small details in high-resolution images.
Where Do Vision Language Models Fail at 3D Reasoning?

Abstract
Vision-language models can get individual judgments right while struggling to recover and consistently use the exact properties of a 3D object. To characterize this gap, we introduce BedBench, a controlled benchmark of thirteen procedurally generated tasks spanning individual judgments and composite answers. Across twelve open-weight and four frontier models, per-color counting exposes a pronounced weakness: humans achieve 93.3% accuracy, compared with 7.2% for the best model. Targeted fine-tuning raises one model's accuracy from 3.0% to 68.6%, but reveals a further difficulty: its answers about the most common colors remain inconsistent with its own per-color counts. Supplying those counts as text improves its judgments, showing that recovering a property does not mean using it consistently. Reasoning, scaling, and added visual cues each improve individual judgments, but none makes exact answers reliable. To locate the difficulty, we probe Qwen3.5-27B: exact per-color counts remain recoverable at the language model's input, yet the model's answers rarely recover them (65.0% for the probe vs. 3.0% for the model). Image-removal controls further show that some apparent successes require no visual evidence. These findings motivate evaluating 3D reasoning by exact correctness, consistent use, and image dependence, rather than accuracy alone. Code and dataset are publicly available: https://github.com/leduckhai/BedBench

Evaluating Cultural Knowledge Editing in Vision-Language Models
Authors: Malvina Nikandrou, Georgios Pantazopoulos, Ioannis Konstas, Alessandro Suglia

Abstract
Current Vision-Language Models (VLMs) frequently fail to recognize culture-specific concepts, especially from underrepresented regions. Effectively injecting this long-tail knowledge into existing models remains a challenge. In this work, we investigate knowledge editing as a targeted alternative by introducing CulturalBind, a benchmark comprising 3.2K images of 1K concepts from 6 domains, designed to evaluate culture-aware cross-modal knowledge as well as concept-level knowledge editing in VLMs. Pre-edit evaluation reveals that textual and visual knowledge is decoupled: models often demonstrate text-based knowledge of cultural concepts but fail to identify them from images. We then evaluate editing methods in single and sequential scenarios. Although current methods successfully correct individual visual recognition errors, they exhibit low cross-modal portability, failing to integrate edits into the model's broader parametric knowledge. Furthermore, performance degrades rapidly under sequential editing, highlighting the need for comprehensive adaptation approaches that better consolidate multimodal knowledge over time.

Abstract
Piping and Instrumentation Diagrams (P&IDs) are the authoritative maps of process plants: isolation, maintenance, and HAZOP decisions depend on what connects to what. Vision-language models describe these sheets fluently, yet they often invent or miss process connections---and an invented or missed link can reverse an isolation or reachability call, so a plant decision cannot trust a fluent answer that was never checked against the linework. We instead recover an explicit graph of the drawing---its symbols, the process connections between them, and the tags that name them---and then require the model to answer only by querying that graph through seven read-only operators, so a topology claim is returned only when it cites the query results that support it. On TopoPID-VQA, a new suite of 3000 topology questions over these sheets, Graph-Grounded Harness (Ours) raises exact match accuracy from 36.7--41.3% under image-only prompting to 74.3--76.0% for Qwen3-VL-4B, Qwen3-VL-8B, and Gemma-4-E4B. It does so on an imperfect substrate: on Digitize-PID dataset the recovered graph scores F1 0.742 on exact process connections, and 0.801 once symbols and tags are pooled in. The residual errors track that gap---grounding pays off where the recovered graph is right, and perception error still breaks topology questions where it is not.

WNM-3D: A World Navigation Model with 3D Scene Conditioning for Closed-Loop VLN
Authors: Yuehao Huang, Yunzi Wu, Xiaotao Zhang, Xinhai Li, Jiankun Dong, Sunyao Zhou, Jiajun Lv, Chi Zhang, Chenjia Bai, Yong Liu, Xuelong Li
First: 2026-08-07T14:29:00+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T07:13:26+00:00

Abstract
Recent vision-language navigation (VLN) systems increasingly adapt pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) into vision-language-action (VLA) policies that map egocentric observations and language instructions directly to navigation actions. Although semantically capable, such action-centric training does not explicitly model how the agent's visual observations evolve under its predicted motion. Generative world-action models (WAMs) jointly predict future observations and actions, yet existing WAMs for continuous VLN do not condition joint future-view and action generation on geometry-aware representations inferred from the observation history. We present WNM-3D, a generative World Navigation Model with 3D scene conditioning for continuous VLN. A frozen feed-forward geometry encoder extracts cross-view representations from monocular RGB history, and a trainable 3D Scene-to-Token Adapter converts them into a fixed-length scene prefix for the world-action Diffusion Transformer. Through block-causal attention, this prefix remains visible to every future video-action block throughout joint denoising, providing persistent geometric context. We train WNM-3D with supervised world-action learning, DAgger-style adaptation on policy-visited states, and Counterfactual DanceGRPO refinement. Across GN-Bench and VLN-CE, WNM-3D consistently outperforms the backbone-matched WNM-2D counterpart, demonstrating gains from 3D scene conditioning across distinct visual domains. WNM-3D further outperforms strong VLN baselines on GN-Bench and demonstrates closed-loop deployment on a physical robot. Component and stage-wise ablations support the Adapter design and the complementary roles of DAgger-SFT and subsequent reward-guided refinement.
Summary / 总结
Recent vision-language navigation (VLN) systems increasingly adapt pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) into vision-language-action (VLA) policies that map egocentric observations and language instructions directly to navigation actions.

Authors: Haotian Lyu, Haoyu Wang, Ru Wang, Feng Ji, Ziwen Li, Jiaxian Guo, Mingming Gong

Abstract
Label-free test-time reinforcement learning adapts vision--language models using rewards derived from their own predictions. However, misinterpreted visual evidence and sensitivity to textual formulation can produce a consistent but incorrect consensus that majority voting reinforces. Cross-view aggregation can expose such errors, but image and text transformations may themselves degrade task-relevant evidence to different extents. We introduce \method, a reliability-aware harmonic consensus framework for multimodal test-time reinforcement learning. \method constructs a $2\times2$ grid combining original and perturbed images with direct-solving and reframing modes, selects pseudo-labels through weighted harmonic aggregation, and adapts modality-level weights using batch-averaged, temporally smoothed shifts in pseudo-label confidence as a heuristic reliability signal. We formulate consensus as a weighted $χ^2$-barycenter problem whose unique minimizer is the normalized weighted harmonic aggregate, without requiring correct-answer invariance. An exact support--dispersion decomposition characterizes when harmonic aggregation preserves or corrects arithmetic rankings, while a sensitivity analysis establishes how modality reweighting changes pairwise ranking margins, informing interpretation of the heuristic updates. Across six benchmarks and three Qwen3-VL model scales, \method outperforms TTRV in 16 of 18 benchmark--model settings on adaptation instances and 17 of 18 settings on held-out instances under matched estimation and optimization rollout counts. These results demonstrate improved target-task adaptation and transfer to unseen instances without further updates.

Label-free test-time reinforcement learning adapts vision--language models using rewards derived from their own predictions.

Abstract
Predicting where to grasp and how to move is fundamentally a geometry problem. Vision-language models treat it as language generation instead. They tokenize spatial coordinates into text and decode them autoregressively. This mechanism ignores spatial structure and truncates continuous precision. Errors compound across coordinate tokens during decoding. In this paper, we show that compact off-the-shelf VLMs with 0.5-2B parameters already encode rich spatial information in their intermediate hidden states. Autoregressive decoding is the dominant bottleneck for trajectory prediction, while affordance prediction depends additionally on feature depth and decoder architecture. We expose the latent spatial information with a lightweight, geometry-compatible prediction head that attaches to a frozen backbone. It reads features from an intermediate hidden layer, conditions a learned query on the task instruction and refines it via cross-attention over the visual tokens to regress affordance boxes and trajectory waypoints directly as continuous values, without modifying the backbone. We evaluate on ShareRobot-Bench and AGD20K. Our 2B variant reduces trajectory error by over 60% and improves ShareRobot-Bench affordance mAP by 85% relative to the autoregressive baseline. It approaches RoboBrain on trajectory prediction (0.251 vs 0.191 DFD) at 3.8x higher throughput. These results demonstrate a favorable accuracy-efficiency trade-off for instruction-conditioned 2D geometric prediction.

Telling Is Not Seeing: Textual Personas Rarely Make Vision-Language Models Answer Like the People They Describe
Authors: Jiawei Chen
First: 2026-10-29T04:26:29+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T04:26:29+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Vision-language models (VLMs) are increasingly prompted with personas, such as "You have low vision", to stand in for people in user studies. We ask whether a persona makes a model see like the person it describes. We compare persona answers with measured people with low vision, color blindness or hearing loss, and with simple rules that use only the facts the persona states. The persona never beats these rules, and it erases the real differences between groups and individuals. Describing blur leaves recognition unchanged in all 14 models we test, while blurring the photo lowers it as it does for people. Inside open models, the description changes neither the image information nor the answer probabilities. Yet the models know the effects they fail to show: they can estimate how blur and illusions affect people. Telling is not seeing: a persona makes a model say what it knows about people, not see what they see.

Angular Supervision for Visual Object Grounding in Autoregressive Vision-Language Models
Authors: Parsa Esmaeilkhani, Longin Jan Latecki
First: 2026-10-23T06:16:32+00:00 · Latest: 2026-10-29T04:08:01+00:00
Abs · PDF · Code1 · Code2
Abstract
Autoregressive vision-language models (VLMs) struggle to ground generated text in the correct image regions, and existing explainability methods, such as attention maps and Logit Lens, often produce poor spatial explanations due to weak text-to-vision attention and sink tokens. We propose \textbf{Angular Relevance} ($R_L$), a simple visualization measure based on cosine similarity between visual token embeddings and unembedding-matrix rows. Because $R_L$ is invariant to token norm, it produces substantially sharper and more faithful heatmaps than attention maps or Logit Lens on out-of-the-box models. Without any training, $R_L$ yields competitive referring-expression segmentation (RES), outperforming prior diagnostic maps and CLIP-based training-free methods. To preserve localized object information during cross-modal mixing, we introduce \textbf{Angular Object-Aware Loss (AOL)}, an auxiliary loss that directly supervises visual token embeddings to align angularly with the vocabulary directions of their corresponding object concepts. Finetuning with AOL alongside the standard next-token prediction objective significantly sharpens object localization in heatmaps, showing that the VLM's own visual tokens can be used directly for grounding and improving RES, without training any segmentation heads and without degrading general VQA performance. We further demonstrate that the learned grounding also transfers to category-level segmentation of object categories not seen during training.

Abstract
Multimodal tool-using agents augment vision-language models with access to external information and capabilities, enabling them to solve knowledge-intensive and visually complex tasks that cannot be addressed using visual context alone. However, existing reinforcement learning pipelines largely train single-role agents to search and answer using terminal rewards, overlooking how humans inspect retrieved evidence, reformulate queries, and revise conclusions during search. Consequently, they fail to optimize this self-corrective process over complete interaction trajectories. We propose MRC-MMAgent, a multi-role collaborative reinforcement learning framework that incorporates trajectory assessment and continuation directly into the executable agent loop. First, the framework establishes a multi-role collaborative loop that integrates solving, inspection, and continuation, enabling the agent to acquire evidence through tools, evaluate the preceding trajectory, and resume problem solving when the current answer is insufficiently supported. Second, we introduce a role-coupled learning objective that combines task success, interaction validity, and successful correction with full-trajectory policy optimization across the decisions generated by all roles. Extensive experiments show that, using only the original baseline training set without constructing additional datasets, our method consistently outperforms the corresponding base models and achieves leading or highly competitive performance across benchmarks.

Abstract
Computer-use agents plan on a screenshot for several seconds and then click at a position on the screen; if the page has changed in the meantime, the click lands on an unintended element. Prior work treats the planning interval as the source of this risk. Measuring planned clicks on 31 public web pages, we find that the risk lies in page loading: 0.4% of clicks planned on fully loaded pages became stale within 30 seconds, against 17% of clicks planned in the first second after a page opened. On these pages, waiting about one second for network activity to stop removes most of this risk, yet the wait rules of eight of ten widely used open-source agents leave more than 1% of clicks stale. Commercial pages keep loading for longer: in the HTTP Archive's public crawl, 15% of 65 desktop booking and buying home pages went 500 ms without network requests within 3 seconds of their HTML being parsed, against 63% of 3,028 .gov pages, and 52% shifted their layout within 6.5 seconds of a network-idle wait capped at 3 seconds, against 8% (61% against 26% among pages recorded in full). Where a check before the click is needed, as before irreversible actions and on pages that are still loading, a pixel comparison verifies the target's position as accurately as a frontier vision-language model at one eighth of the latency, and a 4B model matches the frontier model in verifying whether the previous action succeeded. We release a benchmark generator, a break-even formula for when a check pays, and three rules for agent developers: https://github.com/multimodal-ai-engineering/preflight-bench

Abstract
Weakly Supervised Semantic Segmentation (WSSS) learns pixel-level predictions from image-level tags. Recent work focuses on improving coarse CAMs extracted from large vision-language models (commonly CLIP), but does little to improve their accuracy along segment boundaries. That job is instead delegated to a post-processing method like DenseCRF. However, because DenseCRF relies on low-level colour cues, it can flip correct labels to incorrect ones when neighbouring pixels share similar colours. SAM has recently been adopted as a natural alternative, yet it simply takes on DenseCRF's role as an intermediate "refinement" step that outputs one-hot pseudo-labels in prior work. By discarding the valuable uncertainty in CAMs, these one-hot pseudo-labels turn borderline errors into confidently wrong targets. Our key insight is that CAMs should supervise training alongside SAM boundaries, each through its own loss, rather than being fused together into a single hard target. Inspired by CRF potentials, we propose a framework that disentangles soft pseudo-labels as unary supervision and binary edge maps as pairwise supervision. We realize our framework in a single-stage model, DS-CRF, using CAMs from dino.txt and boundaries from SAM. DS-CRF sets a new state-of-the-art of 56.5% mIoU on MS COCO among training-based methods.

Abstract
While frontier vision-language models demonstrate strong capabilities in user interface (UI) navigation, their high inference costs prohibit everyday use. Conversely, smaller models trained via supervised fine-tuning (SFT) or offline reinforcement learning often memorize static trajectories, leading to brittle execution and an inability to recover from errors. To address these limitations, we propose a novel online reinforcement learning framework that trains UI navigation agents directly on live iOS applications. Our framework decouples the agent into two specialized components: a Planning agent and a UI Grounding model. We rely on an LLM-as-a-Judge to provide comprehensive reward signals, combining dense, step-level feedback to evaluate intermediate progress with a terminal reward for overall task completion. To effectively leverage this dual reward structure, we optimize the agent using Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO) with step-level advantage estimation. Furthermore, we introduce GiGPPO, a novel neighbor-conditioned value estimation method that improves critic stability by leveraging reward signals from visually similar states across concurrent trajectories. To support this online training paradigm at scale, we introduce an efficient infrastructure that orchestrates hundreds of concurrent virtual iOS devices. Evaluations on rigorous benchmarks, including UINavBench, iOSWorld, and AndroidWorld, demonstrate that our online RL trained agents substantially outperform both SFT and terminal-only RL baselines while accelerating training convergence. Notably, our 8B RL agents achieve nearly a sevenfold improvement over the SFT baseline in out-of-distribution generalization to Android environments, while outperforming Gemini-3.5-Flash and tying with Gemini-3.1-Pro on real iOS applications.
